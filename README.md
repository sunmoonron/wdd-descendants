# What a write becomes: the descendant and quotient program on Weight-Dictionary Decomposition

Ronish Bhatt ([ORCID 0009-0000-8835-5380](https://orcid.org/0009-0000-8835-5380)), September 2026. Companion to [Weight-Dictionary Decomposition](https://github.com/sunmoonron/weight-dictionary-decomposition) (WDD), which reads a transformer's residual state as a sparse combination of the model's own write vectors. This repository holds the follow-on program: 630 experiments (e00 to e627 with variants) and about 2,000 recorded runs on five small models, run between 2026-09-19 and 2026-09-24, one to five minutes each on one GPU. Phase 1 (e00 to e356) follows one WDD write through the network and asks what it becomes, with the theory the results support and the literature they sit in; it uses forward passes, ablations, injections and closed-form fits only. Phase 2 (e357 to e373) tests the program's tools against known circuits, the co-selection proposal of the LessWrong post "What if not Circuits?", and per-neuron drift across Pythia and OLMo training. Phase 3 (e374 to e627) turns to readers and readouts and then to self-description: how well a model's own write rows describe its own states. It adds Qwen2.5-7B, and it trains toy transformers, grokking networks and a small sequence model from scratch, some with writer or reader rows frozen. **Start with [`docs/DIGEST.md`](docs/DIGEST.md)**, a ten-minute, theory-first summary that says which routes are closed and why; [`docs/ATLAS.md`](docs/ATLAS.md) organises all 630 experiments into fifty-three technical areas and shows how their results connect, and the table below summarises it.

The short version of the result: a neuron's write is the model's own perturbation of its residual stream. Downstream computation expands that perturbation physically (hundreds of dimensions) while its causally relevant content becomes compressible (tens of dimensions), and that compressibility is a property of the residual stream's response to any perturbation, not of the write. The compressed content is not carried by a privileged subspace: any moderate-dimensional high-variance projection of the perturbation cloud carries it, there is no null space and no equivalence-class structure, and the WDD atoms are not a special basis of it. WDD remains a clean birth coordinate and instrument; it is not the functional dictionary. The negative results are part of the result.

The short version of phase 3: a model's own write rows are a vocabulary in which its states can be re-described sparsely. At the middle depth 32-64 of them keep 90% of the loss, far more than a rotated copy of the dictionary, while the model's largest actual writes need a thousand or more. The vocabulary is absent at initialisation, starts as a per-block second-order accent, becomes word-level between Pythia steps 4000 and 16000, and is written by training the writer rows: writer rows frozen at random are barely used as words. It holds language-independent concept words (15 of 24 nouns in Qwen2.5-0.5B, 22 in Qwen2.5-7B), it is private across seeds and sizes, and it is not the best sparse vocabulary (a learned SAE is better at small k). Several phase-3 numbers were inflated by attention-sink tokens and were corrected in session 44; `docs/ATLAS.md` lists every correction.

## What was found, in order of confidence

Positive, replicated in five of five models unless noted:

1. WDD reads a write at birth (source identification 88 to 98% from the increment) and reading is a prominence phenomenon: it decays with a causal half-life of about two blocks and depends on the context re-writing the direction (rounds 1 to 6).
2. Every trained block is, along any direction, a learned diagonal contraction of a near-isometric mixing map: a write's energy is conserved or amplified while its direction is scattered into thousands of later writers and into attention, exactly accountably (the residual identity).
3. The descendant of a write, the footprint of ablating it at depth, is a vector-determined, compositional, magnitude-invariant, portable image of the write, identifiable to its source far past the point where sparse decomposition over transported atoms fails. Provenance survives on a coherent fraction that falls to 5 to 15% of the descendant's energy by depth; classification needs only that fraction, a decomposition would have to explain the rest.
4. The descendant is a state variable for the perturbation family: it predicts its own future two blocks later (cosine 0.43 to 0.78 through a data-free operator, against 0.03 to 0.12 for the original write) and reproduces the future logit effect; interpolated descendants follow the interpolation of the endpoint trajectories through the rest of the network; the transport law composes in state and in function.
5. The causally relevant content of the descendant is compressible: tens of directions suffice for identity, function, the future and the removal KL, about 128 for a linear reconstruction of the future descendant, the full width for the physical descendant. This holds for every perturbation family tried, sixteen of them, including perturbations built orthogonal to the model's natural residual manifold: the compressibility is a property of the residual stream's response, not of the write.
6. The compressed content is universal across observables (nine observables' quotients overlap at the split-half reliability ceiling), homogeneous and additive in its coordinate, linear along its directions to three to ten times the natural amplitude, read out nonlinearly, read by the later MLPs (which also damp the perturbation) and spread across tokens by attention alone, born by step 4000 of training together with the cross-family convergence, and it collapses rather than expands beyond the linear range.
7. The one exception is the massive-activation neuron of SmolLM2: its write transports by the ordinary law at every amplitude, but the natural response to removing it at its own tokens is anti-parallel to the response to adding it, an even (set-point) response carried by one MLP at block 10.

Negative, and decisive for how the positive results should be worded:

1. The compressed content has no privileged coordinate system. A projection fitted to shuffled targets scores the same as the real one; PCA, PLS, CCA and kernel PCA coincide; the discarded 64-dimensional complement decodes identity, function, the future and token identity as well as the 16-dimensional core; cancelling the core component restores nothing at the logits.
2. There is no null space of the response and no coset structure: the difference of two functionally equivalent perturbations is a full-strength perturbation; fibres of the quotient have the diameter and dimension of random sets.
3. The functional coordinate is not an eigenspace or singular space of the transport operator, not the read-out's high-gain subspace forward or backward, not the residual state's variance subspace, and not confined to the natural manifold.
4. WDD atoms are not a privileged basis of the functional content (no sparser, no more concentrated than random directions), their functional classes carry no vocabulary meaning, coordinate-equivalent atoms are not substitutable, and a block's functional coordinate is carried by the small-coefficient tail of its ledger, not its sparse head.
5. None of this yet explains the models' outputs better than direct logit attribution: the functional atlas built from the framework does not beat it for joint ablations.

## Theory

The documents keep two statements apart: the theorem-like object, which is mathematics conditional on assumptions, and the empirical discovery, which is what the five models showed. `docs/THEORY.md` tags every derived item by what it rests on.

### Objects

Residual stream of token t at level ℓ: x_ℓ(t) ∈ R^D, with a pre-norm block acting as x_{ℓ+1} = x_ℓ + f_ℓ(x_ℓ; X_{≤t}). The write of neuron i of block b at token t is c_i(t) ŵ_i, where ŵ_i is the unit row of the MLP down-projection and c_i(t) = a_i(t)·‖w_i‖ is the WDD coefficient. WDD writes the centred state at the birth level as a ledger over the model's own atoms,

```
x_{b+1}(t) − μ = Σ_i c_i(t) ŵ_i + r(t).
```

The descendant of a write at level ℓ is its natural footprint, d_i(ℓ, t) = x_ℓ(t)[with the write] − x_ℓ(t)[without it]; at ℓ = b + 1 the descendant is the write itself.

### The axiom (first-order transport)

Within the linear range,

```
d_i(ℓ, t) = c_i(t) · J_t · ŵ_i + O(c²),     J_t := ∂x_ℓ(t) / ∂x_{b+1}(t),     J_t = J̄_C + ΔJ_t,
```

with J_t the Jacobian of the downstream map along the token's own trajectory, split into a context-class mean and a token-specific fluctuation. Three measured properties make it concrete: (J1) a generic spectrum, singular values spread 11 to 50 times between the 90th and 10th percentiles, no exact null space, root-mean-square gain of order one; (J2) token dependence, the fluctuation term dominating by mid depth (coherent fraction 0.16 to 0.33 at mid depth, 0.05 to 0.14 late); (J3) a linear range up to about the natural amplitude, with distortion at 3 to 10 times.

The read-out is the un-averaged Jacobian lens, f_i(t) = G_{ℓ,t} · d_i(ℓ, t) with G_{ℓ,t} = W_U · ∂x_final(t)/∂x_ℓ(t), and G is nearly isotropic in gain on the descendant cloud.

### What follows, and what observed it

- Homogeneity, d(αŵ) = α d(ŵ), and additivity, d(Σ c_i ŵ_i) = Σ c_i d(ŵ_i), in the linear range (e250, e278, e241, e248, e337).
- Vector determination: d depends on ŵ, not on which neuron produced it, and every vector has a descendant (e236 to e248, e304).
- Sufficiency and causal carriage: the descendant is a sufficient statistic for the effect given the level, injecting it reproduces the effect, effects interpolate linearly along descendant segments and whole trajectories interpolate without snapping (e268, e269, e271, e280).
- The state-variable property: d(ℓ + k, t) = J_{ℓ→ℓ+k, t} · d(ℓ, t), so the present descendant predicts its future while the write does not (e283, e281).
- Angle preservation without isometry. For a pair (u, v_θ = cos θ·u + sin θ·r) with r random, cos(Ju, Jv_θ) = cos θ·ρ / sqrt(cos²θ·ρ² + sin²θ) with ρ = ‖Ju‖/‖Jr‖. Random bases have ρ ≈ 1 by concentration of measure, which is why pair cosines looked preserved; pairs on the operator's top and bottom singular directions have their cosines raised and lowered exactly as the formula says, within 0.07 in fifteen of fifteen cases (e238, e282, e285). "Isometric transport" in the record means this.
- Provenance versus decomposition as an inequality. With κ(ℓ) the coherent fraction, nearest-centroid identification among K candidates has signal-to-noise about sqrt(κ)·Δ / sqrt((1 − κ)·K/D_eff) and survives down to κ of order K/D_eff, while any reconstruction by a fixed dictionary has fraction of variance unexplained at least 1 − κ (e277, e272).

### The quotient and its equation

Reading a functional coordinate from any perturbation by a linear map on the linear range, z_ℓ(δ) = P_ℓᵀ δ, and composing with the ledger and the transport,

```
z_ℓ( Σ_i c_i(t) J_t ŵ_i ) = Σ_i c_i(t) z_i(t) ≈ Z_ℓ c(t),     Z_ℓ := P_ℓᵀ J̄ [ŵ_1 … ŵ_N].
```

Z_ℓ is the image of the WDD dictionary under transport and quotient. WDD reads c (which atom); the quotient reads Zc (what the atoms do). Tested by joint ablation of random subsets of atoms: the ledger-weighted sum of the atoms' coordinates meets the joint footprint's coordinate at cosine 0.54 to 0.68 in three models and 0.30 to 0.34 in two, against zero for random atoms, and the quotient is additive on unrelated atoms at 0.78 to 0.97 (e313).

### The correction the last rounds forced

The first-order model with a generic J and a near-isotropic G predicted the universality of the functional coordinate across observables and families and its additivity, and both held. It never said the coordinate is a privileged subspace, and the audits show it is not (e336, e338, e340, e346, e347, e348). The consistent reading is that G restricted to the descendant cloud is of moderate effective rank with a long tail (e355) and near-isotropic, so that any high-variance projection of the cloud of dimension a few tens suffices for prediction and none is structurally distinguished. P in the equations should be read as "any such projection". In one line:

```
x_ℓ(t)[with] − x_ℓ(t)[without] = c_i(t) [ J̄_C ŵ_i + ΔJ_t ŵ_i ] + O(c²),     f_i(t) = G_{ℓ,t} · d_i(ℓ, t),
```

with the causal information in d low-dimensional and its coordinates not unique. What the theory does not derive: the numerical values, which component reacts at the exception, why training shapes J as it does, and the sizes of the identity, function and behaviour subspaces beyond "function is low-rank because G is".

## Where this sits in the literature

`docs/RELATED_WORK.md` places the program against its nearest neighbours and states what is and is not new. First-order transport, Green-function propagation, composition and the local linear regime belong to Transformer Field Theory (Olivieri and Pérez Rodríguez, 2026); the learned spectral bottleneck of the residual Jacobian to Fernando and Guitchounts (2025, 2026); the causal-dimensionality wedge to Sarkar and Deka (2026); the averaged read-out to the Jacobian lens (Transformer Circuits, 2026); linear transport of learned features between layers to Activation Transport Operators (Szablewski, 2025); weight-space neuron description to ROTATE (2026) and parameter decomposition to SPD and VPD. ## Self-description length and private languages (e391 to e398b)

Two further chains the same night:

- The first asked what WDD's sparse code is a code of.
- The second looked for a purer direction than the sparse-autoencoder comparison. It treats a network's own weights as its vocabulary and asks three things:

## WDD calibrated against classical theory (e483 to e492)

Session 58 returned to the program's original mandate and read WDD as an instrument, calibrating it against theory older than OMP on the five original models: ten experiments, 58 short runs (details in `docs/FINDINGS.md`, session 58).

| Theory | Experiment | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extreme values (Fisher and Tippett 1928, Gumbel 1935) | e483, e492 | The largest competitor correlation is a Gumbel variable in all five models, its mean grows as sqrt(2 ln m), and its two fitted parameters predict the detection curve of an injected write within 0.1; the resolution is 0.25-0.4 of the state's norm. ## What predicts whether a feature follows its writers, and the model written down (e501, THEORY.md 3m)

Feature by feature on the same states, the TopK family's stronger row-following (e499) is its features' sparsity: within both families the row-following AUC falls with the feature's frequency (Spearman -0.40 ReLU, -0.20 TopK) and rises with its activation strength and its cosine with its top native row; by shared frequency decile the families are alike at both ends (0.79 against 0.77 for the sparsest tenth, 0.53 against 0.51 for the densest); and the family label's effect on the AUC is +0.18 raw, -0.09 given frequency, -0.19 given all controls. Whether a feature follows its writers is a property of the feature, not of the SAE (e501).

`docs/THEORY.md` section 3m states the model the last sessions constrain: an atom is a word for a state when its projection stands above the extreme-value level of the competing atoms; a writer clears it through its coefficient (write sparsity), a non-writer through the second-order alignment of the state cloud with the atoms; the two have different histories in training; and because energy is a sum while wordhood is a maximum, the MLP rows are the vocabulary while attention comes to carry most of the state's energy.

## The model tested and corrected: the ledger under controls, another model, the chord and the crowd (e502 to e506)

Session 64 took the relayed queue as a researcher would. The ledger's advantage over the top row was put through the same controls as the top row (e502): the other native rows add modestly and where the feature is sparse (+0.10 in the sparsest ReLU decile, zero in the densest; +0.02 in TopK), random rows add nothing, the family gap is mostly frequency but TopK's ledger keeps a 0.1-0.25 lead at matched frequency in the middle deciles, and a feature's firing turns out to be a whole-state event in which the MLP writes are the smaller part (all MLP writes 0.77-0.84, the non-MLP part of the state 0.95-0.96). On the only public residual-stream SAE in the Pythia line (Pythia-160m, EleutherAI's TopK; e504) the top word is an MLP row and the top row follows the feature at the GPT-2 level, but the features are not state-like and the MLP writes as a whole carry nothing of the firing.

Then the extreme-value model of `docs/THEORY.md` section 3m was tested with nothing fitted (e503): the floor is right in every model, block and checkpoint (the rotated dictionary's maximum is 0.97-1.00 of the exact Gaussian extreme-value mean; 27 cells), and both terms of the numerator are wrong by 1.5-2. The non-writers' excess is a tail, not the state cloud's second-order alignment (a factor 1.02-1.24 of 1.3-2.3), and a writer's projection is 1.5-2.2 times its own coefficient. The anatomy (e505): a word's projection is its own coefficient (0.3-0.7) plus the position's other large writes through their cosines (0.3-0.7), less the crowd of small MLP writes (-0.05 to -0.5), the same crowd that erases writes in area 05; the top non-writer is the chord's shadow (GPT-2, OLMo) or attention's (Pythia); at Pythia step 512 the maxima are head atoms carried by the dense sum of small writes, and the sparse chord takes over by the end. The prediction from the ledger alone (e506) fails for the largest writes summed as written (twice the level where they are as large as the state, the wrong atom in 84-98% of states) because of the common direction they share, and succeeds once the chord is centred as the states are: within 1-13% of GPT-2's level at every block, the atom named in a third to a half of states; in OLMo the crowd's contraction and in Pythia the small writes are the corrections that matter. Section 3n of `docs/THEORY.md` records what stands (the floor, the two clocks, energy as a sum and wordhood as a maximum) and what replaces the numerator. Area 22 of the atlas holds these runs.

Against the whole projection profile rather than the maximum (e507), the centred chord is a partial predictor: in GPT-2 it is calibrated within 13-25% on the atoms it ranks highest and explains 54-66% of their variance, recovers the largest atom in 32-46% of states and a third of the top ten, and orders the true top hundred at 0.30-0.41; the crowd of small writes takes 0.16-0.31 of it and attention gives back 0.13-0.16. The centred sum of all writes does better in GPT-2 and worse in OLMo, where the crowd removes 0.42-0.46 of the chord. Over Pythia's training the crowd's gain along the chord runs from +1.37 at step 512 to -0.64 at the end, amplifying the large writes' directions early and cancelling them late, while attention's gain rises from 0.08 to 0.30-0.50; at step 512 the state is its MLP write-sum (R^2 0.98-0.99 for the whole profile).

Two follow-ups close the loop (e508, e509). The chord predicts the maximum better than the profile because the maximum is selected: in GPT-2 a null that keeps the chord and permutes the residual across atoms reproduces the maximum's level within 2%, the winner's chord rank within a hundred exactly and the winner's residual excess, and the chord's own top atom is calibrated (observed over predicted 0.94-1.01); in OLMo the residual contracts the chord's largest projections specifically (its top atom wins 0.13-0.19 of states against 0.60-0.79 under the null), and in Pythia's middle training it reinforces them. And the crowd's contraction of the chord is the network's response to the chord: removing the chord from what each MLP sees removes 1.2-1.9 times the crowd's gain along it; the response is born between Pythia steps 512 and 4000 and grows to the end, as area 05's learned contraction does; a random direction of the chord's size is contracted two to three times more than the chord, so the contraction spares the vocabulary; the early amplification is not a response but the anisotropy of the early state; and the rows that write a word come to read it (their response to the chord +0.3 to +0.45 at the end).

The contraction does not know the atoms (e510). As a linear response it is isotropic within 25% across native rows, rows used as words, rows never used, token embeddings, head bases, random, covariance-matched and principal directions, in all three models and at every Pythia checkpoint, with native rows contracted slightly more than random directions (1.01-1.17), and it is born isotropic between Pythia steps 512 and 4000, the MLP at step 512 amplifying on-manifold directions only. The chord's sparing is a nonlinear effect at chord amplitude: at a perturbation of the state's size the most-used rows are contracted a fifth to a third less than random directions in GPT-2 and at Pythia's block 18, and more in OLMo, the same model-by-model pattern as the residual's structure in e508. The sentence "the network learns to preserve its vocabulary" is not supported; the vocabulary is what a blind contraction leaves of a large, in-distribution pattern.

The sparing is the position's own writes (e511): a fake chord of native rows with the position's coefficients and the real chord's norm, whether random rows or the block's most-used, is contracted like a random direction in GPT-2 and at Pythia step 16000 and most of the way to it in OLMo and Pythia's end; only the rows that wrote at the position are spared. And at eighteen Pythia checkpoints (e512, atlas area 23) wordhood turns on as a fast crossover, not a discontinuity: half of the writer-win rate's rise falls between steps 2000 and 8000, the most concentrated change of nine observables; the effective number of writes falls first (midpoint near step 1500-1700), the crowd's gain along the chord and the linear contraction follow the wins (2400-15400), the chord's amplitude grows last (14000-19000), the accent is in place before step 512, and the state's late attention phase erodes the last block's words.

Below step 512 (e513, thirteen checkpoints from initialisation): the gradient concentrates transiently on a few MLP rows in the first 16 steps; the state cloud collapses to 9-16 effective dimensions between steps 16 and 64, attention's share of the state collapses with it and the accent is born (the non-writer tail factor 1.00 through step 32, 1.14-1.48 at step 64); a collapsed plateau holds to step 256; then sparsification begins as the cloud re-expands and attention takes the state back, every row turning selective (active at a fifth of positions instead of half, none dying) while the ledger's tails heavy and the contraction is born at warmup's end. The gradient is spread over two to three times more rows than at initialisation by then: sparsification is not a few rows being reinforced.

The rows that end as native words have no lineage before that (e514, fourteen checkpoints, 256 words per block against non-word rows of the same block matched on eventual magnitude): through step 1000 nothing measured tells them apart (activity, write kurtosis, gradient share, direction and magnitude all at AUC 0.42-0.58), while against random rows they are already the larger and more reinforced rows by step 1000 (AUC 0.64 and 0.59); from step 2000 their selectivity and kurtosis separate one doubling before their usage as words does, and the three grow together to the end, where a word is active at a tenth of positions against a quarter for a non-word of the same size; the gradient reinforces them no more than rows of their size and their directions settle no earlier. Before step 2000 the future words are used less than their controls: the early vocabulary is other rows, and turns over between steps 1000 and 8000. The same run closes a measurement trap in the two sweeps before it: the early writer wins were the token embedding, the maxima of steps 512-2000 are head atoms, and the rise from step 2000 is MLP writers.

The turnover itself, followed row by row (e515, e515b): the vocabulary is replaced in identity, a twentieth of the step-1000 words surviving to the end and seven-tenths of the end's words being new after step 4000, with even the last pair of checkpoints exchanging a quarter of the set; the rate of the turnover is the rate at which the 256 largest rows churn (retention within 0.15 at every pair), so it is not an event of the vocabulary's own; but the boundary moves, the rows entering the word set through steps 256-16000 being more selective and larger than the rows leaving it (AUC 0.66-0.76 and 0.76-0.95), and it is never a magnitude threshold (the words overlap the largest rows by 0.27 at most). A churning population under a wordhood boundary that drifts toward larger, sparser rows and then holds.

What that boundary is a threshold on (e516, e516b): the extreme-value floor. Ranked at a checkpoint by its largest projection over the calibrated Gumbel floor, a non-word row's chance of being a word at the next checkpoint is read at AUC 0.76 from step 1000 rising to 0.97 from step 16000, matching or beating its own current usage and far ahead of its magnitude, selectivity and kurtosis (0.5-0.7), with nothing fitted and nothing from the later checkpoint used. The projection that crosses carries the position's chord, since a row's own write clears the floor for only 0-1% of rows; the own write governs staying instead (stayers over leavers 0.90-0.99). The extreme-value criterion is the selection mechanism, not a description after the fact.

What carries the entering projection (e517, e517b, atlas area 24): not the position's chord. With the 64 largest writes at every position replaced by random rows of the same blocks, permuted among themselves, or removed, and the floor recomputed, the prospective prediction of entry at the next checkpoint is within 0.04 of the real state's (Pythia block 12, origins 2000-32000: real 0.86-0.97, fake 0.85-0.95, permuted 0.86-0.97, none 0.86-0.96), and removing the row's own write costs 0.01-0.05; the projection that predicts a row's future wordhood is carried by what the state holds outside its largest writes, the embeddings, attention's output and the crowd of small writes, with which the future word's direction is already aligned. The entrants rise because their projections rise, from 0.135 to 0.215 of the state norm over training, while the floor drifts from 0.159 to 0.145. And the criterion holds across texts in three models (e518): on one set of eight sequences the largest projection over the floor picks a disjoint set's new words at 0.86-0.91 in GPT-2, 0.95-0.97 in Pythia and 0.90-0.93 in OLMo.

Which part of the state supplies that projection (e519, e519b): none alone. Split exactly into centred parts, the token embedding, attention's output, the row's own write, the other largest writes and the crowd, attention's part ranks the next checkpoint's entrants at 0.56-0.68 (Pythia block 12), the crowd's at 0.69-0.79, the chord's at 0.69-0.89, all the MLP writes' at 0.78-0.86, and the whole state's at 0.86-0.97; each part alone clears the floor for most rows while the whole does for a tenth, so the parts cancel along most atoms and the words are the atoms along which attention, the chord and the crowd add. That is why removing any one part changed nothing: the others carry the same ranking. At the entrants' maxima the carrier moves over training from the crowd to attention and the chord, and the entrants do not write at their own maximum until late. And the composition adds nothing to the size (e520): the agreement index, the constructive fraction and the sign consensus of the parts at a row's best position read the next checkpoint's entrants at 0.35-0.63 against 0.75-0.97 for the projection's size, sit at chance within deciles of the size, and rows near the floor enter at the same rate whether their parts agree or cancel. The extreme-value criterion is a criterion on the resultant alone.

Where the resultant comes from (e521): by first-order transport, validated by ablation of the largest sources (1.02-1.05 of the attribution), a future word's projection at its best position is supplied 0.62-0.75 by the MLP writes at that same position (0.55-0.73 the write vectors themselves, the rest their downstream responses), 0.14-0.24 by attention's outputs, 0.00-0.06 by writes at other positions reaching it through attention, and 0.01 by the embedding; the rows that are words at the origin supply 0.02-0.09, the five largest sources are non-words from blocks below, and the participation ratio of the sources is 30,000-120,000 writes. The state comes to occupy a native row's direction because the mass of small writes at a position comes to point there; nothing is transported from elsewhere, the vocabulary does not build the vocabulary, and no descendant of a particular write does. Size-matched non-entrants have the same source structure, and the unembedding's directions do not separate them.

Counted causally (e521b): the absolute attribution mass is thirty to sixty times the projection, so the participation ratio above measured cancellation; ablating the top ten, hundred, thousand and ten thousand attributed writes removes 0.09-0.18, 0.25-0.30, 0.51-0.57 and 0.89-0.92 of an entrant's projection (the first-order prediction exact to the hundred, under-predicting beyond), while ten thousand random writes at the position remove 0.16-0.31. A few thousand small writes, none above 8%, carry a future word's projection.

## Training's hand: the gradient, the rows' motion, the breadth of a word (e522 to e523, atlas area 25)

Does the loss gradient at the origin select the entrants (e522, e522b)? Not for the part of it that can be measured. One small step along the negative gradient of the loss (12k and 65k held-out tokens; the sign of the gradient as the Adam-like direction; linear, ratio 2.00 when doubled) changes every non-word row's largest projection over the floor in a way that reads the next checkpoint's entrants at 0.47-0.54 over all non-words, 0.48-0.55 within deciles of S and 0.46-0.64 against size-matched non-entrants, with Spearman 0.00-0.02 against the actual change; the local pulls of the loss on the state and on the row do no better. The prediction is reliable enough for the null to count (split-half Spearman 0.19-0.44 across rows); the row's own gradient direction is not (split-half 0.02-0.05, cosine 0.002 with the row's actual change), so that half of the question is open, not closed.

Who moves (e522): the rise of an entrant's alignment from one checkpoint to the next is the row moving, not the states. With the next checkpoint's rows against the origin's states the rise is 0.64-0.78 of the actual rise at block 12 (0.56-0.68 at block 6); with the next states against the origin's rows 0.02-0.24. Every row rotates by a similar angle (cosine 0.82-0.88) and grows (norm ratio 1.19-1.57), whether it enters or not; one gradient step moves the rows by a hundredth of a percent of their norm against a quarter of a percent for the states, so the rows' motion is the accumulated update.

Where the entrants' rows move (e523): into the state cloud's principal subspace (the change's fraction in the top-32 components 0.048-0.063 against 0.030-0.040 for non-words and 0.031 for an isotropic change) and toward the state at their best position (cosine 0.085-0.121 against 0.022-0.073 for matched rows and 0.00 at random positions), about half of that outside the top components. The maximum is not defended at one position: the best position persists for 38-60% of entrants and the rise there is a fraction of the rise of the maximum. And the criterion gains a second term: given how far a row's largest projection clears the floor, the number of positions at which it clears it reads entry at 0.71-0.85, the mean squared projection over positions at 0.68-0.77.

In time, a thousand steps at a time (e524, e524b; sixteen Pythia checkpoints, 321 clean entries at block 12 aligned at their entry): for five thousand steps or more before entry the entrants' rows drift in a consistent direction (consecutive increments at cosine 0.28-0.31, against 0.24-0.25 for size-matched rows and 0.15 falling to negative values for the typical row), their fraction in the cloud's top-32 subspace rising at every checkpoint and their breadth rising steadily (13 to 18 positions over three quarters of the floor, against 9-10) while the maximum barely moves (1.02 to 1.07) and no second position clears the floor. Then, in two thousand steps, the maximum crosses (1.07 to 1.25), the positions over the floor go from 1 to 7, the breadth jumps to 33, and the increment's pull toward the state at the best position doubles relative to matched rows (0.090 against 0.043). Then a plateau, the increments decorrelating. The row's own motion keeps its whole profile over positions (Spearman 0.96-0.99) and its old best position first; the states' motion is what moves the maximum (rank 2-4 with both moved). A future word does not lock onto an occurrence; it lifts its profile, breadth first, and the states decide where the maximum sits.

Is the drift the row's own loss gradient (e525, e525b)? Not at any scale that can be measured. A row's mean gradient at a checkpoint, estimated twice on 1.5M tokens of the training distribution, agrees with itself at cosine 0.14, 0.11 and 0.08 (steps 4000, 8000, 12000), less for neurons that fire more; the row's actual thousand-step update follows it at cosine 0.012-0.013 (positive for 63-65% of rows; at most 0.04 corrected for the estimate's reliability), and Adam's whitened direction, the mean over the per-coordinate noise, has the same consistency and the same following because a row's gradient noise is the same size in every coordinate. The update's size is unrelated to the gradient's size. The entrants' gradients are no more consistent than matched rows' (AUC 0.47-0.59), do not point toward the state at their best position (cosine 0.00) and are not followed more. The gradient at a time neither selects the entrants (e522, e522b) nor steers them; the directed drift is a slow variable of the trajectory, the residue of many short-lived corrections, and what gives it its direction is open.

What the drift follows, and what it does not (e526, e527, atlas area 26): of the directions computable at the origin from the network's own geometry, an entrant's thousand-step increment follows the mean centred state at the positions where its neuron fires (the own write removed) at cosine 0.05-0.10, the block-12 state there and the state at its best position likewise, against about zero for the typical row; not the writes it co-occurs with; and the neuron's read direction with the wrong sign for the typical row, whose write drifts away from what reads it (cosine -0.05 to -0.10 per thousand steps), while the entrants' writes do not. The five targets together explain 2-4% of an entrant's increment (0.4% for random directions), rising to 5-9% over four thousand steps: a small persistent component that accumulates while the rest averages out. The bulk of every row's motion is growth along itself (cosine 0.14-0.32) and a remainder the origin's geometry does not give, and the increments' direction has long memory (cosine 0.11-0.17 between increments two to six thousand steps apart for entrants, 0.03-0.09 for typical rows) with a mean-reverting dip at lag one. The drift is not in flat directions of the loss: the curvature in the row's coordinates along the thousand-step update is 1.13-1.15 times that along a random direction, the gradient's direction 3.80-4.68 times, and the entrants' updates are no flatter than matched rows'.

Does the row set move as one (e528)? Every row rotates by a similar angle and grows, and the states are sums of the rows, so the untested possibility was a common linear flow applied to all 53,248 rows, with vocabulary entry being which rows are positioned to gain from an anisotropic flow that amplifies the cloud's principal subspace. The best such map exists and persists (maps one to six thousand steps apart agree at cosine 0.42-0.48), is mostly symmetric growth early (0.16-0.20 per thousand steps at 1000-3000) and increasingly rotation late, but carries a tenth of the increments' energy at 1000-5000 and a fortieth by 15000 (permutation null -0.02), favours the cloud's top-32 components only in the first interval (1.45 times random, then equal or below), reproduces 0.11-0.26 of the entrants' rise of S before step 5000 and none after, and reads entry at chance from 5000 on. It carries four tenths of the increments' long memory; the rest of the motion, and the entry, are row-specific.

The geometry of drift against oscillation (e529): e527's thousand-step update was mostly the mean-reverting oscillation, so its curvature said little about the drift. Separated by timescale, the eight-thousand-step displacement (mostly drift) is 1.09-1.18 times as curved as a random direction (about as curved as a random direction), the thousand-step update 1.15-1.18, and the oscillation axis, the difference of consecutive increments, 1.15-1.17 (steeper than a random direction); entrants and matched rows do not differ.

Where the explanation stops (e530). Before another mechanism, the bound: predictors of a row's eight-thousand-step displacement fitted on half the rows from everything at the origin and scored on the other half. Growth along the row explains 0.045-0.064 of the displacement's energy; the common flow 0.039-0.056; one scale per origin-time target 0.049-0.072, of which the row's own direction and a repulsion from the neuron's read direction are the whole (the contexts, the co-active writes and the best state at 0.00-0.01); a full linear map on each target 0.023-0.046; the maps without the row 0.001-0.001; and the same maps on the destination checkpoint's contexts, the hindsight bound, 0.035-0.066 with the row and 0.001-0.002 without it. The part of the motion orthogonal to the row, 0.94-0.96 of its energy, is predicted at 0.015 or less by any of them. Entrants are no more predictable than matched rows, and rows of like alignment share no displacement. The size of the motion, by contrast, is predictable at R2 0.71-0.76 from the row's norm, the neuron's firing rate and mean activation, S, the count over the floor and the block. The direction of the drift that carries a row into the vocabulary is not in the network's geometry at either end of the interval; it is a stochastic accumulation along the training trajectory that the checkpoints do not contain. The program's claims therefore stand at the level of the criterion, the time course and the anatomy, and no further arrow is drawn.

The control the criterion needed (e531). The floor was calibrated on a rotated dictionary, but only statically; no fixed dictionary had been followed through training. Followed across the sixteen checkpoints against the moving states, with OMP usage defining each dictionary's own words, a fixed random dictionary foretells its own entries at 0.86 over a thousand steps and 0.81 over four thousand, the rotated native dictionary at 0.86 and 0.81, the native rows frozen at step 4000 at 0.93 and 0.91, and the moving native rows at 0.94 and 0.91. Four fifths of the prospective criterion is therefore the extreme-value statistic of any overcomplete dictionary against a slowly moving cloud; the native rows add a tenth, and their frozen directions carry all of it, with the same number of entries per interval and the same overlap of word sets as the moving rows. The rows' drift decides which rows enter, not how many nor how predictably. What the native directions have is stability: a vocabulary on them keeps half its words over four thousand steps, one on random directions a sixth. The claim of sessions 72-82 that the criterion reflects the native vocabulary's formation is corrected accordingly: the vocabulary's formation, as this program can state it, is an extreme-value process of a slowly moving cloud against the persistent axes that the network's own rows provide.

What the native rows' stability consists of (e532, e533, atlas area 27). Random atoms drawn with the native dictionary's covariance keep 0.19 of their words over four thousand steps and atoms drawn with the state cloud's covariance 0.20, against 0.18 for isotropic atoms and 0.50 for the native rows; the cloud-covariance atoms have the most persistent projections and the most churning words. The stability is therefore not second-order geometry but the rows' individual orientations, the non-Gaussian directions of e488. The native vocabulary's half-life is about four thousand steps against about one for random directions, a native dictionary frozen later keeps more of its words and describes a wider stretch of the trajectory, and the step-1000 vocabulary is unrelated to any later one. And a native word's angular basin is wide: half the words survive a rotation of 26-29 degrees statically and 32-33 degrees prospectively, twice a random dictionary's word, with the rotated atom's projection falling alike for both; the difference is the margin above the competitors, and the rows' own drift over four thousand steps, about 32 degrees, stays inside the basin.

The basin's anatomy (e534). The same angle in structured directions: rotating a native word toward its own direction four thousand steps later loses 0.11-0.15 fewer words than a random direction at 20 degrees and 0.17-0.25 fewer at 30, toward its nearest atom fewer still at 30 degrees, away from its nearest atom more; rotating into the cloud's top-32 principal subspace does not lose words but makes them (0.88-0.98 static, 0.75-0.99 prospective, for native and random dictionaries alike), and rotating out of it is what a random direction already is. The basin is mostly the row's own: every non-word atom rotated 20 degrees in a random direction leaves 0.79-0.89 of the native words (a random dictionary's words 0.44-0.55), while the five nearest atoms rotated toward the word remove 0.30-0.39. And its width is the margin: within quartiles of S, native and random words survive a random rotation alike, and among native words usage, breadth and S read survival at 0.71-0.78, 0.65-0.68 and 0.62-0.67 against 0.54-0.56 for the nearest-neighbour cosine. Read as a curve (e534b), survival rises steeply with the margin for native and random-dictionary words alike, and at equal margin the native words are no more robust (0.50-0.54 against 0.46-0.51 within S 0.9-1.25; native minus random -0.14--0.05 in matched bins): the basin is the margin and nothing else.

Breaking the criterion (e535, e536). Wordhood defined without OMP, by nearest-neighbour assignment, by top-16 correlation, or by the count of positions over the floor with no reconstruction at all, gives different word sets (overlapping the OMP words at 0.41-0.58) and the same phenomenon: S foretells entry at 0.92-0.96 over four thousand steps (OMP 0.91; a random dictionary 0.80-0.85) and native retention is 2.3-3.2 times random under every definition, highest under the definition that reconstructs nothing. And a synthetic null with no training, 8192 unit rows diffusing on the sphere against a fixed Gaussian cloud with the real block-12 covariance, the rows' rotation matched to Pythia's, shows a prospective criterion (0.80 at one interval, 0.75 at four), breadth rising before the maximum (12 positions over three quarters of the floor two intervals before entry against 6 for matched rows), turnover and retention (0.32): the generic four fifths of e531 exactly. It lacks words above the floor (the entrants' S 1.00 at entry against Pythia's 1.25), the crossing to a plateau, the increments' memory and the native retention; a weak persistent pull of the rows along the cloud's covariance supplies the levels (AUC 0.88, retention 0.52, S rising to 1.21 two intervals after entry) but not the memory. The program's phenomenon, stated at its boundary: generic extreme-value dynamics of a slowly changing dictionary against a slowly changing cloud, plus a persistent pull of the network's own rows into its own cloud whose origin the checkpoints do not contain.

Which ingredient makes which statistic (e537). Six synthetic worlds with the same step size: isotropic diffusion gives the generic criterion (0.73 at four intervals), breadth before the maximum, turnover and retention 0.31; temporally correlated diffusion with no relation to the cloud (memory only, lag-1 cosine 0.26) gives nothing more (0.69, 0.24); covariance-shaped noise gives less (0.18); the pull along the covariance gives the native excess (0.88, 0.52, the entrants' S rising through the floor to 1.25), and memory with the pull the same with the lag correlation (0.24). And the replay: Pythia's own thousand-step increments, given to other rows under one fixed permutation and run against the real states, carry the whole phenomenology (0.88, retention 0.47, a plateau at 1.24, against 0.91, 0.50 and 1.27 for the real rows); permuted independently at every interval they collapse (0.70, 0.10). Because the increments are larger than the rows over the run, the fixed permutation does not test whether a row's motion is coupled to its own position; it shows that the phenomenology rides on the increment sequences, which are a temporally coherent motion directed into the cloud. That is the native fifth, described as dynamics; its cause remains the boundary.

What the coupling is (e538, atlas area 28). Pythia's real rows with their increment sequences intact, against the same cloud turned by one fixed random rotation at every checkpoint (the cloud's own slow evolution kept, its alignment with the rows' motion destroyed): the criterion 0.72, retention 0.13, the words at the floor, below a frozen dictionary's generic level, because a moving dictionary whose motion bears no relation to the cloud churns more than a still one. The same sequences against a Gaussian cloud with each checkpoint's covariance: 0.88, 0.41, a crossing to 1.08. The rows move into the covariance directions of the cloud they face; the cloud's specific positions and tails add the plateau's height (1.27) and a tenth of retention. In the synthetic world, reversing the pull's sign destroys everything (0.59, 0.08), and a pull that fades toward a target margin gives a slow approach from below rather than Pythia's jump from 1.11 to 1.22 in one interval and stop at 1.25-1.27. Reversing time keeps most of the excess (0.86, 0.45), because the covariance directions the motion aligns with are stable through training, so the reversal tests nothing about causes; and the radial part of the increments carries the rows' growth and none of the statistics. The native fifth is therefore a temporally coherent motion of the rows into the covariance directions of the contemporaneous cloud, not a property of the rows' identities, not the cloud's positions, and not a fading drift; the fast crossing to a plateau is the one feature of the real dynamics no drift law tried reproduces.

How a word leaves (e538b, atlas area 29). The reversed-time replay's entrants showed no crossing, so the exits were read directly from the cached checkpoints. The floor is a gate in one direction. An entry is a crossing: 0.37 of the entrants are under the floor two intervals before entry and 0.19 one interval before, S rises +0.13 across the event, and usage jumps from 0.60 to 1.40 of the word threshold. An exit is a loss of usage with the projection kept: 0.82 of the leavers are still above the floor at the first checkpoint out and 0.76 two intervals later, S changes -0.09, and usage falls from 1.29 of the threshold to 0.67. The leavers are narrower than words of like S that stay (32 against 41 positions over three quarters of the floor), their breadth falling before the exit as an entrant's rises before entry. Words leave by losing the competition for positions, not the geometry that made them words.

Erosion, not displacement (e539, e539b, e540, atlas area 29). The exit was read at the level of positions, from the cached checkpoints with OMP over the rows alone. At the median seven positions a leaver loses, the sixteenth-largest projection at the position, the cutoff a competitor would have to beat, does not move (0.88 to 0.88); the leaver's own projection there falls (0.92 to 0.79), its rank among all atoms from 6 to 31. At the positions an entrant gains, its own projection rises (0.76 to 0.95) against a still cutoff. The atoms that take a leaver's positions are at the null in every respect: as many and as spread as at random positions (10.69 effective takers per lost position against 11.11), of the same classes (0.91 non-words), at chance cosine to the leaver (0.01) and at a median rank of 22854 among its 53,248 neighbours; entrants displace non-words (0.93) and never a leaver. There is no local replacement and no pairing of births with deaths. Hysteresis is real (at S 1.1-1.3 a native word stays at 0.47-0.64 where a matched non-word enters at 0.02-0.04) but generic: a random dictionary shows a matched difference of 0.32 and the synthetic worlds 0.30-0.72, because usage is the bulk of a row's alignment profile and the bulk persists. The one-way gate is not generic: a random dictionary's words leave through the floor (0.24 over it at exit) and enter from under it (0.76 under it one interval before), isotropic diffusion's leave through it too (0.37 over it), the pull worlds' half (0.52-0.56), Pythia's rows 0.77-0.82. The gate is the persistence of the native maximum, which sits at S 1.25-1.44 while the breadth erodes to the usage threshold. And the erosion is mostly the states' drift: at the lost positions the cosine between state and row falls -0.020, -0.015 from the states' motion and -0.006 from the row's; the cosine at a leaver's old peak falls as much while its S holds, the maximum relocating to another position above the floor. Entry is geometric acquisition, as the relayed framing proposed; exit is not competitive displacement but erosion, and what surrounds the birth boundary is not an ecology of competitors but the slow drift of the states against a row whose peak holds.

What holds the maximum (e541, e541b, atlas area 29). The maximum relocates, and generically: a native word's position of largest projection is the same one interval later in 0.44 of cases, as for random words (0.40), and the states at native peaks persist no more than elsewhere (0.926 against 0.925). Breadth alone does not hold it: independent churn drawn from the isotropic atoms' kernel, which regresses a position's projection by -0.085 to -0.122 per thousand steps, reproduces the random words' maximum exactly and under-predicts the native (1.19 against 1.28). What holds it is that a native row's alignment at a position does not regress: at equal level its projection is 1.01, 1.12, 1.26 a thousand steps later from 1.0-1.1, 1.1-1.2, 1.2-1.4, a random atom's 0.93, 1.03, 1.10, and native non-words nearly as much as words. And that persistence is the cloud's covariance: atoms drawn with it persist like the rows (1.00, 1.09), their maxima are the most persistent of all (0.85 across four thousand steps against 0.83 native and 0.39 random), and a null with their kernel reproduces the native maximum within 0.03. Yet the covariance-matched words churn (retention 0.29) below the floor (median S 0.90): persistence is not stability. A native word has both, the persistent alignments of the cloud's subspace and the individual orientation (e488, e532) that sets it at S 1.28 with 38 positions over three quarters of the floor; the gate is one-way because the first holds the maximum and the second places it high, while the breadth erodes by the states' drift. The lost positions are a word's periphery, less coherent than the kept (0.185 against 0.275 pairwise state cosine), not a chunk of contexts disappearing together.

What the cloud's persistence is, and what a Gaussian cloud cannot give (e542, e543, atlas area 30). The cloud persists in most of its directions: the top eight eigen-directions at 0.99 across a thousand steps, ranks 257-512 at 0.85, the bottom half at 0.58, 277 directions above 0.9; the eigenvectors of the lag-one cross-covariance and of the covariance span the same top-32 subspace (0.95), so there are no slow modes hidden behind the variance modes; the subspace rotates slowly and the rows do not lead it (their weight in the final subspace at steps 1000-4000 is below their contemporaneous weight, and entrants move into the contemporaneous subspace). As a whole a native row's projection profile persists no more than an isotropic atom's (0.905 against 0.899). Its extremes do: the conditional persistence of a native word's projections rises from 0.900 in the bulk to 0.961 at 1.2-1.5, while an isotropic atom's is flat at 0.900, the signature of a Gaussian profile whose extremes regress like its bulk. Then the same-covariance, different-dynamics null: the real rows against five clouds with exactly the real covariance at every checkpoint. Independent samples keep the criterion (0.88) and much of the retention (0.37) with no per-position persistence at all, the spatial part of wordhood; coupled clouds restore the per-position persistence (1.05-1.13 at 1.1-1.2 against 1.12) and the peak's provenance; and every Gaussian cloud gives the same words, median S 1.08, 125-144 entries per interval, retention 0.37-0.42, against 1.28, 96 and 0.50 for the real states. The covariance explains the persistence of the bulk and the spatial part of wordhood; the native fifth, the plateau, the low turnover and the one-way gate, lives in the non-Gaussian extremes of the real cloud, which is e488's non-Gaussianity seen dynamically.

Four projections per position (e544, e544b, atlas area 30). The native fifth was then located by surgery on the clouds, the real rows against each. The coupled Gaussian cloud with the real covariance, given the real projections at each position's top four rows (four of 53,248, solved exactly per position), restores the plateau (the words' median S 1.32 against 1.28 real and 1.07 Gaussian), the turnover (91 entries per interval against 96 and 135), the retention (0.51 against 0.50 and 0.35) and the one-way gate (0.88 against 0.79 and 0.57); the top 16 do the same, and deeper grafts bring S back down as the floor rises with the states' alignment to the dictionary. The real states with the top 16 projections per position replaced by Gaussian values fall below the Gaussian cloud (criterion 0.72, 197 entries, retention 0.16): beneath its extremes the real cloud is inert. The plateau's return is partly by construction, since OMP selects by projection and the transplanted pairs decide the winners at each position; what is not is how few pairs suffice and that the turnover, the retention and the gate ride on them. And the set is row-wise: with the grafted pairs moved to random positions at every checkpoint the vocabulary is unchanged (retention 0.51, 94 entries, gate 0.89) while a row's positions no longer persist (0.58 at 1.1-1.2 a thousand steps later against 1.02) and the maximum never stays put (0.02 against 0.41); scrambling the checkpoints keeps the set's long-range overlap (retention 0.47) and loses its consecutive coherence (125 entries, gate 0.71). The vocabulary is the slow evolution of the set of rows the states make extreme somewhere; where is interchangeable. A prospective panel on the real states closes a side question: given S, the tail's mass (the count over three quarters of the floor, 0.86) reads entry four intervals ahead and its shape (kurtosis, 0.58) does not.

Who writes the extremes (e545, atlas area 31). At steps 4000, 8000 and 16000 the state at every position was split into its writes, the 53,248 MLP neurons of blocks 0-12, the thirteen attention outputs and the embedding (the state reconstructed at cosine 1.000), and for every top-4 extreme pair the contribution of each writer to the projection was taken. An extreme is not a sparse event: 9232 effective MLP writers at step 8000, the ten largest carrying 0.01 of the absolute contribution, the thousand largest positive contributors summing to 8.4 times the net (a cancelling mass), and the attention outputs, writes transported from other positions, supplying 0.57 of the projection. The same population writes a row's extremes wherever they occur: the writer vectors of a row at two of its extreme positions have cosine 0.489 at step 8000 and 0.597 at 16000, against 0.001 for different rows and 0.024 for the same row at a typical position, the activation patterns at a row's extremes alike before any weighting (0.213 against 0.037). The ensembles are the row's own, not the big writers of any direction (random directions' cohere at 0.083); they are not shared across words (the words-by-writers matrix has effective rank 63.6 for 247 words, and the words' activation means at their extremes are no lower in rank than random position sets); and they persist (0.882 from step 8000 to 16000, 0.710 from 4000 to 16000, against 0.002 for different words). A word is a persistent, word-specific ensemble of thousands of writers whose aligned writes, in a recurring kind of context, sum to an extreme along the row. The coalition precedes the word (e546, e546b, atlas area 31). The temporal question was then put with a control: for every clean entrant and a near miss matched on S one interval before the event (a non-word with the same S that stays a non-word), at the row's top-4 positions at the event and at every checkpoint from four intervals before to two after, the writer vectors and their coherence. Four intervals before entry, at the positions that will be its extremes, a future entrant's writer vectors already cohere across positions at 0.42 (at entry 0.50), where the near miss's cohere at 0.20 at every checkpoint; the activation patterns alone at 0.25 against 0.16, and their stability is generic (0.72 against 0.69). Both have S 1.12 one interval before; the entrant crosses to 1.21 and 1.27, the near miss falls back to 1.08 and 1.02. What rises across the entry is the coherence of the contributions (2.45 four intervals before, 3.72 at entry, 2.72 two after; the near miss's rises to 2.47 and collapses to 0.64), the own neuron's contribution (0.06 to 0.12; the near miss's own neuron does not fire at its positions) and the alignment. Split additively, the rise of the projection from four intervals before to entry is a quarter the row's rotation (0.25), a quarter the attention outputs' own motion (0.25, transported writes arriving), 0.13 the local writers' motion, and the activations' change is slightly negative (-0.06); e522's reading, the rise is the row moving, is a quarter of the account. The coalitions are private: a word's top-500 ensemble aggregate write has cosine 0.575 with its own row, 0.013 with other words on average, and the own row is the largest entry for 0.95 of words. And their persistence is not a selection artefact: a word's writer vector at its step-8000 positions has cosine 0.873 with the same positions' vector at 16000, against 0.599 when the same positions are weighted by a random native row's alignment and 0.787 for the activations alone. A word is a row with a private coalition of thousands of writers at a recurring kind of context; the coalition exists before the word; entry is the row turning toward the coalition's aggregate write while transported writes arrive; a near miss has the projection without the coalition and falls back.

The channel that was a selection artefact, and the neuron that keeps firing (e547, e547b, atlas area 31). A step back over the arc suggested a reading: a word is an MLP neuron whose output row has rotated onto a channel, the aggregate write of its coalition at a class of contexts. Its direct test defined the coalition at the event by the writers' alignment with the row and followed the channel back through training, and its own control caught it: for random non-words defined the same way the channel is as stable (0.75 against 0.79 eight intervals before), the row approaches it as much (0.43 to 0.62 against 0.41 to 0.61) and the row's displacement points at it as well (0.458 against 0.488). An object defined by the row's own direction inherits it, and says nothing about which came first; the construction is retired, and e546's precedence stands because it was measured across positions against a near miss matched on S. What survives the control: the entrant's displacement points toward the states at its future contexts (0.171 against 0.028), its coalition is context-specific, and the families of coalitions are few (176 of 256 words alone at cosine 0.3, the largest family of 11) and are near-duplicate rows extreme at the same contexts (0.79 of family pairs sharing a token). The clean part came from the neuron itself: an entrant's own neuron is selectively active at its future extreme contexts eight intervals before entry (z-score 1.61, the contexts in the top 0.21 of its positions), rising to 2.85 at entry, where a random non-word's is not (0.02); a near miss's neuron is as selective eight intervals before (1.91) and loses it (0.57 by four intervals before), while its row approaches the class as an entrant's does (0.140 against 0.174, random rows 0.038). The developmental ordering that sessions 95-96 give: the neuron's selectivity for a class of contexts and its row just under the floor there come first, the coalition's coherence is most of the way by four intervals before, and the crossing comes last. The input side of the neuron selects the class, the output row follows, the coalition is what the class writes, and the word is the coincidence of the three holding long enough to cross.

WDD is not a proxy for neuron selectivity (e548, atlas area 32). The necessity test the previous result called for: if ordinary neuron statistics, computed without the projection, predict future entry as well as S does, the decomposition was an indirect measure of something simpler. At every checkpoint the candidates were the non-words that enter at one, two, four, six or eight intervals and 4,000 random non-words that do not, with ten features at the origin: S and the count over three quarters of the floor on the WDD side; the neuron's mean positive activation, the kurtosis and the sparsity of its activation over positions, the coherence of the states at its sixteen most active positions, the row's norm and its growth on the activation side; the row's cosine with the states at those sixteen positions as output geometry with positions chosen by the activation; and the coalition's coherence there. Out of sample (trained on even origins, tested on odd, and the reverse), the activation side predicts entry four intervals ahead at 0.74, unchanged by the output geometry and the coalition (0.74); S and breadth alone at 0.88; all ten at 0.88. WDD added to the activation side raises the AUC by +0.142; the activation side added to WDD by +0.001. Alone, the best activation-side statistics are the mean positive activation (0.64), the coherence of the neuron's most active contexts (0.67) and the coalition's coherence there (0.65); kurtosis and sparsity are at or below chance, and the row's cosine at its own most active positions reaches only 0.64. S and breadth lead at every horizon (0.83 and 0.85 eight intervals ahead against at most 0.64). The reason is in the previous sessions: a word's extreme contexts are the coalition's, where the neuron fires selectively (the top seven percent of its positions at entry) but not most, and where its own write is a tenth of the extreme; the projection sees those contexts through the row, the neuron's own firing does not. The families of session 96 fire in related contexts (activation correlation 0.26 against -0.01 for random word pairs) with different peaks (overlap 0.06). The record keeps the word "word" for the statistic; what it names is a neuron whose output row the states make extreme at a class of contexts, written there by a private, persistent coalition of thousands and preceded by the neuron's selectivity for that class.

What a feature dictionary recovers of the association (e549, atlas area 32). The fair competitor for the larger claim is a learned feature dictionary of the states. The only public residual-stream autoencoders in the Pythia line are EleutherAI's TopK autoencoders for Pythia-160m, so the test ran there, at the end of training, block 6, with 32,768 latents of which 4626 are active at eight or more of the 8170 positions. Each native word's context set was its eight positions of largest projection over the floor. No feature matches such a set as a set: the best-matching feature by Jaccard reaches 0.25 for 0.28 of words against 0.17 of random non-word rows and 0.17 of random directions, with a median Jaccard of 0.19, and it is a feature of 5 positions sharing two of the eight, its decoder unrelated to the row (cosine 0.04). The feature side does see the row, and half of its contexts: the feature nearest to a word's row by decoder cosine sits at 0.48 (random rows 0.16, random directions 0.13) and fires at 0.50 of the word's eight extreme positions (random rows 0.00, the words with shuffled positions 0.00). In reverse, the nearest row to a live feature is a word for 0.20 of features against a base rate of 0.01, a seventeenfold enrichment, though only 0.10 of features have any row within cosine 0.5. So the three lenses on a word: the neuron's own statistics see the association at 0.74 (e548); a learned feature dictionary sees the row at cosine about a half and half of its contexts; the projection sees the association whole and names the row. WDD's contribution, as far as these tests go, is the specific set of contexts where one native output direction is extreme, and the row itself; the feature side gives the class in the large and the direction in part. The second model (e550-e553, atlas area 33). The critique of the second model, anticipated and run (e554, e555, atlas area 33). Two of session 99's differences did not survive re-measurement. The object: class, direction or row (e573-e577 with e573b, e575b, atlas area 36). The one question left above the map, whether a context class or its row is the object, generalised into five hypotheses and run. The class is the partition, and the partition is the corpus's surface structure: the token, the previous token, the position and four flags predict a word's context set at AUC 0.853 at the median; it is not an output partition beyond that (the predicted next token is as pure within a set as the input token, 0.22 against 0.24); two vision models with different objectives share only its spatial layout (0.88 against 0.02 at the level of patches). The direction carries the function: when a class changes its row between step 16000 and the end, the new direction is not the old one (cosine 0.133) and the direction's downstream effect changes with it (0.156 against 0.449 for classes that kept their row); within one model, two words sharing a class have effects as alike as their directions (correlation 0.86, 0.185 when the directions differ and 0.917 when they agree); and the words are read by the other blocks no more than any other row. The row is the implementation: a fresh network trained with the grokked toy's rows frozen describes its states by them (advantage 0.55 against 0.05 for random frozen rows) without re-forming their classes (profile correlation -0.01, random pairs 0.00), and a later neuron's direction transplanted into an earlier model becomes a word without carrying computation (it changes the predictions at its class by 0.0021 nats and moves them away from the later model). The data fixes the classes, training fixes the directions, and the rows are where the directions live; WDD reads the direction through the row.

What has to travel, and what the instrument is for (e578, e579, atlas area 36). The coalition transplant factorial: for forty entrants the direction alone makes 0.60 of them words in the earlier model, the coalition of their 128 strongest producers alone 0.47, both together 0.93, and 512 producers with the direction 0.83; but none of these moves the earlier model's predictions at the class toward the later model by more than a few percent of the gap (-0.066 for 512 producers with the direction), while the readout alone (blocks 13-23, the final norm and the unembedding) closes -0.873 of it at the classes and -0.994 elsewhere. ## A looped model (Ouro-1.4B, [`docs/LOOPED.md`](docs/LOOPED.md))

Ouro-1.4B runs one 24-layer stack four times with shared weights, so one dictionary describes every loop; SmolLM2-1.7B, with the same width, depth and tokenizer, is the standard comparison. Replaced by 16 native atoms in the middle of loop 1, the state keeps 89% of the loss (rotated dictionary 30%, one-shot cosine 79%), and the state one loop hands the next keeps 82-92% at 64 atoms against 31% or less for cosine ranking. Later loops repair a single error (+2.33 nats at the loop-1 exit, +0.08 by loop 4), but dense substitution compounds in both models. Loop 1 is its own stage and loops 3-8 one machine, in the neuron ledger and, from the state alone, in WDD's word lists; each loop erases about 95% of its input by its middle and rebuilds it. WDD adds nothing for halting, loop provenance or per-token dynamics, where the exit gate, the shift of the prediction and the ledger do better.

## Scope and caveats

Five models under 1.1B parameters, block-2 writers for most runs, one corpus (wikitext-2, with a Pile check), tokens in typical-norm range (attention-sink positions excluded), perturbations at the natural amplitude unless a sweep says otherwise, and decoders limited to nearest-centroid, kNN, ridge and closed-form kernels so that nothing is trained. Every number is a median over held-out tokens unless the script says otherwise. Retracted artifacts and superseded designs are listed in `docs/KILLED.md`; an unsigned transport residual (e275) and a mis-designed additivity reference (e285 part 3) were corrected by e275b and e285b, and a cross-token measure in e315 was discarded for dense injection. The one large outage of the run, two hours without network mid-program, did not lose results because the launcher is resume-safe.
