# The positive claims against the literature (session 124)

Written 2026-10-09 after the user asked for a literature review of the results this program calls positive. The claims are taken as [`PAPER.md`](PAPER.md), [`DIGEST.md`](DIGEST.md) sections 4 and 5, and sessions 110 to 123 state them. For each claim the review gives the nearest prior work, says what the literature already had, and says what is left that is specific to WDD. The verdicts use three words. **Known**: the claim as stated is in the literature. **Known in another unit**: the same ordering or effect is reported for sparse-autoencoder features, activations or neurons rather than for the parameter row. **Not found**: no source found on 2026-10-09; this bounds the search, not the literature.

Sources marked (full) were read in their HTML version, those marked (abstract) from the arXiv page, and those marked (checked) are papers known before this session whose arXiv page was fetched to confirm title, authors and year. Nothing here was taken from a summary site without checking the paper's own page.

## 1. The extreme-value floor

**The claim** (e483, sessions 58 and 72). Over a dictionary of m unit atoms the competitor maximum of a state's projections is a Gumbel variable whose mean grows as the square root of 2 ln m; the two fitted parameters predict the detection curve of an injected write within 0.1 in five models; the floor is the one-way gate of wordhood and predicts the next checkpoint's entrants out of sample. For a rotated dictionary the scale of the law is the atoms' second moment; for the native dictionary it is 2.9 to 6.2 times that (the provenance factor).

**The literature.** The Gumbel limit for the maximum of many variables with Gaussian-like tails is classical, and the square root of 2 log n is the universal threshold of Donoho and Johnstone (Biometrika 1994). The maximum absolute correlation among many columns (the coherence) has three limit regimes depending on the number of columns against the sample size ([Cai and Jiang, JMVA 2012](https://repository.upenn.edu/handle/20.500.14332/47985)), and a 2026 preprint locates where the Gumbel approximation stops holding for sparsely equicorrelated fields ([arXiv 2603.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05306), abstract). Detection theory calibrates thresholds by extreme-value statistics rather than by simulation ([Broadwater and Chellappa 2010](https://sites.umiacs.umd.edu/node/12495)), and a hyperspectral detector takes the largest similarity over the atoms of a dictionary and bounds its false-alarm rate as a function of dictionary size and atom correlation ([arXiv 1702.00609](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00609), abstract). In interpretability I did not find a dictionary method whose membership test is calibrated by an extreme-value null; sparse autoencoders fix their sparsity by an L1 or TopK rule.

**Verdict: known as statistics.** The floor is standard extreme-value theory applied without modification, and the calibration against a rotated dictionary is the ordinary way to get a null. What is WDD's is the measurement, not the method: the provenance factor of 2.9 to 6.2 and its two-term account (THEORY sections 3m and 3n). That the floor predicts entrants out of sample (session 72) is a finding about the model's training, not about the statistic.

## 2. The native vocabulary and the row as the unit

**The claims** (e388 to e504; session 121's wording). Thirty-two to sixty-four of a model's own rows keep 90% of the next-token loss at the middle depth where its largest actual writes need a thousand (e388, e391); the property is learned and private across seeds; concept words are the same across four languages (15 of 24 nouns in Qwen2.5-0.5B, 22 at 7B, e448d); the rows are the heavy-tailed directions of the state cloud (e488, e489); an MLP row is a sparse-autoencoder feature's top native word in 93% of cases and a quarter of the most used rows are features outright (e494, e497); attention carries most of the state's energy and almost none of the words, since energy is a sum and wordhood a maximum (e500). Session 121 called the row "a unit the literature does not have".

**The literature.** The neuron is the unit of a large literature, and the MLP down-projection column is its write side. Lakretz et al. found two "number units" carrying long-distance agreement in an LSTM language model ([NAACL 2019, arXiv 1903.07435](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07435), checked). Dalvi et al. analysed individual neurons of NMT and language models ([AAAI 2019, arXiv 1812.09355](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09355), checked). Geva et al. read each feed-forward update as a sum of sub-updates, one per parameter vector, each promoting concepts in vocabulary space, and used that to cut GPT-2's toxicity by about half ([EMNLP 2022, arXiv 2203.14680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14680), checked). Dai et al. edited facts through "knowledge neurons" in the feed-forward layers ([ACL 2022, arXiv 2104.08696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08696), checked). Gurnee et al. found single neurons dedicated to contextual features in the middle layers of models from 70M to 6.9B by k = 1 sparse probes ([2023, arXiv 2305.01610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610), checked), and Voita et al. sorted OPT's FFN neurons into dead, n-gram and positional in the neuron basis ([2023, arXiv 2309.04827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04827), checked). Gurnee et al. 2024 (universal neurons across seeds) and Bricken et al. 2023 (features against neurons) are already in [`RELATED_WORK.md`](RELATED_WORK.md). For concepts shared across languages, Wendler et al. showed the middle layers of Llama hold the right concept before its language, biased toward English ([ACL 2024, arXiv 2402.10588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10588)), Dumas et al. separated the concept from the language by activation patching and improved translation by averaging a concept's representation across languages, with Qwen 1.5 7B among the models in an appendix ([ACL 2025, arXiv 2411.08745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08745)), Tang et al. found language-specific neurons that steer the output language ([ACL 2024, arXiv 2402.16438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16438), checked), and Stańczak et al. found neurons for gender and number shared across 43 languages in m-BERT and XLM-R ([NAACL 2022, arXiv 2205.02023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02023)). On the energy share across training, nothing found reports attention's share of the residual stream's energy against the MLP's across Pythia checkpoints; the adjacent fact is that attention and MLP output norms both keep growing late in training ([arXiv 2601.04890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04890), abstract).

**Verdict: known in another unit, and one sentence withdrawn.** "A unit the literature does not have" is wrong as worded: the neuron is the standard unit, and the down-projection column is Geva et al.'s value vector. Concept words shared across languages are precedented at the representation level (Wendler, Dumas) and at the neuron level in encoders (Stańczak). Not found: a sparse decomposition of the state over the rows in which provenance is the only thing separating the dictionary from its control, the figures that tie a learned feature to a row (93%, a quarter), and the separation of energy from wordhood over training (e500). Those are measurements inside a known framing, not a new kind of result.

## 3. The developmental law

**The claims** (e584 to e591, [`PAPER.md`](PAPER.md)). Every one of 241 eventual words' classes coheres in the step-1000 state cloud of Pythia-410m before any row carries it (e584); the class's own direction is at cosine 0.85 with its step-16000 direction at step 4000, when the row that will speak it is the speaker for 6% of classes (e585); two checkpoints before recruitment the eventual row is already first of 53,248 rows by projection ratio over the class for 52% of words and in the top ten for 84% (e587); recruits are forecast two checkpoints ahead from row-free classes at AUC 0.927 and 0.932 on Pythia-410m and OLMo-1B (e588); recruitment is not a performance event, the class's loss falling as its token-matched controls do (difference -0.0102 nats, interval -0.0292 to +0.0116), and the row's direction is load-bearing before recruitment (e591); the class's own positions supply 0.215 of the row's gradient at 0.006 of positions (e589); a blocked row is replaced, a third of the classes re-appearing on substitutes in the grokking toy (e586), while at scale thirty removed speakers get substitutes for 0.17 of their classes within a 2.5M-token proxy continuation against 0.23 with no training and 0.30 unfrozen (e590, narrowed).

**The literature.** Two studies follow sparse-autoencoder features across Pythia checkpoints and report the same ordering. Stecher, Radovanović, Sikimić and Kahle train TopK autoencoders at seven checkpoints (steps 0, 1k, 10k, 40k, 80k, 120k, 143k) of Pythia-160M and 410M, at four layers each, and find that a feature's firing identity is largely settled early while its decoder direction "has barely begun to calibrate", function converging faster than direction; a logistic predictor on early-checkpoint features separates the features that persist to step 143k at AUC 0.80 (160M) and 0.83 (410M) under five-fold cross-validation; no neuron or parameter row is tracked, and there is no ablation during training ([May 2026, arXiv 2605.18789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18789), full). SAE-Track (Xu, Wang, Huang and Wang) warm-starts an autoencoder at each checkpoint from the previous one on Pythia-410m-deduped layer 4 (with 160m, 1.4b and the Stanford GPT-2 checkpoints in appendices), finds three phases of formation, and reports that a feature's semantics stabilise while its decoder direction keeps drifting, which it calls a decoupling; no parameters, no prediction ([Dec 2024, arXiv 2412.17626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17626), full). Both predate the program's developmental sessions (September 2026). The theory of small networks has the alignment-before-fitting picture: weight vectors concentrate at a few data-determined directions under small initialisation ([Maennel, Bousquet and Gelly 2018, arXiv 1803.08367](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08367), checked); the tangent kernel's eigenstructure aligns before the loss appreciably decreases, the silent alignment effect ([Atanasov, Bordelon and Pehlevan, ICLR 2022, arXiv 2111.00034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00034), checked); first-layer neurons of a two-layer ReLU network align early with the positive or the negative data according to their second-layer weight ([Min, Mallada and Vidal, ICLR 2024, arXiv 2307.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12851), checked); and the initial alignment phase has a quantitative description for orthogonal inputs ([Boursier, Pillaud-Vivien and Flammarion 2022, arXiv 2206.00939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00939), checked). These are alignment at initialisation in toy networks; the program's recruitment is mid-training, to a direction the cloud already speaks (session 77). Forecasting during pretraining exists for circuits and capabilities: induction-head formation is predicted from batch size and context size ([Aoyama and Wilcox, arXiv 2511.16893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16893)); a per-seed forecast of induction onset from the previous-token head's formation reaches Spearman 0.977 over 30 runs with a median lead of 975 steps and passes blind pre-registered gates ([arXiv 2609.19000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19000), abstract); Mechanistic Data Attribution traces heads to training samples and moves their emergence by data interventions (Chen et al., ICML 2026, arXiv 2601.21996, session 123); and the quantization model discovers skills tied to data clusters from gradients ([Michaud et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2303.13506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13506), checked). For replacement, Lo, Cohen and Barez pruned concept neurons, retrained, and found the concepts quickly relocated to earlier layers and to "primed neurons with similar semantics" ([2024, arXiv 2401.01814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01814), abstract); the Hydra effect is the inference-time version (McGrath et al. 2023, already cited).

**Verdict: known in another unit for the ordering; not found for the parameter.** The sequence "positions first, direction later" and the early forecastability of persistence are in Stecher 2026 and SAE-Track 2024 for autoencoder features, and the toy theory had alignment before fitting earlier still. Replacement after removal is in Lo et al. 2024, with the program's own scale test (e590) finding none within its budget, so on replacement the literature's evidence is stronger than the program's. Not found anywhere: the identity of the parameter recruited (the nearest row by projection ratio), its forecast two checkpoints ahead at AUC 0.93, the attribution of the row's gradient to the class's positions, and the loss-matched control showing recruitment is not a performance event. Those are the parts a feature study cannot do because it has no parameter. The paper's sentence should read: WDD reproduces at the row level an ordering already reported for features, and adds the parameter's identity, its forecast and its gradient source.

## 4. The unlearning audit

**The claims** (e592b, e597 to e618). Under every gradient unlearning method the rows that wrote the forgotten classes are unchanged (cosine 0.9991 to 1.0000) while their neurons are silenced in activation; the writers' activation tracks how fast the forgetting is undone (Spearman 0.82 over 39 conditions on two model sizes); on the pre-registered held-out test the ranking travels (AUC 0.85 on new domains, 0.91 on new methods, 0.93 on Qwen, cluster bootstrap) while the fixed threshold does not; activation-side measures add +0.31 leave-one-domain-out accuracy over loss-side measures; "steps back within 0.1 nats" is a floor artefact because zeroing any set lifts the reachable loss (e618e); and a neuron set chosen by activation difference does as well as the dictionary's writers (e602, e618b).

**The literature.** That unlearning is shallow is established: fine-tuning on ten unrelated examples recovers most of what RMU removed, jailbreaks work when applied carefully, and removing activation directions recovers capabilities ([Łucki et al. 2024, arXiv 2409.18025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18025), checked); sharpness-aware minimisation is proposed for relearning resilience ([Fan et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.05374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05374)). That representation-side measures predict reversibility is also established. Xu et al. measure representational drift by PCA similarity and shift, CKA and Fisher information across methods, domains and models and sort forgetting into four regimes by reversibility and catastrophicity ([ICML 2026, arXiv 2505.16831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16831), abstract). Song et al. read the accessibility of the forgotten concepts through a Jacobian lens on 398 public unlearned models from eight methods and find pre-attack accessibility predicts recovery at the model level, and that optimising the audit teaches the model to hide from it ([Aug 2026, arXiv 2608.11408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11408), abstract). Goel, Ritter and Gurevych audit representations by partial information decomposition ([EACL 2026, arXiv 2601.15111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15111)); a restoration test with sparse autoencoders separates suppression from deletion in image unlearning ([arXiv 2602.18505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18505)). On the weight side, L2 distance and linear mode connectivity to the starting checkpoint forecast relearning resistance in vision classifiers ([Siddiqui et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.22310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22310)), and a follow-up finds the connectivity barrier predicts little on TOFU and proposes a forget-retain alignment gap instead ([arXiv 2608.25429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25429)). For the naive-set result, Kantamneni et al. find sparse autoencoders do not beat simple baselines at probing once ensembles are compared ([2025, arXiv 2502.16681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16681), checked).

**Verdict: known in substance.** That the activation side predicts reversibility while the output loss does not, and that weight distance is a weak predictor in language models, were both in print before session 110. Not found: the per-row parameter reading (rows unchanged, neurons silenced), a fixed rule scored leave-one-domain-out across methods and an architecture with the +0.31 increment, and the floor-artefact warning for steps-back metrics. The naive set's equality is the direction Kantamneni et al. report for autoencoders. The audit is a careful replication with a transfer test, not a new diagnostic.

## 5. Single-row attribute dials, free text and the cross-lingual effect

**The claims** (e620 to e626). Row 3019 of block 4 in Qwen2.5-0.5B negated flips gender in 0.34 of discovery items and 0.28 of held-out items at a Pile cost of +0.0003 nats (e622); in free text it moves the probability of the subject's pronoun from 0.96 to 0.81 and generated agreement from 0.99 to 0.88 (e624); inside French, Spanish and German it moves agreement from 0.93 to 0.58, 0.93 to 0.58 and 0.84 to 0.53 while the largest change from a random row is 0.00, and on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct it does nothing (e625); Pythia-160m carries gender on row 2857 of block 4 with a dial of +0.343 (e626); the voted row is the top neuron by activation difference in seven of eight cases; and the collateral lands where the neuron fires (AUC 0.98 and 0.99).

**The literature.** Single units carrying a grammatical attribute: Lakretz et al.'s two number units (above); causal mediation finding gender-bias effects "concentrated in a small part of the network", in neurons and heads ([Vig et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2004.12265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265), checked); the same method on subject-verb agreement, with two mechanisms that depend on sentence structure and shared neuron sets ([Finlayson et al., ACL 2021, arXiv 2106.06087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06087), checked). Gender neurons edited: interpretable neuron editing of gender and general neurons in five LLMs ([arXiv 2501.14457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14457)), and neuron sets for feminine, masculine and gender-neutral generation activated or masked in two open models, in English, at the activation level ([You, Nikeghbal and Diesner 2026, arXiv 2605.30717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30717), abstract). Gender neurons shared across languages: Stańczak et al. 2022 in encoders (above). Weight-level edits of single feed-forward vectors: Geva et al. 2022 and Dai et al. 2022 (above). Language-independent concepts under patching: Dumas et al. 2025 (above).

**Verdict: known in another unit.** Single-unit grammatical dials, edited gender neurons and gender neurons shared across languages are all in the literature. The locator the program uses (the vote) is the ordinary contrast-activation selector, as the program itself found (rank 0 by activation difference in seven of eight). Not found: a single down-projection column of a decoder model whose sign flip moves agreement inside three other languages at near-zero language-model cost, with placebo rows and a free-text pronoun measure. That is a measurement of a known effect at the weight-row level, and session 121's "things the literature has not thought up" does not hold for this line.

## 6. Programmable placement

**The claims** (e626, e627). A pinning loss puts the gender difference on a designated quiet row (vote share 1.00 on three seeds) but the row's dial stays near zero on a trained model (-0.029, +0.027, -0.006), while from the step-8000 checkpoint one seed gets both (dial +0.128).

**The literature.** Assigning concepts to designated units by a loss is the concept-whitening idea, which aligns latent axes with known concepts in CNNs ([Chen, Bei and Rudin, Nature MI 2020, arXiv 2002.01650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01650), checked), and the concept-bottleneck idea ([Koh et al., ICML 2020, arXiv 2007.04612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04612), checked). Training for monosemantic units is in Jermyn, Schiefer and Hubinger ([2022, arXiv 2211.09169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09169), checked), codebook features ([Tamkin, Taufeeque and Goodman 2023, arXiv 2310.17230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17230), checked) and the decorrelation view of monosemanticity ([arXiv 2406.17969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17969)). Lo et al. 2024's polysemantic mixing of old and new concepts in single neurons is the mechanism that would make a designated row take the description without the behaviour.

**Verdict: known in another unit, with no claim left.** The program's own checks (e627) narrowed placement to the description without the behaviour on a trained model, so the only positive remainder is the one-seed early-checkpoint installation, which needs seeds before it is a claim.

## 10. Is any of it novel? The method and the surviving claims (session 126)

Written 2026-10-10 after the user asked whether anything in the program is novel, and pointed out that the WDD paper never cited a paper that does the same decomposition on another architecture with simultaneous orthogonal matching pursuit. That paper was found. Searches ran on 2026-10-10; the papers marked (full) were read in their HTML version, the rest from arXiv pages.

### 10a. The method: a fixed dictionary of the model's own weights, solved by pursuit

**Head Pursuit** ([Basile, Maiorca, Doimo, Locatello and Cazzaniga, NeurIPS 2025 spotlight, arXiv 2510.21518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21518), full). Each attention head's write into the residual stream, collected over samples as a matrix, is decomposed by Simultaneous Orthogonal Matching Pursuit onto a dictionary whose atoms are rows of the model's own unembedding matrix, restricted to a concept's tokens; heads are ranked by the variance the restricted dictionary explains, about 50 pursuit steps; the logit lens is shown to be the single-sample, single-step special case; editing 1% of the heads so chosen suppresses or enhances the concept. Models: Mistral-7B-Instruct, LLaVA-NeXT-7B and 13B, Gemma3-12B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Controls: random heads matched in number and layer, and the logit lens; no rotated dictionary and no statistical threshold. This is the same object as WDD, the model's own weight rows as a fixed dictionary and orthogonal pursuit with no training, with three differences: the dictionary is the unembedding (read-out directions) where WDD's is the write rows (MLP down-projection rows, head output bases, embeddings); the thing decomposed is one head's write where WDD's is the whole state; and the question is specialisation of heads where WDD's is provenance of a state. The WDD paper (September 2026) should have cited it and did not; this note is the record of that omission, and the companion repository's related work needs the entry.

**SITH** ([Gentile et al., CVPR 2026, arXiv 2603.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24653), full). Data-free: each attention head's value-output matrix of CLIP's vision transformer is factorised by SVD and each right singular vector is explained by Coherent Orthogonal Matching Pursuit as a sparse non-negative combination of CLIP text embeddings of ConceptNet concepts; weights are decomposed onto concepts, no activations enter, and there is no null dictionary or threshold. The direction is the reverse of WDD's (weights explained by an external concept dictionary), but the pursuit-over-a-fixed-dictionary framing is shared.

**The neuron basis as the unit.** [Arora, Wu, Steinhardt and Schwettmann, ICML 2026 spotlight, arXiv 2601.22594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22594): MLP neurons are as sparse a feature basis as sparse autoencoders, and a gradient-attribution pipeline traces circuits in the neuron basis (about a hundred neurons control subject-verb agreement; neurons carry the latent steps of a multi-hop task). This is the strongest recent statement of what the program called "the row as the unit", made with more impact than the program's self-description results. Earlier: [Geva et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14680) (value vectors promote concepts), [Gurnee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610), [Voita et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04827).

**Other fixed-dictionary or weight-space decompositions.** ITDA ([arXiv 2505.17769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17769)) decomposes activations by matching pursuit onto a dictionary of other activations; MP-SAE ([arXiv 2506.03093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03093), NeurIPS 2025) makes matching pursuit the encoder of a learned dictionary; ROTATE ([arXiv 2604.06005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06005)) disentangles MLP neuron weights in vocabulary space by kurtosis-maximising rotations; ASPD ([arXiv 2609.37731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37731)) grounds learned parameter components in the activation features they read and write; bilinear MLPs ([Pearce et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.08417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08417)) allow weight-based decomposition by changing the architecture; Millidge and Black 2022 found the SVD directions of weight matrices interpretable in token space.

**Verdict on the method.** Every ingredient is in print: the model's own rows as a fixed dictionary solved by orthogonal pursuit (Head Pursuit, with the unembedding), the neuron basis as a sparse and faithful unit (Arora et al.), extreme-value thresholds (section 1). The combination WDD uses, the write rows of every component as the atoms of a whole-state decomposition, a rotated dictionary as the control that keeps everything but provenance, and a Gumbel floor for membership, was not found in one place. That is a method variant, not a new kind of instrument, and the WDD paper's claim to the read-write framing needs Head Pursuit cited as the nearest prior work.

### 10b. The surviving claim: the recruited parameter is the nearest row

**Theory predicts it in small networks.** Under small initialisation, weight vectors concentrate on a few data-determined directions ([Maennel, Bousquet and Gelly 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08367)); the early alignment phase is quantified and shown to be two-edged, giving sparse solutions and spurious stationary points from the same mechanism ([Boursier and Flammarion, JMLR, arXiv 2401.10791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10791)); initial condensation's direction and timescale are derived ([Chen et al. 2023, arXiv 2303.06561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06561)), inside the condensed regime of the phase diagram ([Luo, Xu, Ma and Zhang, JMLR 2021](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-1123.html)); first-layer neurons align to the positive or negative data by their output weight's sign ([Min, Mallada and Vidal, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12851)). For sparse-autoencoder training, [Chen, Sheen, Xiong, Wang and Yang 2025, arXiv 2506.14002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14002) prove feature recovery and, in an appendix lemma reported in a search excerpt, assign each feature to a neuron whose initial alignment with it exceeds a threshold of order the square root of 2 log(M/n), the same extreme-value scale as the program's floor; this was read from an excerpt, not the full proof. [Wang 2026, arXiv 2608.06766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06766) shows that when neurons start in the same direction, the balance of input and output weights decides which one takes the feature, by an early-advantage mechanism; that is the read-write asymmetry e631 saw, where the direction without the firing did not drift and the firing without the direction did not either.

**Developmental tracking in trained models has no parameter.** Crosscoders across pre-training snapshots find most features forming around one point of training ([Ge et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv 2509.17196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17196)); features migrate across layers during vision transformer training, early and toward shallower layers ([Järve et al., CIKM 2026, arXiv 2608.20134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20134)); class-selective neurons appear in the first epochs and are needed then but not later ([Ranadive et al. 2023, arXiv 2305.17409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17409)), the picture behind the program's "selectivity precedes entry" (session 96). Stecher et al. 2026 and SAE-Track (section 3) remain the nearest.

**Verdict on the claim.** As a principle, that the parameter recruited for a feature is the one already nearest to it is predicted by small-initialisation theory and proved for sparse-autoencoder training. As a measurement in language-model pretraining, which row of 21,000 to 53,000 is recruited for a class present in the states, forecast two checkpoints ahead, on five runs, with the learned dictionary's decoder unable to name it, nothing was found. The program's contribution is the empirical one, and it should be stated as a test of a prediction the theory already made, not as a discovery of the mechanism.

### 10c. The gradient reading and the planted row

That the class's positions supply the row's gradient follows from the gradient formula: a write column moves only at positions where its neuron fires, by the upstream gradient there. e589's figure (0.215 of the gradient from 0.006 of positions) quantifies it at scale, and e631's drift (the planted neuron moves only when it fires at the class and points near it) observes it causally at a small scale. Data attribution for interpretable units exists for heads (Mechanistic Data Attribution, section 3) and for autoencoder latents by influence functions on the autoencoder's own training set (a LessWrong report); neuron-level knowledge attribution at inference time is [arXiv 2312.12141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12141). None traces a neuron's recruitment to the pretraining positions that drove it. Not novel as a mechanism; a measurement.

## 11. The attribute-editing claims checked against the literature (session 146)

Written 2026-10-15. After sessions 144 and 145 (e659 to e662), Gemini 4 claimed the attribute edits "broke the curse" of WDD tying one-line baselines and drew six consequences. A background literature search (2026-10-15, about a hundred searches, every citation below found and checked on arXiv, the ACL Anthology, PMLR, proceedings pages or the original post) and four experiments (e663, e664, e664b, e665, atlas area 61) answer them.

**A. "Jakobsonian markedness in silicon" (e661): overstated; the transformer row-level form is partly new.** The closest precedent is [Jumelet, Zuidema and Hupkes, CoNLL 2019](https://aclanthology.org/K19-1001/): LSTM language models fall back on a default class (masculine, singular) carried in the weights, number and gender information mattering mainly for the non-default class; [van der Wal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10245) followed the asymmetry's emergence. An informal note on Gemma-2-2B ([eggsyntax, 2025](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/Lm7yi4uq9eZmueouS/eggsyntax-s-shortform/comment/ybLNhrQLaTCe8ayCt)) reports the same one-way flip. Against a general law: [Lakretz et al., NAACL 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07435) found mirror singular and plural units. Two confounds stand: markedness tracks word frequency ([Haspelmath 2006](https://zenodo.org/records/815500); [Linzen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04179)), and gated SiLU rows are one-sided by construction, so the real claim is which pole gets the rows. What may be new: the asymmetry at the level of single MLP write rows in transformers across three oppositions, with old/young as a null case, and the measured cost gap between stripping and setting a mark.

**B. "Activation echo chamber; the cumulative WDD vote is a write-origin localizer": the reader/writer distinction is standard, and the claim fails against the standard tools.** Layers read and write a shared residual stream ([Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)); what a neuron encodes is not what the model uses ([Antverg and Belinkov, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07483)); causal-tracing localization does not predict which layer edits best ([Hase et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)); knowledge neurons sit mostly in top layers and the thesis is contested ([Dai et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08696); [Niu et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02421)). Tested here: attribution patching ([Nanda](https://neelnanda.io/attribution-patching); [Syed, Rager and Conmy 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10348); [Kramár et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00745)), causal block patching and a plain direct-contribution ranking with no pursuit all match the vote (e664, e664b); only the naive activation difference fails, and the vote leans to early rows as the cumulative dictionary would make it. The neuron-level gradient-attribution baseline to cite is [Arora, Wu, Steinhardt and Schwettmann, ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22594).

**C. "1000x cleaner surgical erasure of marked behaviours (sycophancy, hedges, backdoors)": overstated.** That ablating is more surgical than adding is known ([Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717): Pile loss 2.348 base, 2.362 after directional ablation, 2.469 after activation addition). Removing behaviour through neurons or weights is established ([AURA, Suau et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/suau24a.html); [task-vector negation, Ilharco et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089); [DAMA, Limisiewicz et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18913); [fine-pruning, Liu et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12185)), and the fair comparisons are ablation, [LEACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819), [INLP](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.647), AURA and conditional steering ([CAST, Lee et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907)), not an unconditional steering vector. e661 itself limits the gap to stripping: setting a mark costs what steering costs. Nothing shows sycophancy or backdoor triggers live in a few one-pole rows ([CAA steers sycophancy in the residual stream](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.828/)).

**D. "Write once, patch everywhere": expected for light post-training, and e665 adds the heavy case.** Base-model SAEs transfer to chat models ([Kissane et al. 2024](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/fmwk6qxrpW8d4jvbd/saes-usually-transfer-between-base-and-chat-models)); fine-tuning mostly enhances existing mechanisms ([Prakash et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811); [Jain et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12786)); DPO leaves toxic MLP vectors intact ([Lee et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/lee24a.html)); against it, refusal directions shift between base and post-trained models ([Du et al., COLM 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02904)) and features drift in when they fire ([Galichin et al., EACL Findings 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.96/)). The search found nothing on heavy continued pretraining; e665 finds base rows edit Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B and its instruct model (rows at median cosine 0.63 and 0.44 with the base) as well as their own chains. "Every derivative" is still untested for distillation, quantization and merges.

**E. "A relay chain across blocks beats one row": the principle is MEMIT's.** [MEMIT (Meng et al., ICLR 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229) spreads an edit over the causally traced MLP layers; Arditi et al. ablate at every layer; cross-layer transcoders describe relays as amplification ([Ameisen et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html)); self-repair explains why single sites are compensated ([McGrath et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771); [Rushing and Nanda, ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15390)). Sign-flipping individual rows chosen by a vote is new in form only.

**F. Gemini's pole-split "two-way surgery" (e663): the mechanism holds, the payoff does not.** The vote mixes rows more active on each pole, all pushing along the pole difference, but negating the unmarked pole's rows does not set the marked pole, and four-quadrant composition fails.

**Must cite:** Jumelet, Zuidema and Hupkes 2019; Arditi et al. 2024; Suau et al. 2024 (AURA); then MEMIT, Hase et al. 2023, Kissane et al. 2024, Arora et al. 2026. Not searched: prior uses of OMP over down-projection columns beyond section 10.

Gemini's descriptions of the figures' JavaScript (readout honesty marks, weak-ablation sweeps, the sign-quadrant widget) were not checked: the archive it read was not available here.
- The first fusion's 'every neuron falls into one of three species' leaves out the largest class: 0.85 to 0.95 of neurons pass no null at all (e677); its unlearning arm (the e600 and e646 runs) could not be run because those checkpoints no longer exist, so instruction tuning stood in.
- The double negative is not the unexamined route the essay and Gemini present: GLUScope (Gerstner and Schütze 2026) already shows the gate-negative, value-negative quadrant doing a neuron's work in OLMo-7B, and Kong et al. (ICLR 2026) clamp negative gate pre-activations against a perplexity-matched control. No paper isolates the double negative's ablation, and the literature check also gave the mean-replacement control (R-Sparse; Sun et al. 2024 on massive activations), which changed e678's reading: zeroing is catastrophic, the mean nearly free.
- The scale fusion's premise holds (Pythia-160m and Pythia-410m saw the same data in the same order); its figure is from vision models, and the cleanest two-route Pythia neurons are unrelated token pairs, so a split does not distinguish branching from capacity.

**Tested.** e677: in the middle of Pythia-410m and Qwen2.5-0.5B nearly every neuron whose removal matters works through later blocks (indirect routers 0.97 and 1.00 of them at block 12), at the end nearly every one speaks directly (0.02 and 0.02 routers); early, most neurons that move their own block's readout are washed out before the output (0.94, 0.97) (e677). e678: SwiGLU's double negative carries 0.15 to 0.19 of the MLP activation energy; zeroing it everywhere is catastrophic (wikitext loss +1.25 to 4.40 nats) and worse than an L2-matched weak-activation control, its per-neuron mean is nearly free (+0.17 to 0.21), and no variant raises most of eight benchmarks while every one lowers GSM8K (Qwen2.5-1.5B 0.62 to 0.56 at the gentlest) (e678). e679: Pythia-410m divides Pythia-160m's two-route neurons into separate specialists in 0.34, 0.28, 0.49 of cases against 0.19, 0.17, 0.24 for one-route neurons and almost none for random splits, but no 410m neuron covers more than about a third of a 160m neuron's top firings (e679).
