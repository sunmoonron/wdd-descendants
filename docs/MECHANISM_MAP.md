# The mechanism map: from data to behaviour through the native vocabulary, with every arrow annotated (session 102)

After 560 experiments the question above the program is no longer "what is a WDD word" but "why does a trained network organise its computation into persistent associations between native write directions and classes of its state distribution". This page is the map of that mechanism as the record supports it: the nodes, the arrows between them, and for each arrow its status, its evidence per model, and what would still change it. WDD is the instrument; the arrows are the claims.

**Status vocabulary.** *Established*: measured against controls on more than one model. *Supported*: measured on one model or with a weaker control. *Intervened*: shown by changing the cause and watching the effect, not by correlation alone. *Correlational*: the order or the co-occurrence is measured, the cause not intervened on. *Model-dependent*: the sign or the size differs between models. *Contradicted*: the record argues against the arrow. *Unknown*: not measured.

## The loop, as the record now reads it

The data fixes a partition of the state space into recurring context classes (A1), and the cloud's covariance holds the directions along which those classes are extreme (A2). Neurons become selective for a class before any row is extreme there (A3). A row enters the vocabulary by turning into a direction the cloud already speaks; the cloud does not grow along the row (A4). The extreme is then built by thousands of small writes that cohere across the class, the row's own write first among them in OLMo and Mamba and far down the list in Pythia (A6). What a later checkpoint's neuron carries into an earlier model is its write direction, not its norm and not its input weights (A7). None of this makes the ledger a judge of behaviour: it says which native directions built a state, and a wrong answer or a corrupted context is built from the same directions as a right one (A8).

## The object, after session 103

Three things had been conflated under "a word". The class is the partition of contexts, and the partition is the corpus's surface structure: shared by every language model trained to predict the corpus (0.3-0.5 twins across seeds, scales, architectures and families), predicted at AUC 0.85 from the token, the previous token and the position, not an output partition, and shared by two vision models with different objectives only in its spatial layout. The direction along which a class is spoken is the model's own and carries the class's downstream effect: two words sharing a class have effects as alike as their directions (0.86), and a class that changes rows changes its direction and its effect. The row is the direction's implementation: recruited to a spoken direction (e571), able to draw usage onto itself when frozen without bringing its class (e576), transplantable as a label without computation (e577), and read by later blocks no more than any other row (e574). The data fixes the classes, training fixes the directions, and the rows are where the directions live. WDD reads the direction through the row. Session 104 adds the qualification GPT asked for and the record supports: the class's downstream effect is a function of the direction, the coalition that produces it and the readout that consumes it together. The coalition with the direction carries the association between checkpoints (0.93 of entrants become words) and nothing short of the whole model carries the computation; in the later model the class's computation needs its coalition specifically (e578). And what the instrument is for, stated once: it says which of the model's own directions built a state, with provenance; in the toy that statistic separates every generalising run from every memorising one from the weights and the training inputs alone, at the moment generalisation happens (e579).

## The instrument placed (session 105)

Two tests designed to let WDD lose. First, the strongest existing method on the same decomposition: EleutherAI's sparse autoencoder for Pythia-160m, on the same states as the words at block 6, has features whose producer coalitions are as coherent as the words' (0.69 against 0.67; random directions 0.15) and as necessary (zeroing the top 128 producers drops a feature's activation at its class by 0.92, a word's projection by 0.77; random neurons 0.00-0.01), with different producers (a feature and its nearest word share them at Jaccard 0.07) (e581). The producer-consumer decomposition of A6 belongs to any spoken direction of the cloud that attribution can reach, from either dictionary. Second, the detector benchmark (e580, e580b): over twenty toy trajectories with the classic label-free signals logged beside the WDD statistics, the native excess predicts generalisation a thousand steps ahead at AUC 0.81 (its change 0.82), above every generic signal (the weight norm 0.69, the states' participation ratio 0.48, the ReLU density 0.61, training loss 0.05) and below the task's own Fourier concentration (0.83, its change 0.96), which only a modular-arithmetic toy affords; it separates the runs that will generalise from those that will not without ordering the moment within a run. The placement that follows: WDD is not a better feature-discovery method. Its distinct element is parameter provenance of the association, the row behind a direction, which can be transplanted (the association travels, the computation does not), frozen (usage travels, the class does not) and followed through training (the class precedes the row; the row turns into a direction the cloud already speaks). That provenance buys the developmental questions, A3 and A4 and C1, and not the descriptive ones, A5 and A6, which a learned dictionary reaches too.

## The developmental line, with its control (session 106)

Sixteen checkpoints of Pythia-410m on 24 sequences, a sparse autoencoder trained at every checkpoint, and the histories of 241 eventual words (e582-e584). Every class coheres in the cloud from the first checkpoint, measured without any row: the corpus's partition (A1, e574) is there before the model has words for it. The rows are recruited at step 8000 at the median, spread over the whole window, and the coalition coheres five checkpoints before recruitment for 84% of the words that reach it (A6, session 95 at the finer resolution). The per-checkpoint autoencoder has a persistent feature for 31% of the classes; where it has one, the feature precedes the row's recruitment (61%); at recruitment its feature's decoder has cosine 0.11 with the row (7% at 0.3 or more), and it comes to point at the row, for 33 of 241 words, seven checkpoints later. The order of birth, class then coalition then row, is a fact about the model that either dictionary can see where the learned one has a feature at all; which parameter takes responsibility, and when, is what the native dictionary carries for free. Limits: the row-free presence measure saturates (a surface-matched null is needed to date the class), and a longitudinally matched, larger autoencoder is the stronger control still to be built.

## Responsibility migration read directly (session 107)

Three experiments outside the control loop. The relay (e585): over sixteen checkpoints a class has one speaker row at the median, after a gap of eight checkpoints in which no row is over the floor at half of it; the rare handoffs (187 across 253 classes) pass through overlaps (183). The class's own direction, the unit mean of its states, is at cosine 0.85 with its step-16000 direction at step 4000, when the eventual row is the speaker for 6% of classes, and at 0.86 at the final model, when the 16000 row is still the speaker for 38%: the latent object is the class with its direction, present and pointing the right way before any row speaks it, and it outlives the row that does (C1, sharpened). Recruitment as geometry (e587): two checkpoints before a row is recruited it is already the first of 53,248 rows by its projection ratio over the class for 52% of words and in the top ten for 84%, eight checkpoints before for 62%: which row a class will get is predictable from the class alone (A4, made predictive). The blocked recruit (e586): in the toy, with the 32 eventual words frozen from step 1500, the network still generalises (test accuracy 1.00) and forms a full vocabulary on other neurons (advantage 0.72, words at 0.97 against 0.40 rotated); the frozen rows are words at the end for 3%; a third of the base classes re-appear on substitute rows at profile correlation over 0.5 (median 0.38), against half for the natural sharing of a class between two words in the unfrozen run (0.52): the row is replaceable, and in the toy the classes themselves are only partly fixed by the task. The data gives the class and its direction, training recruits the nearest row to it, and the row is replaceable: A1, A4 and A5 as one process.

## The forecast, with no future in the class (session 108)

The centrepiece a critic proposed, run as a real forecast (e588): at every checkpoint the states are clustered with no row involved (spherical k-means, 512 clusters on Pythia-410m's 12k positions, 128 on OLMo-1B's 2k), every row's class-side score is its largest mean projection ratio over any cluster, its pull toward the class it is nearest to, and the target is which rows that are not words at t are words at t + k. Two checkpoints ahead the class side forecasts recruitment at AUC 0.93 on both families (Pythia-410m 0.927, OLMo-1B 0.932; norm 0.42 / 0.48), and the row's own maximum over the floor, the signal of area 32, forecasts at 0.93 / 0.97; combined, 0.93 / 0.97. The class side adds nothing to the row's own maximum: the pull of a class and the row's maximum are one quantity seen from two ends, and either reads the assignment ahead of time (the usage of a row that is not yet a word does slightly better still, 0.96 / 0.97). The stricter class-side score, the pull toward a class the row would own outright, is degenerate (most rows own no cluster) and sits at chance. The clock does not work: an entrant's arrival extrapolated from its pull's slope two to four checkpoints before correlates with the actual recruitment checkpoint at 0.51 / 0.47, all of it the origin's time; with the origin removed the leads correlate at 0.04 / 0.04. Which row will be recruited is forecastable; when, from the pull's slope, is not. What the native dictionary supplies is the row the forecast names; the forecast itself any method can compute. This is the stopping signal the critic proposed, in the good direction: A1, A4 and A5 close as a predictive chain.

## The learning signal behind the turn, and the removed speaker at scale (session 109)

A4's mechanism: two checkpoints before recruitment the eventual row's gradient step, on 24 sequences, points toward its class's row-free direction at mean cosine +0.020, four standard errors above zero and above non-recruits matched on their current cosine to the class (+0.008, not significant), random rows (+0.002) and a class-shuffled null (-0.003); the class's positions, 0.6% of all positions, supply 22% of the row's gradient (matched non-recruits 9%, random rows 4%), and that part of the step points at the class (e589). The turn is a drift the class's own loss drives, small in any one batch and accumulated over the two billion tokens of a real interval; it is not a jump, and it does not point at the row's eventual direction any more than at its current one. A5's replaceability at scale: thirty words' write columns zeroed and frozen at step 8000 cost their classes +0.005 nats (the coalition builds the state; the row's own write is small), and 2.5M tokens of continued training on other Pile text produce no substitute speakers (0.17 of classes, against 0.23 with no training and 0.30 in the unfrozen continuation, all of them the classes' pre-existing second speakers) while raising the loss everywhere by about 0.1 nats, so the proxy degrades the model rather than continuing its training (e590). Replacement at scale is not reached within the proxy's means; the real test needs the training data order, and the record says so. The paper (PAPER.md) states the whole map as one sequence.

## The arrows still open, in order of value

1. The readout's specificity: a block-wise transplant of blocks 13-23 within one checkpoint's geometry (the mixed-checkpoint readout of e578 moved everything away), and whether a class's readers are the same neurons across checkpoints.
2. The detector at scale: whether the native excess of a language model's words tracks held-out loss through training (Pythia's checkpoints have both) and separates memorised from generalised text within a model (e579 found it a label-free detector of generalisation in the toy, never false and never early).
3. A6 and A2 on Mamba: precedence, the gate and the graft need checkpoints; a small SSM trained on the box would give them.
4. The reverse loop past grokking: the frozen-row toy run to 25000 steps, and at scale a row frozen through Pythia's training.
5. The vision partition with more images and two models of the same objective (the image level was uninformative at 40).
