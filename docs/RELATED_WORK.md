# Related work, as it bears on the descendant program

Compiled 2026-09-19 from the sources listed at the end. Where only an abstract or summary was read, the entry says so. Each entry ends with how the program relates.

## 1. Classical sparse approximation (what WDD is)

- Matching pursuit (Mallat and Zhang, 1993) and orthogonal matching pursuit (Pati, Rezaiifar and Krishnaprasad, Asilomar 1993) are the greedy sparse-coding algorithms WDD uses over a fixed dictionary; the recovery guarantees under mutual coherence and the restricted isometry property (Tropp, 2004, "Greed is good"; later k-step analyses) are the theory of when a greedy pursuit finds the true support. Stability selection (Meinshausen and Bühlmann, JRSS-B 2010) is the resampling certificate the program adopted for OMP supports.
- Relation. WDD is this classical object with the model's own write directions as the dictionary. The program's result that reading works at birth and fails as a decomposition downstream is a statement about the dictionary (the atoms are transported and mixed), not about the pursuit. The certificate results (stability selection; the per-atom null-model z-score) are the classical remedies applied unchanged.

## 2. The residual stream, lenses and the Jacobian lens

- The residual-stream framework (Elhage et al., 2021, "A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits") treats every component as reading from and writing to a shared stream; the logit lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) reads an intermediate state through the unembedding; the tuned lens (Belrose et al., 2023) learns an affine map per layer before the unembedding; Dar et al. (2022) project weights and neurons into vocabulary space.
- The Jacobian lens and J-space (Gurnee, Sofroniew, Lindsey et al., Transformer Circuits, July 2026, "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models"). J_ℓ = E_{t, t' ≥ t, prompt}[∂h_final,t'/∂h_ℓ,t], averaged over positions and a corpus, composed with the unembedding; J-space is the span of sparse non-negative combinations of J-lens vectors (10 to 25 components by gradient pursuit), accounting for 5 to 10% of activation variance, present in intermediate layers, and shown to mediate verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible generalisation and selectivity (Claude Sonnet 4.5, Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5, 4.6).
- Relation. The descendant is the un-averaged, per-write Jacobian image of a WDD atom between two intermediate levels; the operator of e248 is the J-lens construction between intermediate levels; the descendant-to-logit map of e262 to e269 is J-space applied to a descendant; e275b and e283 measure the gap between the corpus-averaged operator and the per-token Jacobian (cosine 0.07 to 0.47 at mid depth for natural centroids, yet the per-token descendant pushed through the averaged operator predicts its own future at 0.43 to 0.78). J-space indexes what the model can say; WDD indexes what it writes; the theory in THEORY.md is the bridge.

## 3. Linearity and dynamics of the residual stream

- Razzhigaev et al. (ACL 2024, "Your Transformer is Secretly Linear") report near-perfect Procrustes linearity (0.99) between consecutive residual states in decoders, dropping when the residual is removed because each block's update norm is small relative to the stream; layers can be pruned or linearly approximated with little loss.
- Szablewski (2025, "Activation Transport Operators", arXiv 2508.17540). Affine, rank-constrained linear maps from an upstream residual state to a downstream one k layers later, evaluated in the feature space of downstream SAE decoders; "transport efficiency" (R² relative to the best rank-r linear predictor) distinguishes features linearly transported from those synthesised by non-linear computation; many early-to-middle-layer features transport efficiently.
- Fernando and Guitchounts (2025, "Transformer Dynamics", arXiv 2502.12131; and May 2026, "Dynamics of the Transformer Residual Stream: Coupling Spectral Geometry to Network Topology", arXiv 2605.14258). The residual stream as a dynamical system; a full Jacobian eigendecomposition across three production LLMs shows a monotonic spectral gradient with depth from non-normal, rotation-dominated early layers to near-symmetric late layers, and a cumulative low-rank bottleneck funnelling perturbations into a small fraction of dimensions; both learned rather than architectural; graph-community position predicts amplification or suppression.
- Heimersheim and Turner (2023) document exponential growth of residual norms across depth (about 4.5% per layer in GPT-2 XL) and attribute it to LayerNorm making features easier to overshadow than to cancel.
- Older residual-network views: ensembles of shallow paths (Veit et al., 2016), unrolled iterative estimation (Greff et al., 2017), iterative inference (Jastrzębski et al., 2018); the interacting-particle and clustering analysis of attention dynamics (Geshkovski, Letrouit, Polyanskiy, Rigollet, 2023 and Bull. AMS 2025).
- Relation. The program's transport operator is an activation transport operator in Szablewski's sense, fitted data-free on random injections rather than on natural states, and applied to individual writes rather than to SAE features; e275b, e281 and e283 are the program's transport-efficiency statements. Razzhigaev's near-linearity is the between-state statement whose perturbation counterpart the program measures: the per-token response is linear in the perturbation to about the natural amplitude (e278) and composes (e281), while the state-to-state map is dominated by the identity path. Fernando and Guitchounts's spectral gradient and low-rank bottleneck are the closest published measurements to the program's Jacobian findings (generic spectrum with a spread of 11 to 50, e282; no null space, e273; rotation zones after birth and in the last block, e279); the program adds the per-write, causal, cross-token-resolved version and the functional consequences, and finds no dimensional collapse of function (e273).

## 4. Self-repair, compensation and error correction

- McGrath et al. (2023, "The Hydra Effect"). Ablating an attention layer causes later layers to compensate; late MLPs down-regulate the maximum-likelihood token; at middle layers about 70% of the logit reduction is restored; layers are loosely coupled.
- Rushing and Nanda (2024) explore self-repair further (not read here). Ferreira da Silva and Heimersheim (June 2026, "Evidence for feature-specific error correction in LLMs", arXiv 2606.24964). Residual-stream perturbations along genuine feature directions (contrastive, MELBO, SAE decoders) are less robust than along random or PCA directions, modelled as an L_p response with p > 2 for features and p ≈ 2 for controls, in six models; read as error correction privileging feature directions.
- Relation. The program's erasure matrix, learned diagonal contraction (−0.15 to −0.45 along any direction) and exact residual identity (footprint = −write + later MLP + attention responses) are the write-level anatomy of compensation; the finding that the contraction is a diagonal of a mixing map rather than a cancellation reframes "self-repair" as dispersal with conserved energy. Ferreira da Silva and Heimersheim's feature-specific response is the closest published claim to the program's e276, which found no leverage difference between natural descendants and random-vector descendants at the natural footprint norm; the two are not in conflict (different quantity: response shape versus response magnitude; different directions: SAE features versus neuron writes) but a direct comparison is an open item. The massive-neuron reaction carried by one MLP (e284b) is a compensation of the Hydra kind localised to a single component.

## 5. Sparse dictionaries learned from data: SAEs, transcoders, crosscoders

- Sparse autoencoders (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024) learn overcomplete dictionaries of the residual stream; transcoders (Dunefsky, Chlenski, Nanda, NeurIPS 2024) replace an MLP by a sparse wide layer and factor circuits into input-invariant and input-dependent parts; sparse feature circuits (Marks et al., 2024/2025) build causal graphs over SAE features; cross-layer transcoders and attribution graphs (Ameisen et al., Lindsey et al., Transformer Circuits 2025) let a feature read at one layer and write to all later MLPs, motivated by the observation that similar features activate across many layers (cross-layer superposition); crosscoders (Lindsey et al., 2024) share a dictionary across layers; multi-layer SAEs (Lawson, Farnik, Houghton, Aitchison, ICLR 2025) train one SAE on every layer's residual stream and find that a latent typically activates at a single layer for a given token while different tokens activate it at different layers, with more multi-layer activation in larger models.
- Relation. The program's dictionary is the model's weights, not a learned one, so its atoms are exactly the writes; the descendant results say what happens to such an atom after it is written: it becomes a dense, token-specific vector with a small coherent part (e277), which a fixed cross-layer atom cannot represent (the transported-dictionary kill) but which remains classifiable and functionally sufficient. Lawson et al.'s single-layer activation of latents and the CLT motivation of cross-layer features are two sides of the same fact the program measures directly: a write's consistent component persists across layers at 5 to 33% of its energy while the rest is recomputed per token. The distinction the reviewer raised, transporting basis vectors versus transporting features, is in these terms the distinction between J̄ ŵ (a fixed transported atom) and J_t ŵ (the per-token descendant); cross-layer transcoders learn something between the two.

## 6. Superposition and feature geometry

- Toy models of superposition (Elhage et al., 2022) and the linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2023) frame features as directions packed into fewer dimensions.
- Relation. The program's superposition results (e241: additivity error 0.14 to 0.24 for two superposed writes rising to 0.37 to 0.60 for sixteen at natural amplitudes; recall above chance throughout) concern the transport of superposed writes, not their packing; the theory (THEORY.md D2, e285b) attributes the rising error to the total perturbation norm entering the nonlinear range, not to interference between features. The descendant is not a low-dimensional feature: it needs the full dimension to reconstruct (e272) and has no functional null space (e273).

## 7. Massive activations, attention sinks, super weights, outlier dimensions

- Sun et al. (2024, "Massive Activations in Large Language Models") show rare, huge activations in a few fixed dimensions, largely input-agnostic, acting as biases and creating attention sinks (StreamingLLM, Xiao et al., 2023, keeps the sink tokens); Gu et al. (2024, "When Attention Sink Emerges") and a 2026 survey (arXiv 2604.10098) trace their emergence; Vemula (June 2026, arXiv 2606.20743) finds that models given a protected decode-only stream rebuild massive activations inside it, i.e. they are functional rather than incidental. Yu et al. (2024, "The Super Weight") find single scalars whose pruning destroys generation; Timkey and van Schijndel (2021) and Kovaleva et al. (2021) describe rogue and outlier dimensions dominating similarity measures; later work links outlier dimensions to token frequency.
- Relation. The program's massive neuron (SmolLM2 block-2 neuron 90, coefficient 13× the median) is a massive-activation writer in Sun et al.'s sense at the neuron level. The program's contribution is causal and transport-level: its direction transports by the generic law at every amplitude, its natural removal response is anti-parallel to its transplant, and that reaction is one MLP at one block (e278, e284b). GPT-2's massive channel 447 explains the one exception to the no-null-space result (e273b). The user's separate superweight study (section 22, Y29) tested the J-lens verbalisability of a super weight and found it modestly lower per unit norm; the present program did not verify that claim.

## 8. Steering, function and task vectors; causal abstraction

- Function vectors (Todd et al., 2023) and task vectors (Hendel et al., 2023) are residual-stream vectors, extracted from in-context demonstrations, that induce a task when added at a layer; activation addition and steering vectors (Turner et al., 2023; Arditi et al., 2024 on refusal) add directions and read the behavioural change. Causal abstraction (Geiger et al., 2023) and distributed alignment search (Geiger et al., 2024) find linear subspaces whose interchange interventions realise a high-level variable.
- Relation. Every steering experiment implicitly relies on the transport the program measures: the added vector's effect is G · J · v. The program's specific results, that an injected vector's effect is linear in the vector to about the natural amplitude, that it interpolates without snapping through the whole rest of the network (e280), that the leverage is generic (e276) and that the descendant coordinate predicts its own future (e283), are the perturbation-dynamics facts steering methods presuppose. In causal-abstraction terms the descendant space is a coordinate in which single-write interventions compose and interpolate; the program did not test interchange interventions between contexts.

## 9. Signal propagation and isometry at initialisation

- Dynamical isometry (Saxe et al., 2014; Pennington, Schoenholz, Ganguli, 2017) is the condition that all singular values of the input-output Jacobian concentrate near one; residual networks achieve it universally at initialisation (Tarnowski et al., 2019); signal-propagation theories of deep transformers at initialisation identify rank collapse and its avoidance (e.g. the 2025 unified theory of signal propagation).
- Relation. The program's finding that transport is the identity at initialisation and that dispersal, erasure and late rotation are learned (e243, e217, e279) is the trained-network continuation of dynamical isometry: training moves the Jacobian from near-identity to a generic spectrum with an order-of-magnitude spread (e282) while keeping the root-mean-square gain of order one (energy conservation). The angle preservation the program first called isometry is, by THEORY.md D8 and e285, concentration of measure for random-component pairs, the same mechanism the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma relies on.

## 10. Priority check after the quotient results (added 2026-09-19, session 30)

A citation-chain pass from the nearest papers, looking specifically for the object the program ended with: a low-dimensional functional coordinate induced on arbitrary residual perturbations, transported almost unchanged, shared across perturbation families near the output, unrelated to the operators' spectra, and algebraically connected to the write dictionary (z = Z c). Sources below were read at abstract level unless stated.

- Fernando and Guitchounts (May 2026, arXiv 2605.14258). Full Jacobian eigendecompositions of production models; a learned, cumulative low-rank bottleneck funnelling perturbations into few dimensions. Closest in spirit. Distinguished by e305 and e308: the program's functional coordinate has chance-level overlap with every spectral subspace of the transport operator (top and bottom singular, update, slowest and fastest modes) and with the backward sensitivity subspace, and generic gain. Their object is the operator's preferred subspaces; the program's is the equivalence structure the read-out induces on perturbations, which e310 shows is acquired even by perturbations orthogonal to the natural manifold. Cite and state the negative.
- Olivieri and Pérez Rodríguez (May 2026, arXiv 2605.25225, "Transformer Field Theory"). Patching as source insertion, first-order sensitivity fields, Green functions for propagation, adjoint patch selection, a bounded local linear regime, composition of Green operators, anisotropic propagation, reduced response descriptions, in GPT-2-style models. This is the theoretical predecessor of the program's transport J and its composition (e281), linear range (e278), impulse response (e299) and forward-backward comparison (e308); those parts are not novel and should be attributed. The abstract does not define equivalence classes of perturbations or a shared low-dimensional functional coordinate; the program's quotient (e306, e310 to e313) is the step beyond.
- Sarkar and Deka (May 2026, arXiv 2605.08740, "Causal Dimensionality of Transformer Representations"). Causal dimensionality as the effective rank of the expected Jacobian outer product, estimated through SAE width sweeps and attribution patching in Gemma-2; a "representational-causal wedge" with far fewer causally influential directions than representational ones (κ about 0.86 of the width by their estimate, a participation-ratio lower bound near 280, invariant across model sizes). Related in name and in the wedge. Distinguished by construction and by result: the program's coordinate is defined by held-out prediction and intervention on actual perturbations, not by attribution-patching estimates over an SAE basis; it finds no null space in the logit response (e273, e285: gain is isotropic across principal-component classes and no operator direction is null), so the "most directions leave the output unchanged" reading is not what the five small models show at the natural perturbation norm; and the funnel measured here is tens of directions for effect and identity, about 128 for the linear future, the full width for reconstruction (e286, e294). Cite and contrast the definitions.
- Gurnee, Sofroniew, Lindsey et al. (July 2026, Transformer Circuits, J-lens and J-space). Averaged Jacobian to the final state read through the unembedding, with interventions on J-space directions. Distinguished by e305 and e308 (the functional coordinate is not the read-out's high-gain subspace) and by e316 (the quotient adds to an averaged-Jacobian lens of the writes in predicting joint ablations; see the session-30 entry).
- Sharma, Dawes and Raval (April 2026, arXiv 2604.22128). Decodable but causally unused residual subspaces in bracket-sequence transformers. The same decodability-versus-use distinction the program drew between provenance (readable) and function (used), and between the token-specific remainder (carried, unread; e295) and the core.
- Avrahamy, Gur-Arieh and Geva (COLM 2026, arXiv 2604.06005, ROTATE). Weight-space decomposition of MLP neurons in vocabulary space by kurtosis-maximising rotations, no forward passes. A weight-first neuron description method; the program's functional atlas (e316) is weight-first too but maps neurons to a transported functional coordinate validated by joint ablation and cross-block substitution rather than to vocabulary channels.
- Bushnaq et al. (SPD 2025; VPD, Goodfire, April 2026, "Interpreting Language Model Parameters"). Rank-one parameter subcomponents with read and write directions, scaled to a 67M-parameter transformer. Parameter-space mechanisms; WDD's atoms are the model's own down-projection rows, already rank-one write directions, so WDD is a fixed special case of the coordinate system these methods learn, and the quotient is what that coordinate system becomes downstream.
- Park, Choe and Veitch (ICML 2024) and Garg, Kleinberg and Peng (2026). The causal inner product for linear representations; the separation of linear representation from linear accessibility. The program's causal metric (e296) is the empirical analogue of the former on perturbations; the physical-versus-accessible dimension split (512 to 1024 against 8 to 32) is an instance of the latter.
- Klabunde et al. (ACM Computing Surveys 2025) and Smith et al. (ICML 2025, "Functional Alignment Can Mislead"). Functional versus representational similarity, and the warning that functional alignment does not imply informational similarity. The program's e311 is the intervention-level form: functional equivalence of perturbations is nearly independent of physical proximity and of family.
- Geiger et al. (causal abstraction, JMLR; DAS). The framework-level predecessor: a lower-dimensional causal model faithfully abstracting a network. The program runs the inverse procedure, from perturbations and their effects to the induced quotient, without a pre-specified high-level variable.
- Todd et al. (function vectors, ICLR 2024). Compact, composable, transferable causal vectors for tasks; a special case in the program's terms of a coordinate in the functional space, tied to a specified function.

Priority assessment. Almost certainly prior art: the dynamical-systems view of the residual stream, first-order Jacobian transport, Green-function propagation and composition, causal intervention on directions, compact causal directions, function vectors, causal abstraction, functional-versus-representational similarity, parameter-space decomposition, weight-space neuron description. Not found as such: the equivalence structure induced on arbitrary residual perturbations by the read-out, measured by held-out prediction and intervention; its acquisition by perturbations off the natural manifold; its convergence across perturbation families with depth; its independence from the operators' spectra; its algebraic link to the write dictionary, z = Z c, tested by joint ablation; and the functional atlas of neurons built from it. Those are the claims to make, in that scope.

## 11. Phase 2 (circuits, co-selection, drift; added 2026-09-23, session 32)

The phase-2 agenda was anchored on CarolusRenniusVitellius, "What if not Circuits?" (LessWrong, 2026-09-21), and its comments (Lehalleur on singular learning theory, Bushnaq on redundancy versus superposition and on two kinds of drift, Brendan Long on neuron turnover, cdt on speciation, testingthewaters on analog computation). The work it needed to be checked against:

- Olsson et al. (2022, Transformer Circuits, "In-context learning and induction heads") and Wang et al. (2022, "Interpretability in the wild", IOI in GPT-2 small). The ground truth for the two positive controls: the head classes used for GPT-2's IOI circuit (name movers, backup and negative name movers, S-inhibition, induction, duplicate-token, previous-token) are theirs, and the prefix-matching and previous-token attention scores follow Olsson et al.
- McGrath et al. (2023, "The Hydra effect") and Rushing and Nanda (2024, self-repair). Compensation after ablation. The controls' super-additive pairs and the joint-over-sum ratio of about 7 for GPT-2's top-8 induction heads are the redundancy these papers describe; the near-zero joint effect of GPT-2's top-8 IOI heads is the same phenomenon plus series structure.
- Baker, Wang, Hoogland and Murfet (April 2025, arXiv 2504.18274, "Structural Inference: Interpreting Small Language Models with Susceptibilities"). Linear-response susceptibilities of component observables to data-distribution perturbations, estimated with local SGLD, factorised into signed per-token contributions; the response matrix's low-rank structure separates multigram and induction heads in a 3M-parameter transformer. This is the prior, and more principled, form of the post's co-selection idea. The program's e363 (per batch) and e368 (per write position) are first-order, posterior-free analogues (the gradient of the loss along each head's own write), and e368's move from batches to tokens repeats their design choice of per-token factorisation.
- Gordon, Baker, Wang, Snell, van Wingerden and Murfet (January 2026, arXiv 2601.12703, "Towards Spectroscopy: Susceptibility Clusters in Language Models"). 510 susceptibility clusters in Pythia-14M, half matching SAE features; clusters are data-distribution modes rather than heads or neurons.
- Wang, Baker, Gordon et al. (August 2025, arXiv 2508.00331, "Embryology of a Language Model"). Developmental structure in susceptibility space over training; the prior art for any "developmental motif" claim built on co-selection across checkpoints (e363 and e368 on Pythia checkpoints, e364).
- Luick (November 2024, arXiv 2411.07071, "Universal Response and Emergence of Induction in LLMs"). Weak single-token residual perturbations with a scale-invariant response regime, used to locate where induction emerges across layers of Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B and GPT-2-XL. A perturbation-response method for induction close in spirit to the program's injection families (e310 onward) and to e365's basis-free image of the quotient.
- Representational drift in neuroscience (for example Driscoll et al. 2017, Cell; Rule, O'Leary and Harvey 2019, Current Opinion in Neurobiology). Stable behaviour over drifting single-unit tuning; the source of the post's framing. The phase-2 drift measurements (e360, e362) compare units by a basis-free logit signature so that implementation drift (write direction) and function drift (signature) can be separated, which is Bushnaq's distinction.

- McGrath et al. (2023) and Rushing and Nanda (2024) on self-repair: e377 and e382 add a measurement caveat to loss-based readings of backup behaviour: the convexity of cross-entropy makes additive logit effects super-additive on the loss, and the circuit/readout split of a loss interaction is path dependent.
- Lenc and Vedaldi (2015) and Bansal, Nakkiran and Barak (2021, "Revisiting model stitching"): model stitching with a learned affine layer. e378 applies it across training time within one model: late lower parts are not swappable raw, and an affine map repairs about half of the penalty.
- Jensen (1906): the convexity inequality behind e382; the softmax readout is a log-sum-exp (Gibbs free energy), convex in the logits.

## 13. Literature check of the phase-3 claims (added 2026-09-23, session 34)

A targeted search (abstracts and key sections, 2024-2026 included) with a verdict per claim.

1. Reader-weighted WDD recovering circuit edges. PARTLY KNOWN. The exact hook-based split of an attention head's query or key input over upstream components is standard: ARENA's decompose_qk_input (induction via K-composition), and Franco and Crovella (NeurIPS 2025; ACC++, arXiv 2602.13483), who project a reader's inputs onto its QK singular vectors, split scores over upstream heads, MLPs and embeddings with the reader's norm frozen, and recover IOI circuits in GPT-2, Pythia-160M and Gemma-2 from single passes. The same bilinear split over learned features: He et al. 2024, Ge et al. 2024, Kamath, Ameisen et al. 2025 (QK attributions for attribution graphs). Weight-only edges: Merullo et al. 2024 (SVD channels from S-inhibition heads to movers), Yamagiwa et al. 2026 and Wang 2026. Not found: a state-only upstream basis made of the model's own write vectors (no cache, no learned dictionary). The exact split is its upper bound.
2. Loss convexity biasing interaction signs. PARTLY KNOWN. Scale dependence of interaction is textbook (VanderWeele and Knol 2014); Heimersheim and Nanda 2024 and Zhang and Nanda 2024 note additivity in logits and saturation of log-probability and probability, without analysing interaction signs; Rushing and Nanda 2024 attribute about 30% of self-repair to final-norm rescaling. Backups are also reported on linear readouts (IOI logit difference, Wang et al. 2022; Hydra effect on centred logits, McGrath et al. 2023; Aghilar et al. 2026). Not found: the measured sign-flip rate of head-pair interactions between loss and logit; the second-order argument itself is two lines.
3. First-order selection vanishing at convergence. KNOWN (Mozer and Smolensky 1989; LeCun et al. 1990, Optimal Brain Damage; Molchanov et al. 2017; Michel et al. 2019 use absolute gradients for this reason). Co-selection of circuits across checkpoints: PARTLY KNOWN through developmental interpretability (Wang et al. 2024 rLLC; Baker et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025 Embryology; Lee et al. 2025 on influence at transitions); gradient co-selection of known circuits across checkpoints, the LessWrong proposal, not found.
4. Dropout manufacturing redundancy. PARTLY KNOWN and contested. Wang et al. 2022 conjectured dropout causes backup heads; McGrath et al. 2023 report that the Hydra effect occurs "even in language models trained without any form of dropout" (Chinchilla 7B); Rushing and Nanda 2024 find self-repair in Pythia (dropout 0); Singh et al. 2024 (ICML) find emergent redundancy in small attention-only induction models trained without dropout, on accuracy; DropHead and LayerDrop train for robustness to removal. The phase-3 toy result is a toy-and-readout statement.
5. Stitching across training time. PARTLY KNOWN. Bansal et al. 2021 stitched ResNet bottoms across epochs through a learned layer and found them compatible; SVCCA (Raghu et al. 2017) and Martinez et al. 2024 report bottom-up convergence of layer representations (Pythia layers stabilise early). Not found: block swaps between checkpoints of one language-model run; e378's result (late lower blocks not swappable, an affine map repairs about half) runs against the bottom-up reading.
6. The removal knee as a softmax-readout effect. PARTLY KNOWN (saturation of log-probability, Heimersheim and Nanda 2024; saturated attention softmax and attribution-patching false negatives, Kramár et al. 2024; downstream curvature dominating attribution-patching error, Zhang and Wang 2026). Not found: head-scale curves split into readout nonlinearity and logit-trajectory nonlinearity; the locally quadratic loss is the Optimal Brain Damage assumption confirmed.

## 14. WDD judged as a sparse autoencoder (added 2026-09-23, session 35)

- The loss-recovered splice metric is the standard evaluation of residual-stream sparse autoencoders (Bloom 2024, open-source GPT-2 small residual SAEs; Gao et al. 2024, "Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders"); e388 applies it to WDD's non-learned dictionary, with OMP inference instead of a learned encoder.
- Korznikov, Galichin, Dontsov, Rogov, Oseledets and Tutubalina (2026, arXiv 2602.14111, "Sanity Checks for Sparse Autoencoders: Do SAEs Beat Random Baselines?"): baselines with random feature directions or random activation patterns match trained SAEs on interpretability, sparse probing and causal editing. e388's rotated and random dictionaries reach the same loss recovered as WDD at 64-128 atoms, consistent with that finding; WDD's advantage appears at 8-32 atoms and requires the model's own write rows (e389, e390).
- Not found in a search: the model's own write vectors (embeddings, MLP rows, head output bases) used as a fixed dictionary and evaluated by loss recovered against rotated and random dictionaries.

## 15. Self-description, convergence and private languages (added 2026-09-24, sessions 36 and 37)

- Minimum description length. Rissanen 1978 and Grünwald 2007 give two-part MDL. Ayonrinde, Pearce and Sharkey 2024 (arXiv 2410.11179, MDL-SAEs) measure the description length of SAE explanations of activations under a learned dictionary. Session 37's self-description length uses the network's own weights as the codebook, which costs nothing extra, and measures adequacy functionally (loss recovered) rather than by reconstruction.
- Sparsifying computation rather than activations. Jacobian SAEs (Farnik et al. 2025, arXiv 2502.18147) ask for sparse input-output Jacobians. The dark matter of SAEs (Engels et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.14670) is the part of the SAE error that is linearly predictable from the input. e391's dense writing (thousands of actual writes) against a sparse own-word description (dozens) is a different axis: sparsity of the description, not of the writes or the Jacobian.
- Features across training. SAE-Track (Xu et al. 2024, arXiv 2412.17626) follows features across checkpoints with recurrently initialised SAEs and reports formation phases and directional drift. e396 is the weight-native counterpart with a functional test: which stage's words can describe which stage's states.
- Features across seeds. Gurnee et al. 2024 (arXiv 2401.12181) find 1-5% of GPT-2 neurons universal across five seeds by activation correlation. PolyPythias (van der Wal et al. 2025, arXiv 2503.09543) released the seeds used in e398. Fehlauer, Mahowald and Pimentel 2025 (arXiv 2509.26643) find four phases of cross-seed convergence (uniform, sharp convergence, sharp divergence, slow reconvergence). Whether e397's step-4000 false friends coincide with their divergence phase is untested.
- Stitching and feature transfer. Lenc and Vedaldi 2015 and Bansal et al. 2021 stitch networks through fitted layers. Chen, Merullo, Stolfo and Pavlick 2025 (arXiv 2506.06609, NeurIPS 2025) transfer SAEs, probes and steering vectors between Pythia sizes through affine maps between residual streams. e398b's caution applies to any such transfer: random directions pushed through a state-fitted linear map already describe the target as well as its own words (0.59 against 0.59), so transferred features need random-direction controls.
- Shared subspaces carry function. SVCCA (Raghu et al. 2017) keeps accuracy after projecting onto the directions two networks share. e398b is the same observation in language models, measured by description.
- Lexicon alignment. Orthogonal maps between embedding spaces through a shared vocabulary are the cross-lingual alignment method (Mikolov, Le and Sutskever 2013; Smith et al. 2017; Conneau et al. 2018). e398 applies it to two seeds' token embeddings. It fails because the two embedding matrices are not rotations of each other (R2 -0.10), and it translates none of the internal words.
- Massive activations (Sun, Chen, Kolter and Liu 2024, arXiv 2402.17762): a few input-agnostic, very large activations that act as biases. They are a candidate for e395's late variance compressibility (4 own words leave 16% of the variance from step 63000) and for the high-variance, function-light directions the step-4000 false friends align with (untested).
- Hidden-state description length as a measure of computation: Herrmann, Csordás and Schmidhuber (ICML 2025, arXiv 2503.13431) insert a PHi layer, an information bottleneck with a learned predictive prior on the residual stream, and measure the information gained per step. Their code is learned and predictive, and their question is how much new information a computation step carries. Here the code is the network's own weights, and the question is how many of its own words describe a state.
- Not found in searches: a network's own weights used as the description language of its states, with the description length measured functionally across training; and mutual intelligibility of such vocabularies across checkpoints or seeds. The WDD paper itself (Zenodo record 22749478) introduces the dictionary. Sessions 36 and 37 add the functional description-length reading.
