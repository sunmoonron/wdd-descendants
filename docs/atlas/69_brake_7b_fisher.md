# 69. The brake at 7B and the Fisher-graded dictionary (S161)

**Question.** The usable round: does the brake and the two-field dictionary hold at 7B, where the critics will ask, and can the output's own grade (read energy, area 68) choose a position's atoms better than Euclidean energy? Beside the two experiments, the session's outputs are the note on the brake (docs/notes/weak_activations_are_a_brake.md (withdrawn 2026-10-20; its claims, pre-registrations and verdicts stand in docs/REVIEW_POSITIVE_CLAIMS.md and docs/atlas/)) and the library (wdd/).

**Established.**
- At 7B: the sixteen-input damage is 0.85 to 0.87 off the class and sixteen excitatory wires alone cost 59.43 to 284.33 of the ablation damage against 1.06 to 2.70 with sixteen inhibitory wires, and both fields bring sixteen wires from 0.90 to 2.90 to 0.48 to 1.01 at the firings: reading: the brake and the fields hold at 7B: at Qwen2.5-7B in fp32, blocks 14 and 21, every input reproduces the neuron, the sixteen-input simulation's damage comes 0.85 to 0.87 from off the class, sixteen excitatory wires alone cost sixty to two hundred and eighty times the ablation damage over all positions and 1.1 to 2.7 with the sixteen strongest inhibitory wires added, which carry 0.64 to 0.78 of the crowd's off-class opposition; at the firings sixteen wires cost 1.2 to 2.9 of the damage, the signed wires 1.0 to 1.3 and the wires with the weak bus 0.7 to 1.4, the halving statements holding at the mid block and not at the late one. The two fields bring sixteen wires from 0.9 and 2.9 of the damage at the firings (blocks 21 and 14) to 0.5 and 1.0, beating a random subspace of their dimension by 0.3 to 1.2, the strong field alone doing as much as the weak, and the two overlapping by a quarter. The shape is the small models' with the sixteen wires worse at the strongest firings, which the fp32 ledger at 7B already showed (e700): a 7B neuron's sixteen largest inputs carry a smaller share of its input, so the brake and the fields matter more there, not less. The transfer question is answered for the brake; the two-field dictionary transfers with its limit, half the damage left at the mid block. (e713).
- The Fisher-graded dictionary: sixteen atoms chosen in the blended Fisher metric recover 0.68 to 0.97 of the mean-ablation KL against 0.71 to 0.98 for sixteen Euclidean atoms and 0.85 to 0.99 for thirty-two, the pure Fisher metric -0.19 to 0.47: reading: the output's grade does not choose atoms better than energy does: sixteen atoms of rows and fields chosen for a position by pursuit in the blended Fisher metric recover no more of the mean-ablation KL than sixteen chosen in the Euclidean metric (within 0.05 at every block, below it in Pythia-160m), although they leave almost none of the content's read energy where the Euclidean choice leaves a third to a half; the pure Fisher pursuit, fitting the sampled Fisher's range perfectly, recovers less than mean ablation in Pythia and less than half the cost elsewhere, with a reconstruction of twice the content's energy, because the directions the sampled Fisher cannot see are read at finite scale. The field atoms are chosen a sixteenth of the time by either metric, and sixteen Euclidean atoms beat the stream's sixteen principal directions in every model. The lesson is e707's again from the dictionary's side: the Fisher quadratic form grades a given part well (Spearman 0.83 to 0.92) and is not a better objective for choosing parts, since what it misses at first order, the reconstruction's effect through LayerNorm and the later blocks, the Euclidean fit already pays for. The library keeps the blended pursuit as a tool and the Euclidean pursuit as the default. (e714).
