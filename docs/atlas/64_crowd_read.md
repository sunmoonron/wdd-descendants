# 64. The crowd read: summaries, shells and scale (S156)

**Question.** Session 155 left one blocker in the way of the already-clean camp: the crowd, thousands of sub-floor writes that a neuron reads and that the weak activations are from the writers' side. Can it be read without training, as a lexical table, as a function of the sparse edges, as a few guilds, as a low-rank write, or as the residue of growth by generalization, and does it change at 14B?

**Established.**
- No training-free summary of the crowd lets a neuron's sixteen largest inputs reproduce it: across 8 model-blocks the KL at its strong firings relative to ablation is 0.41 to 1.95 with nothing, 0.16 to 1.43 with a per-token table, 0.24 to 1.20 with the crowd of the nearest same-token position in those inputs and 0.25 to 1.21 with sixteen co-activation guilds (random guilds 0.24 to 1.30); the crowd's net sum at the firings is mostly each unit's corpus mean: reading: the crowd can be summarised without training where it is shallow or lexical, and nowhere else: at the sixth block of Pythia-410m, GPT-2 medium and Qwen2.5-0.5B a per-token table of the crowd (held-out text) brings the sixteen-input simulation to 0.16 to 0.46 of the ablation damage and the crowd of the nearest same-token position in those sixteen inputs to 0.24 to 0.44, each for most neurons; at GPT-2 medium's block 12 the table still halves it (0.35); at Pythia-410m's blocks 12 and 18 only the neighbour does, for half the neurons (medians 0.47 to 0.50 against 0.60 to 0.92 for the table and 0.78 to 1.22 for a random same-token position), so there the crowd is partly a function of the sparse part, missing magnitude rather than missing information; at Qwen2.5-0.5B's block 12 and SmolLM2-135M's block 15 nothing training-free halves it (best 0.77 to 1.20); the sixteen largest guilds summed exactly do as well as random groups of the same sizes (write-row guilds beat random in one block of eight) and at group resolution, one edge per guild, they do nothing, so there is no guild structure in the weights or in co-activation that a reader's input exploits; the stream's principal directions do nothing; the crowd's 200 largest contributors have classes containing the neuron's strong rows at 1.3 to 2.5 times chance, not a district; and at the strong firings the crowd's net sum is mostly each upstream unit's corpus mean (0.27 to 0.92), token-specific only at the shallow blocks (0.32 to 0.50) and contextual up to 0.42 at depth (e692).
- The removed weak activations' summed write projected on sixteen principal directions of itself recovers 0.60 to 0.96 of the zeroing cost in 11 models (sixty-four: 0.75 to 0.97), against 0.15 to 0.95 for a per-token table and -0.04 to 0.94 for the per-neuron mean; the sixteen directions are not native rows, not the stream's dominant subspace and not shared across blocks: reading: the weak activations matter through a small collective subspace per block: the removed activations' summed write projected on sixteen principal directions of itself recovers 0.60 to 0.96 of the zeroing cost in every model (sixty-four: 0.75 to 0.97), more than the per-token table (0.15 to 0.95, which halves the cost only in the GELU and GPT-2 models) and the per-neuron mean (-0.04 to 0.94) in every model, and the ladder has the same order in every family, mean, then token table, bigram table and same-token resample together, then four, sixteen and sixty-four directions; the first direction is the mean weak write (cosine 0.96 to 1.00) and the next fifteen are not native rows (largest cosine with a write row 0.25 to 0.42), not the block's dominant write directions from the weights (which recover 0.07 to 0.69), not the stream's principal directions (overlap 0.09 to 0.28, and projecting on them often costs more than zeroing) and not shared between consecutive blocks (overlap 0.11 to 0.41); so the essay's 'ablatable' is true after a sixteen-dimensional correction per block that training-free linear algebra finds on held-out text, and Nanda's 'semantic information' sits in those directions, which neither the neuron basis nor the weight spectrum names (e693).
- A neuron's class, read as nested shells of activation rank, stays adjacent to its core in ledger space for 0.25 to 0.61 of the 1-5 percent shell and 0.05 to 0.31 of the 5-20 shell, the core's root inputs carrying 0.10 to 0.80 and 0.58 to 0.95 of those shells' net drive against 0.17 to 0.81 of the core's; the crowd's net sum opposes the sixteen largest inputs by -0.99 to -0.12 of theirs off the class and -0.10 to 0.62 at the core: reading: the residue of growth by generalization is not in the final model, and the ledger says what the crowd does instead: a neuron's class taken as shells of activation rank is not connected level by level, the 1 to 5 percent shell being adjacent to the top-1% core in ledger space for 0.25 to 0.61 of its positions, the 5 to 20 shell for 0.05 to 0.31 and the 20 to 50 shell for 0.03 to 0.13, with the frontier at the first shell in GPT-2 medium and Qwen2.5-0.5B and at the second in Pythia-410m; each outer shell is nearer the shell before it than the core (0.64 to 0.92), a gradient away from the core rather than a break; there is no depth gradient (the contribution-weighted upstream depth is the same at every shell), a weak lexical seed only (the top 20 firings' one-token share is 0.15 to 0.20 against 0.07 to 0.12 for the top 1 percent), no read row made of steps (the class direction explains 0.01 to 0.15 of the read row and sub-class steps add what random directions add); but the net ledger shows the crowd's job: at the core the sixteen largest inputs carry 0.46 to 0.97 of the net drive and the crowd's net sum lies between -0.10 and +0.62 of theirs, while in the bottom half of positions it opposes them by 0.46 to 0.99 of theirs in six of seven blocks, falling monotonically from the core outward; so the crowd is the suppressor that keeps a neuron off where its drivers are present, the sixteen largest inputs do explain the firing, and a graph pruned to them fails for the silence it cannot explain (e685's simulation over all positions at one to seven times the ablation damage), not for the drive at the firings (e695).
- At 14B the counts hold and the ladder holds: the crowd is 0.96 of the resultant, a neuron's sixteen largest inputs carry 0.01 of its input (its simulation is below bf16 resolution), keeping the strongest quarter of activations costs 0.465 nats of which the removed write's sixteen principal directions recover 0.51 and the per-token table 0.22: reading: the scale point repeats the small models where it can be measured: at block 24 of Qwen2.5-14B the crowd beyond the sixteen largest terms is 0.96 of the resultant along the most prominent row (3300 effective terms, 206 rows over the floor per position), a neuron's input has 31000 effective contributors of which the sixteen largest carry 0.01, and the weak ladder has the gated models' shape, the strongest quarter kept costing 0.47 nats of which the per-neuron mean recovers 0.19, the per-token table 0.22, the removed write's sixteen principal directions 0.51 and sixty-four 0.61, with the stream's directions costing twice the zeroing; what cannot be measured is the ledger's simulation: at 14B in bf16 a neuron driven by every one of its inputs still costs 0.95 of its ablation damage, the split's relative error (1e-2) being the size of one neuron's effect on the output, so the K-input ratios at 14B are void and that test needs fp32 or group ablations (e694).
