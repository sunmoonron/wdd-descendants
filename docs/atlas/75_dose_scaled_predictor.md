# 75. The dose-scaled landmine predictor: the read energy at the zoo's dose scored against e728's measured costs (S167)

**Question.** e728 ranked every write row of Qwen3.5-4B by the sampled-Fisher read energy per unit norm, R_dir, and found that the global bottom-8 rows, all in layer 31, cost more than random rows at the zoo's 0.8 dose, which the session blamed on the dose: 0.8 of each layer's median residual norm, 2.5 at layer 0 and 52.7 at layer 31. Does the second-order KL of the injection, P = 0.5 (0.8 MED_L)^2 R_dir, predict what the measured rows cost: the 24 stage-1 rows, the 40 stage-3 rows and the 8 zoo specs against their controls, with the two landmines and the 43 cards placed in its global ranking?

**Established.**
- the dose-scaled landmine predictor: scaled to the zoo's dose the read energy ranks the measured costs at 0.8 worse than unscaled (Spearman 0.30 against 0.35 over 24 rows, 0.11 against 0.33 over 40, 0.15 against 0.43 over 78), its depth profile is U-shaped while the cost falls with depth (Spearman -0.49), the bottom-8 rows of layer 31 get the smallest P (0.044 nats) and cost 0.73 nats, and the landmines fall to percentiles 0.36 and 0.11: reading: the dose-scaled predictor is dead, and it dies the way the pre-registration's own arithmetic said it would. Scaling the read energy per unit norm by the squared dose makes it a worse predictor of the cost at 0.8 residual norms than the unscaled energy: Spearman 0.30 against 0.35 with the held-out NLL cost over the 24 stage-1 rows, 0.11 against 0.33 over the 40 stage-3 rows, 0.15 against 0.43 over the 78 pooled rows, and the unscaled energy is itself weak (0.43, and 0.41 once the extreme stage-1 groups are removed). The failure is structural: the measured cost at the zoo's dose falls with depth (Spearman -0.49 with the layer over the pooled rows, -0.56 in stage 1) while the squared dose rises 450 times from layer 0 to layer 31 and the median read energy falls 1,500 times, so P is U-shaped in depth (1.0 nats at layer 0, 0.11 at layers 7 to 16, 0.29 at layer 31) and the bottom-8 rows of layer 31, which cost 0.73 nats of held-out NLL, get the smallest P of any group (0.044 against 0.15 for the random rows and 16.7 for the top-8). P overpredicts the early rows by an order of magnitude (10.9 times the measured KL for the top-8) and underpredicts the late rows by one (0.11 for the bottom-8); 6 of 24 rows land within a factor of three. Within a layer P ranks exactly as R_dir, so it leaves the landmines where R_dir left them in their layers (percentiles 0.93 and 0.40) and drops them globally (0.36 and 0.11 against 0.66 and 0.49), and the 43 cards stay ordinary (P percentiles 0.11 to 0.88, median 0.46, none in the top tenth). The pairs say the same: the WDD-chosen row has the larger P in 18 of 20 layer-matched pairs and the larger measured KL in 7, and the zoo's rows beat their controls in 4 of 8 specs by P and 7 of 8 by KL. A second-order expansion in the injected norm does not describe a perturbation of 0.8 residual norms; the quadratic form of the model's own logits predicts removals and small injections (e707, e728's ablation proxy at 0.88) and stops there. A landmine predictor at the zoo's dose has to be measured at the dose (e729).
