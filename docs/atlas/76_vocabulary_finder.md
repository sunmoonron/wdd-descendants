# 76. The native-vocabulary finder: a write row's direct-path vocabulary as a generation-free readout of what the overdriven row says, and as a finder (S168)

**Question.** Session 167 left one angle where WDD could find the viral steers rather than audit them: a write row's native vocabulary, the tokens its direct path promotes through the final norm and the unembedding, read from the weights in one matrix product. If an overdriven direction's theme is mostly that vocabulary, the finder is instant and the inverse problem is a lookup. Is a write row's direct-path vocabulary, the tokens it promotes through the final norm and the unembedding at the zoo's dose, a generation-free readout of what the overdriven row says, and a finder: does it retrieve the zoo's own rows from their regexes (A1), separate the 43 cards from random rows by concentration (A2), track the blind judge's consistency over e728's random rows (A3), read as a theme to a blind judge of token lists (A4) and name the theme the generation judge saw (A5); do the most concentrated rows carry a persona at the zoo's dose (B1 to B3); and does the lookup's best row for each regex produce the theme (C1, C2)?

**Established.**
- the native-vocabulary finder: the direct-path vocabulary retrieves 6 of 9 zoo rows in the top 1 percent of 589,824 candidates and, for rows no one chose, names what the steer says (the list and generation judges agree on 4 of 4 themed and 22 of 23 unthemed rows, 7 of 11 themes matched against 0 of 11 shuffled), but the hand-found cards are not vocabularies (12 of 43 lists readable, random 11 of 43), the most concentrated rows loop (12 of 20 degenerate; loops excluded, 7 of 20 themed against 6 of 20 random), and 5 of 8 retrieved rows loop on the retrieved words, one of eight producing the theme cleanly: reading: the direct-path vocabulary is a generation-free readout of what an overdriven row will say when its effect is a vocabulary, and a selector of word-spammers when used as a finder. From the weights alone, one matrix product per row, it retrieves six of the nine zoo rows inside the top 1 percent of 589,824 (row, sign) candidates by the mass on each card's own regex, including the layer-4 and layer-5 rows the pre-registration expected to fail (the pair's two rows and the layer-5 Galactic row are the misses). For rows no one chose by hand it says what the steer will say: over e728's forty judged rows the token-list judge gives a clear theme to all four rows whose generations had one and to none of the 23 whose generations had none (Spearman 0.79 over the twenty random rows), and a second blind judge matches 7 of its 11 themes to the generation judge's and none of 11 shuffled pairs. What it does not do is find the zoo: the cards' lists read as themed no more often than random rows' (12 of 43 against 11 of 43), their concentration is ordinary (AUC 0.66), and the pair's rows are not retrieved, so most hand-found personas are made downstream of the row, not in its direct path. As a finder it selects loops: the twenty most concentrated rows (N_eff 2 after the single-token spikes are excluded) get consistency 2 from the generation judge on 19 of 20 against 9 of 20 for their same-layer random controls, but 12 of 20 are degenerate (controls 3 of 20) and cost a median 1.26 nats against 0.41, and with the loops excluded they carry a theme on 7 of 20 against 6 of 20; the lookup's best row for each regex hits its words at raw rates that pass the bars (6 of 8 within 0.2 of the card's, 5 of 8 at least half) and loops on them in 5 of 8 cases, with one clean retrieval (the Hugs regex: 10 of 10 non-degenerate answers on theme at 1.14 nats against the card's 2.46) and one partial (Galactic). Seven of ten statements held, three of them (B1, C1, C2) on looping outputs the bars did not exclude; B3, written for that, failed. A finder from the weights needs the dose as a second knob, since a row whose direct path is one or two words loops at 0.8 residual norms, and the themes made downstream need the vocabulary measured at the dose (e731), not the direct path (e730).

## How the results flow

- `74 e728 → e730`: e728's regexes, cards, judged random rows and judge ratings are the test set; the zoo's dose and recipe are the steer; e730 asks whether the row's direct-path vocabulary carries what e728 measured by generation.
- `75 e729 → e730`: e729 killed the predictor from the weights for the cost at the dose; e730 is the predictor from the weights for the content at the dose, and finds it for vocabularies and not for personas.
- `e730 → e731`: the themes the direct path does not carry (31 of 43 cards) need the vocabulary measured at the dose; the finder's rows need the dose as a second knob.
