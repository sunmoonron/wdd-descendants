# 29. How a word leaves: the one-way gate, the takeover, whose motion erodes a word, and what holds the maximum (S89-S91)

**Question.** Entry into the native vocabulary is the crossing of the extreme-value floor by a row's largest projection (areas 23-24). Is exit the same crossing in reverse; if not, is a word pushed out by competitors at the positions it loses, and by whom; is wordhood path-dependent beyond S and breadth; and is any of this specific to the native rows?

**Established.**
- The floor is a gate in one direction: an entry is a crossing (0.37 of the entrants under the floor two intervals before, S +0.13 across the event, usage from 0.60 to 1.40 of the word threshold), an exit is a loss of usage and breadth with the projection kept (0.82 of the leavers above the floor at the first checkpoint out and 0.76 two intervals later, S -0.09, usage 1.29 to 0.67, positions over three quarters of the floor 35 to 26 against 41 for stayers of like S) (e538b).
- An exit is not a displacement: at the median seven positions a leaver loses, the sixteenth-largest projection at the position (the cutoff) does not move (0.88 to 0.88) and the leaver's own projection falls (0.92 to 0.79, rank 6 to 31); at the positions an entrant gains, its own rises (0.76 to 0.95) against a still cutoff; the atoms that take a leaver's positions are at the null in number (10.69 effective takers per lost position against 11.11), class (0.91 non-words), cosine to the leaver (0.01) and neighbour rank (22854 of 53,248), and entrants displace non-words (0.93) and never a leaver; a random dictionary and the synthetic worlds show the same (e539, e540).
- Hysteresis is generic: at S 1.1-1.3 a native word stays at 0.47-0.64 against a matched non-word's entry at 0.02-0.04, but a random dictionary shows a matched difference of 0.32 and the synthetic worlds 0.30-0.72; the one-way gate is not: a random dictionary's leavers are under the floor at exit (0.24 over it) and its entrants under it before entry (0.76), isotropic diffusion 0.37, the pull worlds 0.52-0.56, Pythia's rows 0.77-0.82. The gate is the persistence of the native maximum, which sits at S 1.25-1.44 while the breadth erodes (e539, e540).
- The erosion is mostly the states' drift: at the lost positions the cosine between state and row falls -0.020, -0.015 from the states' motion and -0.006 from the row's; at the gained positions it rises 0.022 (0.011 states, 0.008 row); a leaver's kept positions fall too (-0.006) and the cosine at its old peak falls as much as at the lost positions while its S holds, the maximum relocating above the floor; the floor changes by under one percent (e539b).
- What holds the maximum is the cloud's covariance, not breadth, peak memory or usage clusters: the maximum relocates at the random rate (0.44 the same position one interval later against 0.40), the states at native peaks persist as everywhere (0.926 against 0.925), independent churn from the isotropic atoms' kernel reproduces the random words' maximum and under-predicts the native (1.19 against 1.28), and the lost positions are the least coherent of a word's (0.185 against 0.275); at equal level a native row's projection at a position does not regress (1.01, 1.12, 1.26 from 1.0-1.1, 1.1-1.2, 1.2-1.4) where a random atom's does (0.93, 1.03, 1.10), covariance-matched atoms persist like the rows (1.00, 1.09) and a null with their kernel reproduces the native maximum within 0.03 (e541, e541b).
- Persistence is not stability: the covariance-matched atoms' maxima are the most persistent (0.85 across four thousand steps against 0.83 native and 0.39 random) and their words churn (retention 0.29) below the floor (S 0.90); a native word has the persistent subspace and the individual orientation that sets it at S 1.28 (e541b).

## How the results flow

- `e538 → e538b`. The reversed-time replay's entrants showed no crossing; the exits read directly show the one-way gate.
- `e538b → e539, e540`. The gate taken apart: not displacement, not a local takeover, hysteresis generic, the gate native.
- `e539 → e539b`. Whose motion: the states' drift erodes the marginal positions; the maximum relocates.
- `e539b → e541 → e541b`. What holds the maximum: not breadth, peak memory or usage clusters but the persistence of the native alignments, which is the cloud's covariance; and persistence is not stability, the orientation of sessions 84-86 placing the word above the floor.
