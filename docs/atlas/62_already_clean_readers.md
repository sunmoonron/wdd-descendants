# 62. The already-clean debate I: weak writes, readers, poles and width (S155)

**Question.** Cammarata's essay reads the network from its readers' side: weak activations are ablatable, the negative pole is a near neighbour, a neuron's inputs can be read at variable resolution with little error, and bigger models are cleaner; Nanda, Gao and Heimersheim doubt each. The program's floor, ledger and exact input split turn each into a number with a null.

**Established.**
- A native write injected after the middle block costs the output no more than a random direction of the same norm at any amplitude from a quarter of the floor to four floors (own write scaled 0.82 to 1.36 of random at two floors, a foreign row 0.99 to 1.02, the rotated row 0.99 to 1.04 at most) in 5 models: reading: the floor is an identification bound, not a reader boundary, and native directions are not privileged: a foreign native row and its rotation cost what a random direction of the same norm costs at every amplitude from a quarter of a floor to four floors in every model, with no change of regime at one floor; only the position's own largest write differs, by model rather than by the floor (1.3 to 1.7 times a random direction below the floor in GPT-2 small, Pythia-410m, SmolLM2-135M and Qwen2.5-0.5B, 0.7 at four floors in Pythia-160m); the next block contracts every injected direction alike (area 05); so 'below the floor' says what a projection can identify, not what the readers ignore, and a reader's sensitivity to a direction is about the position's content, not its provenance (e680).
- Removing all but the strongest quarter of each neuron's activations costs 0.24 to 8.68 nats of next-token KL in 11 models (mean replacement 0.07 to 2.31; below a tenth of the maximum 0.48 to 14.09), the cost sitting in the dial tertile (0.10 to 1.85) more than the detector tertile (0.05 to 2.71) and the flips mostly near-ties: reading: the essay's 'almost all weak activations can be removed for almost no cost' fails under zeroing: keeping the strongest quarter costs 0.2 to 8.7 nats, more at depth and in the larger models; in the GELU and GPT-2 models most of that cost is the removed activations' mean, which per-neuron mean replacement recovers (57 to 94 percent; 0.07 to 0.7 nats remain), in the gated models a minority of it (22 to 66 percent, none in SmolLM2-135M), whose signed SwiGLU activations have a less useful mean; what remains is token-specific, sitting in the dial tertile and the second half of the blocks of the gated models (ten to twenty times the detector tertile) and in the detector tertile of GPT-2 and Pythia, and the flips it causes are mostly near-ties; so weak activations matter as a bias the network depends on more than as a signal, e678's double negative generalised, and 'ablatable' is true only of their variation around that mean (e681).
- A mid-network neuron's input is a crowd: 1874 to 15033 effective upstream contributors at its strongest firings, the sixteen largest carrying 0.02 to 0.05, the over-floor writes 0.02 to 0.19 of the energy; driven by its sixteen largest inputs the neuron does 0.43 to 2.11 of its ablation damage, and halving the damage takes 16 inputs at best and more than 1024 in 5 of 12 model-blocks: reading: a mid-network neuron's input is a crowd of thousands, its sixteen largest inputs carry 2 to 5 percent of it, and the writes that clear the floor at the position carry 2 to 19 percent of the input energy, so the readers read mostly below the floor; driving the neuron by its sixteen largest inputs costs half to twice the damage of removing it (0.43 to 2.11 of it), and at blocks 12 and 18 of Pythia-410m and Qwen2.5-0.5B and block 12 of GPT-2 medium even its 1024 largest cannot halve that damage, because what they leave out is not a constant the mean could stand in for but position-specific (e685b); the pruned neuron attribution graph the essay expects to carry 'almost no error' carries, without an error term for the crowd, half to twice the error of deleting the node, which is the attribution-graph literature's error node made concrete at the neuron level, and Nanda's expectation; e685b adds the crowd's mean back as that error term and finds it a minority of the crowd's effect (e685).
- A neuron's negative pole is nearer its positive pole than a random direction's tails are to each other for 0.91 to 0.97 of neurons in the gated models and 0.00 to 0.48 in the GELU models at the middle block (read axes projected out, null the two tails of a rotated read row): reading: the gate picture holds for SwiGLU and not for GELU: in SmolLM2, OLMo and Qwen2.5 the two pole classes' mean states, with the read axes projected out, are nearer than the two tails of any rotated read row for 0.91 to 0.97 of neurons at the middle block, and their next tokens overlap more than the null's for a fifth to two fifths; in Pythia and GPT-2 the near share is 0.0 to 0.5 and the token overlap sits at the null; the product of a gate and a value puts a related input of the opposite sign on the negative pole by construction, while a GELU neuron's suppressed side is whatever its read direction anti-selects, so 'the negative pole is the nearest neighbour' is a fact about the nonlinearity, not about neurons (e686).
- From 70M to 7B parameters a position's state has 14 to 226 rows over the floor and a crowd carrying 0.91 to 0.97 of the resultant along its most prominent row (399 to 6636 effective terms); within families the over-floor count scales with width at Spearman 0.80 to 1.00 and the crowd share at 0.80 to 1.00: reading: with width the readable part and the crowd grow together: rows over the floor number 14 to 226 per position (one to nine percent of the dimension) across seventeen models of four families and rise with width within each family (Spearman 0.8 to 1.0), but the resultant along the most prominent row is 91 to 97 percent crowd at every size, the effective number of its terms rises from 400 to 6600, and the three largest writes' share of the state falls from 0.29 to 0.09 in the GPT-2, Pythia and SmolLM2 families while Qwen2.5 keeps a third of it from 0.5B to 7B; Heimersheim's objection to the squeeze experiments is answered in his favour at the level of writes: the crowd does not shrink with dimension, and a layer crushed to ten neurons says nothing about it (e689).
- What a neuron's readers do with it correlates with its activation label at Spearman -0.02 to 0.04 over pairs (weight-only reader vectors 0.58 to 0.87), the activation neighbour being among the ten reader neighbours for 0.03 to 0.11 (chance about 0.01): reading: what a neuron's readers do with it is not predicted by its activation label: over pairs of neurons the token bags of their top activations correlate with their causal downstream responses at Spearman 0.00 to 0.04, their mean input states little better, while the weight-level reader vector predicts the causal response at 0.6 to 0.9 and the direct logit image at 0.05 to 0.3; a neuron's nearest neighbour by tokens is among its ten nearest by readers for 3 to 11 percent (chance 1 percent); so 'become another reader' and 'label the neuron' are different projects, and the readers' side is the one the weights already encode (e691).
