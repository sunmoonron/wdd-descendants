# 48. The statistics, the missing controls and the per-word test (S117)

**Question.** Three things the held-out test (area 47) left open: what the intervals say about the writers against the naive set and about the incremental gain; whether silencing works because the neurons are selective or merely active; and whether the dictionary's word structure localises where an unlearning failed.

**Established.**
- With cluster-bootstrap intervals over jobs the writers' AUC is 0.85 [0.76, 0.93] on the held-out domains, 0.91 [0.81, 0.98] on the held-out methods and 0.93 [0.54, 1.00] on the architecture; the difference-selected set's gap over the writers is +0.05 [+0.02, +0.10] on the domains, +0.05 [-0.03, +0.15] on the methods and -0.13 [-0.48, +0.04] on the architecture (e618a).
- The incremental gain of the writers over the forget rise, the output KL and the drift is +0.31 [+0.17, +0.47] of leave-one-domain-out accuracy (0.53 to 0.84), the difference-selected set's +0.33 [+0.23, +0.50], and the writers minus the difference-selected set -0.02 [-0.16, +0.03]; for the recovery magnitude the loss-side model explains R squared 0.04 out of domain, the writers alone 0.37, the difference-selected set alone 0.41 (e618a).
- The normalised score (the forget writers' ratio over the retain writers') tunes to a cut of 0.781 on the training conditions, next to the raw cut, and scores 0.60 on the held-out domains (majority 0.55, AUC 0.84): the calibration failure is not a scale problem (e618a).
