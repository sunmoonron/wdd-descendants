# 74. The zoo through WDD: the neuron zoo's steers as rows at their own amplitude, the bridge to sparsified MLPs, and the cost of the weak activations with its controls (S166)

**Question.** The neuron zoo of Qwen3.5-4B finds persona neurons by inference on a topic and steers them at 0.8 residual norms; the sparsified-MLP paper finds that what transcoders learn is mostly single gated neurons plus a linear map; and the record's best-generalising claim, the cost of the weak activations, had never been run with the controls that could kill it. What does WDD's view of a write row as a dictionary atom with its own natural amplitude and its own read energy say about each?

**Established.**
- The bridge to sparsified MLPs: on Qwen3-4B the 64 largest neurons per token leave 0.05 nats, the closed-form rank-16 field map recovers 0.10 to 0.33 of it, a trained rank-16 map 0.07 to 0.21, a trained full map 0.09 to 0.22 and a random rank-16 map -0.00 to 0.00: reading: the premise was wrong by a factor of ten and the comparison still came out in one order. On Qwen3-4B keeping the 64 largest of 9,728 neurons per token leaves 0.050 and 0.047 nats of KL at blocks 24 and 30, not the half nat pre-registered, against 0.151 and 0.184 for replacing the whole block by its mean; a late block of this model is cheap, and its 64 largest neurons per token carry two thirds of what it does. Of the remainder the closed-form rank-16 map of the weak write from the normalised block input recovers 0.332 (0.31 to 0.35) at block 24 and 0.105 (0.08 to 0.13) at block 30, exactly the ceiling of its own output subspace (the true weak write projected on it recovers 0.337 and 0.131), against 0.004 and -0.002 for the same map on a random rank-16 subspace, and above both trained maps at 200 steps: the trained rank-16 map 0.210 and 0.072, the trained d-by-d map on the pruned output 0.216 and 0.090. The variance left unexplained orders the maps the same way (top-64 alone 0.56 and 0.48, with the field map 0.37 and 0.29, with the trained full map 0.46 and 0.37), so the KL and FVU orderings do not differ as predicted. Two of six statements held (random under half of the field map at both blocks; the trained rank-16 map within 0.1 of the closed-form one at block 30 only). The honest limits: the trained maps were still improving when training stopped (the rank-16 loss fell from 0.047 to 0.028 over the 200 steps), so their deficit is a budget statement, not a ceiling; the weak write is 0.48 and 0.38 of the MLP output's energy on held-out text at these blocks, and a rank-16 linear map of it from the block's input explains 0.65 of its variance at block 24 in the fit, so what the map puts back is the linear, low-rank part of the dropped neurons' write, which is most of what a sparsified MLP loses by KL at these two blocks (e726).
- The weak-activation cost with its controls: the weakest three quarters cost 0.89 to 5.19 nats across 8 models against 4.09 to 12.35 for a random three quarters and 4.65 to 15.20 for the mass-matched strongest slice, and per unit of energy removed the three slices cost 5.63 to 20.30, 5.45 to 16.73 and 5.70 to 18.64 nats: reading: the controls that could have killed the cost of the weak activations did not kill it, but they re-size it. A count-matched random three quarters costs 4.09 to 12.35 nats against 0.89 to 3.37 for the weakest three quarters of the six pretrained models, and the mass-matched strongest slice 4.65 to 15.20, so the weakest three quarters are the cheapest three quarters by count, and the cost tracks the energy removed: per unit of energy the three slices cost 5.6 to 20.3, 5.5 to 16.7 and 5.7 to 18.6 nats across the eight runs, the weak slice 0.73 to 2.27 times the random slice (OLMo-1B 2.1 and TinyStories-33M on wikitext 2.3 at the top, Pythia-160m 0.73 at the bottom). The claim that the weak activations matter beyond the energy they carry has no support in these eight models at this measurement; what survives is the plain statement that they carry 5 to 37 percent of the activation energy and cost 0.89 to 5.19 nats when removed, with a cost per unit of energy within a factor of about two of any other slice. The tokens whose top prediction flips under the weak ablation have a lower top-two share than the run of tokens (0.63 to 0.67 against 0.64 to 0.73), the predicted direction with a gap of 0.01 to 0.09. The TinyStories prediction failed twice: measured on wikitext the two models are out of their distribution (base loss 10 to 11 nats), and measured on their own text (base loss 1.5 to 1.6) their weakest three quarters cost 1.27 and 4.48 nats, the random three quarters 4.48 and 7.38 and the strongest slice 4.24 and 7.82, the pattern of the pretrained models at the same cost per unit of energy (7.4 and 12.0 against 6.0 and 9.8 for the random slice). A children's-story model with a tiny vocabulary keeps its weak activations as costly as GPT-2 does, so the cost is not a product of broad pretraining (e727).
- The zoo through WDD: the zoo's dose is 5 to 86 times the row's own q999 activation, the zoo rows sit at R_dir percentiles 0.22 to 0.86, their personas appear at 0.8 (0.10 to 1.00 hit rate) and not at 3 x q999 (0.00 to 0.10), the top-R_dir rows cost +1.52 nats at 0.8 against +0.40 for random rows, and before a blind judge WDD-chosen rows reach consistency 2 on 2 of 10 at 3 x q999 and each layer's top-R_dir row on 0 of 10 at 0.8: reading: the zoo and WDD rank rows by different things, and the run says which is which. By the sampled-Fisher read energy of all 294,912 write rows of Qwen3.5-4B, measured from the model's own logits on natural text, the zoo's 43 cards are ordinary rows: global R_dir percentiles 0.08 to 0.89 (median 0.57, none in the top tenth; within their layers 0.07 to 0.98), R_nat percentiles 0.01 to 1.00; the two known landmines sit at 0.66 and 0.49. What the zoo steers is a direction at a dose far outside the row's own range: 0.8 residual norms is 5 to 119 times the row's 99.9th-percentile activation over the cards (median 28; 5 to 86 over the nine validated rows, Meowscow the one below 10). The personas appear between 0.4 and 0.8 residual norms (six of seven at hit rate 0.7 or more at 0.8; the evil pair 0.3 at 0.8 and 0.5 at 1.0, under the pre-registered 0.6) and nowhere at 1 or 3 times the row's own amplitude (hit rate at most 0.1; at 10 times Meowscow alone reaches 0.6; at 30 times, which is 0.3 to 0.6 residual norms, six of seven), while random rows of the same layer and sign hit at most 0.1 at every dose. The zoo's rows are the expensive directions, not the cheap ones: 0.37 to 3.77 nats of KL on the model's own answers at 0.8 (median 1.55) against 0.31 to 1.09 (median 0.58) for the random rows. A blind judge (97 shuffled sets of six answers, key withheld) was calibrated by the zoo itself, giving seven of eight steers consistency 2 (Meowscow 0), none of their random controls and not the base model; it then gave a nameable theme to 4 of 20 random rows at the zoo's dose (bookbinding, finger-to-nose tests, cold refusals, an emotional loop) and a partial one to 7 more, so an overdriven direction is a persona about one time in five, under the pre-registered bar of 6 in 20. WDD's own choices carry no persona: the top-R_nat row of each layer is a massive-activation row (q999 4.7 to 187 against a typical 0.4), silent at the zoo's 0.8 (median KL 0.16) and destructive at 3 times its own amplitude (median 3.9 nats, 7 of 10 degenerate before the judge, 2 of 10 with a theme), where random rows at 3 times their amplitude do nothing (KL at most 0.03, 0 of 10 changed); the readers' favourite row of each middle layer at 0.8 gets 0 of 10 and costs less than a random row of its layer (median 0.57 against 0.73 nats). The global top-8 rows by R_dir per unit norm (layers 0 to 2) cost +1.52 nats of held-out NLL at 0.8 against +0.40 for random rows, but the bottom-8, all in layer 31, cost +0.73, because the zoo's dose is in units of each layer's residual norm (2.5 at layer 0, 52.7 at layer 31) and R_dir per unit norm is not the predicted cost at that dose; the dose-scaled predictor was not pre-registered. The ablation proxy is sound (Spearman 0.88 over 24 rows). Three of twelve statements held (e728).
