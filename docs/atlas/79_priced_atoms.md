# 79. Priced atoms as the control group: the native rows of an MLP layer of Pythia-410m against a public TopK SAE of the same MLP output, both priced by one sampled-Fisher gradient and costed by removal, on a 2017 card (S171)

**Question.** The program's standing claim after the persona sessions is that the model's own write rows, priced by what the output reads from them, are the control group every learned dictionary has to beat. On one MLP layer of Pythia-410m, with EleutherAI's 65,536-feature TopK SAE trained on exactly the vector the native rows write (the MLP output), both dictionaries priced by the same sampled-Fisher gradient: does the price predict the cost of removing an atom for the rows (P1) and for the features (P2), is the price more than the activation energy (P3), at matched count (C1) and matched energy (C2) do the SAE's most-read atoms cost more to remove than the native rows', does the SAE lose more by existing than the native basis loses by losing its 256 most-read rows (C3), and do the prices separate rows (C4) and features (C5) from random ones? Layer 12 primary, layers 6 and 18 replicated with no change.

**Established.**
- priced atoms on a 2017 card: on three layers of Pythia-410m the sampled-Fisher price predicts what an atom costs to remove for the native rows and the learned features alike (Spearman 0.88 to 0.95 over 40 of each), but it is nearly the activation energy (0.94 to 0.97 over all 4,096 rows); by count the SAE's most-read atoms cost less to remove than the native rows' at every layer for k of 64 and above (layer 12, k 64: 0.0052 against 0.0074 nats), per unit of energy removed the native rows cost more at layers 6 and 18 and the same at 12; the SAE's reconstruction costs 0.017 to 0.023 nats, more than the 256 most-read rows at layer 12 and less at 6 and 18; random rows cost 11 to 39 percent of the most-read 256 and random features nothing; and the two dictionaries agree at the top, the SAE's second most-read feature at layer 12 being a native row by context and vocabulary and both top lists at layer 6 being the paragraph-break rows: reading: the control group runs on a 2017 card, three layers in a quarter of an hour, and it says three things. The price works for both dictionaries: over 40 native rows and 40 SAE features removed one at a time, R_abl predicts the KL at Spearman 0.89 and 0.89 at layer 12 (0.95 and 0.94 at layer 6, 0.88 and 0.87 at layer 18). It is, for removal, almost entirely the loudness: over all 4,096 rows R_abl and the activation energy rank at 0.97, 0.94 and 0.97, and energy alone predicts the single-row cost as well (0.92, 0.95, 0.84), so the Fisher term that distinguishes the read energy from the activation variance barely varies across the rows of a layer; what the read energy added on Qwen3.5-4B was for steering at a dose, where the direction matters, and e733 does not touch that. And the learned dictionary does not concentrate what the model reads: at matched count its most-read atoms cost less to remove than the native rows' at every layer for k of 64 and above (layer 12: 0.0052 against 0.0074 at k 64, 0.0089 against 0.0145 at k 256, 0.0156 against 0.0335 at k 1024), and per unit of energy removed the native rows cost the same at layer 12 (0.0028 against 0.0030 nats per unit) and 1.3 to 2.3 times more at layers 18 and 6, because the SAE's largest features are high-energy directions the output reads little: at layer 6 its sixteen most-read features hold 86 of its 108 energy units and all fire on the paragraph break, as do the sixteen most-read native rows (33 of 88 units), and removing either set costs 0.031 nats. The SAE's existence costs 0.020, 0.017 and 0.023 nats at the three layers, more than losing the 256 most-read native rows at layer 12 (0.0145) and less at 6 and 18 (0.056, 0.032). The prices separate: random rows cost 11 and 22 percent of the most-read 256 at layers 6 and 18 and 39 percent at layer 12, where the rows are more uniform, and the least-read 256 cost 8, 10 and 36 percent; random TopK features cost nothing, which makes the random-feature control hollow and the matched-energy and bottom-k comparisons the ones to read. The three questions read off both dictionaries: at layer 18 the two most-read native rows after the bracket row are a British-against-American pair (one promotes pound signs, Yorkshire, Scotland, Edinburgh, BBC and Labour and suppresses Minnesota, Color and favorable; the other the reverse), at layer 12 a row promotes musical, songs, film and soundtrack and fires inside film and game titles, and the SAE's second most-read feature at layer 12 fires in the same contexts as native row 3736 and promotes the same tokens, so the learned dictionary re-found a native row. Five of eight statements held at the primary layer and four of eight at each replication; the ones that failed say that the read energy is loudness for removal and that a learned dictionary's top is cheaper than the native basis's top, which is the control group's result (e733).
