# 01. Method: solvers, the dictionary as an object, invariance, calibration

**Question.** What computation is WDD, which decoder serves which purpose, and how well are coefficients estimated?
**Established.** WDD is classical sparse approximation over a fixed, provenance-labelled dictionary, exactly invariant under the gated-MLP neuron gauge (e07); it reproduces the paper within 0.01 (e00) and beats its rotation at every depth of every trained model, and PCA where tested, but not with random-init states or atoms (e01, e43, e80). The advantage over the rotation is an extreme-value property of the first ~8 atoms, not second-order frame alignment (e03, e09). OMP reconstructs best and identifies worst: the dual-frame projection and LASSO add 11-21 points in all five models (e04, e45, e93); identification is limited by competition, not budget (e10, e51, e122); and function follows reconstruction, not attribution (e105). Coefficient errors are bias, largely removed by a two-parameter calibration that transfers across corpora but mostly encodes each neuron's typical magnitude (e12, e95, e52).
**Start here:** e01, e04, e09, e10, e105 · **Sessions:** S1, S2-3 · **Scripts:** `scripts/eNN_*.py` per id; shared solvers in `scripts/wdd_common.py`

## How the results flow

- `e00 → e01 → e80`: the gate matches the paper; with depth recall decays but the lead over rotation holds; random-init states or dictionaries sit at the rotation floor. Correction: the lead already seen at Pythia step 1000 (FVU 0.49 vs 0.59) is mostly a per-block second-order accent (e443: 0.82 of the loss-recovered gap); words appear between steps 4000 and 16000.
- `e03 → e09 → e118 → e388`: the best-atom correlation, not frame alignment, explains the gap (e03); it peaks near k=8 and is exhausted in the residual (e09); e118 traces the competitor level to the state covariance; e388 finds the loss advantage also lives at small k.
- `e04 → e45 → e93 → e154`: OMP identifies worst (e04); the dual one-shot buys identification with budget (e45); hard thresholding repeats the trade-off (e93); reweighting halves LASSO's shrinkage (e154).
- `e10 → e51 → e122 → e74`: competition limits identification (e10); a found write is picked first or second (e51); a second pass finds no new true writes (e122); the residual is noise-like (e74).
- `e03 → e61 → e63`: coherence bounds are vacuous (e03); the linear null-space fraction is trivially ~97% (e61); the sparse statement (ERC, conditioning) finds the true top-3 support unique (e63).
- `e12 → e48 → e95 → e52`: the error is bias removable by calibration (e12), not noise (e48); the calibration transfers across corpora (e95) but mostly predicts each neuron's typical magnitude (e52).
- `e16 → e116 → e145`: late atoms are mostly explained by earlier ones (e16) and their nearest partners are earlier MLP atoms (e116, whose own-span column was trivial); e145 tests head subspaces against a random control.
