# 58. What the calendar answer reads at the name token (S132)

**Question.** Rotating the calendar ring's writers turned the ring by about a class step and the answers mostly did not follow (e639, area 57). Which part of the name token's state does the next-day or next-month answer read, until which depth, and is the ring's angle part of it?

**Established.**
- The answer reads the name token through its class-mean subspace: replacing only the k-1 directions spanned by the centred class means transfers the source's answer as well as replacing the whole state, within 0.11 in agreement, at every swept depth in all 22 model-calendar pairs of 11 models, and replacing everything outside that subspace transfers at most +0.07 beyond the baseline agreement (e640).
- The name token stops setting the answer before the ring's best block in 11 of 22 pairs (both calendars of GPT-2 small, GPT-2 medium, GPT-2 large, Pythia-410m, Pythia-1.4b, and SmolLM2-135M months): the depth at which the ring is cleanest comes after its token has stopped feeding the answer, so at that depth the ring is read by nothing at that position (e640).
- Inside the subspace, the ring plane patched from the adjacent class transfers less than the mean of two random planes in 22 of 22 pairs (mean follow 0.08 against 0.20 at the depth where the full patch is followed most), while the rest of the subspace transfers most of it; adjacent classes sit close on a ring by construction, so this alone does not decide whether the ring is read as an angle (e640).
- Setting the ring plane to the ring's radius at 24 angles moves 0.38 to 0.66 of the answers at the earlier swept depth, but half of the moved answers land on the class after the angle's class in only 4 of the 32 sweeps that move 0.2 or more, and at the best block of the three GPT-2s and both Pythias nothing moves (e640).
- Pushed toward every other class by the same distance, the ring plane moves the answer to the class after the target in 0.31 of trials at two ring radii on average over 22 pairs, the random planes inside the class-mean subspace in 0.23 and the next plane in 0.22; the ring's advantage over the random planes is -0.14 to +0.27 by pair; the ring plane's following goes from 0.22 at one radius to 0.32 at four, the random planes' to 0.29 (e642).
- Reading: per unit push the ring plane is the most-read plane of the class-mean subspace for the months (its advantage over random planes +0.00 to +0.24, positive in 10 of 11) but not reliably for the days (-0.14 to +0.27, positive in 6 of 11), where random planes in a six-dimensional subspace overlap the ring plane by chance; random planes and the next plane steer the answer too, and the ring plane's following stops growing between two and four radii in 16 of 22 pairs; so the readout is a class code in which the ring is the most-read plane for the months, not an angle read alone, and e640's adjacent-class patch understated it because neighbouring classes sit close on the ring.

**Session 135 (the subspace floor).** Against isotropic random planes of the same dimension every calendar class-mean subspace has a write row lying deep inside it (22 of 22 pairs above the 95th percentile; largest squared projection 0.10 to 0.62 against null 95th percentiles of 0.02 to 0.08); against planes drawn from the states' covariance it still does in 18 of 22, the exceptions being GPT-2 medium months, Pythia-410m days, Pythia-410m months, Pythia-1.4b months; the ring plane alone clears the covariance null in 11 of 22. With Gemini 4's guardrails the result stands: against a spectrum-matched null (the subspace rotated inside bands of 16 neighbouring principal components, which keeps its variance profile) the class-mean subspace still has a write row deeper inside it than 95 percent of the rotations in 20 of 22 pairs (not in Pythia-410m days, Pythia-410m months), and in 20 of 22 with block 0's token detectors excluded; the ring plane alone in 11 with block 0 and 11 without. Reading: the calendar's class-mean subspace is anchored in the write rows beyond its place in the high-variance part of the state, in every model but Pythia-410m and without block 0, so the subspace e640 found the answer reading is one the parameters write rather than a slice of the state's variance; the exception is a parallel-residual Pythia, as for phase coding, though Pythia-1.4b passes, and the ring plane by itself is anchored in only half of the pairs.

**Session 136 (what the floor sorts).** Autoencoder blocks built as Engels et al. did pass the spectrum-matched floor almost always (0.93 to 1.00), so the floor cannot sort them; arbitrary noun class subspaces pass it in 0.21 of draws against the calendar's 0.91. Reading: the calendar's anchoring is specific in most models: arbitrary noun classes rarely pass the floor that the calendar's class-mean subspace passes in 20 of 22 pairs, except in OPT-125m, OPT-1.3b, OLMo-1B, where noun classes pass in 0.40 to 1.00 of draws and the floor does not single the calendar out; elsewhere e645b's reading stands; autoencoder blocks pass almost always because their features are built near rows (e495), which makes the floor a test for subspaces read from states, not for subspaces built from a dictionary.

## How the results flow

- `e645b → e647, e649`: the floor turned on autoencoder blocks, and on arbitrary noun classes as the control that decides whether the calendar is special.
- `e640 → e645 → e645b`: the subspace the answer reads, asked whether the parameters write it, under a null that keeps its variance profile.
- `e639 → e640 → e642`: the rotation moved the ring but not the answers; the patching located what the answer reads and when it stops reading; the matched pushes asked whether the ring's angle is part of it.
