# 05. Cancellation, erasure and the learned contraction

**Question.** When a write fades from the residual stream, what removes it, by what mechanism, and when does training build that mechanism?
**Established.** Physical erasure of a dominant write is rare (1-6% of tokens) and comes from a crowd of small MLP writes, not from attention, partner writes or dedicated neurons (e08, e87, e185). The erasure matrix shows within-block cancellation in GPT-2 and a next-block chain in Qwen, OLMo and Pythia, but the chain is not a wire: ablating the next block never restores survival, and later blocks take over (e173, e183, e186). It is a generic learned contraction: every trained block answers a perturbation along any direction with a linear gain of -0.16 to -0.42, derived as normalisation gain times the MLP Jacobian trace, zero at initialisation and born in Pythia as the warmup ends (e194, e210, e217). Provenance persists because other components keep writing the same directions, while a write's own causal half-life is 1.5-2 blocks (e193, e208). Session 4 narrowed the law (direction-independent within 0.2, product law over three blocks, energy lost only in GPT-2; e214, e215, e219), and e221 (area 06) reframes the contraction as scattering of a write's direction, not loss of its energy.
**Start here:** e08, e173, e183, e194, e210 · **Sessions:** S1, S2-3, S4 · **Scripts:** `scripts/e08_cancellation.py`, `scripts/e173_erasure_matrix.py`, `scripts/e194_linear_response.py`, `scripts/e210_jacobian_trace.py`; the rest are `scripts/<id>_*.py`

## How the results flow

- `e06 → e08 → e87 → e141 → e185`: exact accounting splits reinforcing (GPT-2, SmolLM2) from cancelling models; e08 finds raw erasure rare and GPT-2's apparent erasure mostly mean subtraction; e87, e141 and e185 rule out pairwise, single-neuron and dedicated-neuron cancellation.
- `e173 → e183 → e186 → e187`: the erasure matrix suggested wired erasers; ablation never restores survival (e183), later blocks take over (e186), and only GPT-2's within-block cancellation has a weight signature (e187). The wired eraser is killed.
- `e185 → e191 → e192 → e194`: a proportional crowd (e191) that is not the bias (e192) suggested a feedback gain; e194 found the same gain along random directions, so e173's next-block entries (-0.33 to -0.36) are the generic contraction, and GPT-2's next-block +0.16 is about +0.5 of re-writing on a -0.34 contraction.
- `e194 → e207 → e209 → e210 → e213 → e216`: learned and MLP-borne (e207, whose LN-versus-weights split was retracted), anti-aligned rows in GELU models (e209), derived from weights (e210); its normalisation factor predicts the 1/norm law (e213), which freezing the scale confirms (e216).
- `e188 → e193 → e208 → e212`: raw survival mixes persistence with state growth, so e193 re-measures on centred states; persistence beats passive decay because the direction is written anyway (e208); late-born writes keep more (e212).
- `e214 → e220`, `e194 → e215 → e221`: session 4 narrowed the contraction (0.2 band, GPT-2 direction-specific, no spectral law, product law for three blocks); e221 and e222 (area 06) show the energy is conserved while the direction scatters: decorrelation, not damping.
- `e173 → e209 → e217`: Pythia's block-5 canceller is already -0.51 at step 1000 (e173), when the generic gain is only ~-0.03; the read/write anti-alignment forms between steps 512 and 2000 and leads the gain: specific cancellation before generic contraction.
- `e173 → e197`, `e173 → e199`, `e06 → e219`: the damping is readable as an opposite-sign echo in the chain models; canceller blocks are not the unreadable ones; energy loss inside blocks is GPT-2's alone.
