# 02. When a write is readable: the prominence law, neuron identifiability, certificates

**Question.** When can a write be read from the residual state, which neurons are readable, and how can a reading be certified without the ledger?
**Established.** A dominant MLP write is identified when its prominence (projection on the centred state over its norm) beats a competitor level: recall is a step function of prominence in all five models (e56), moving only the write's own projection moves OMP identification causally (e60), and a per-level covariance-matched 64th-order threshold predicts identification at every depth with no fitting, held out, and for the dual decoder (e127, e129, e178). Per the audit, the one-shot form is definitional and the match to the isotropic floor sqrt(2 ln m/d) was a coincidence (e57, e62, e118). Identifiability is a stable per-neuron trait (ICC 0.95-0.97; e05, e24), mostly the neuron's typical prominence (e130), and unrelated to single-neuron causal importance (e59, e59b); never-read neurons are neither a magnitude class (e30) nor a static geometric class (e206), and outside GPT-2 their misses are swamping, not aliasing (e41, e181). Spurious atoms get two certificates, stability selection for OMP (e195) and a per-atom null z-score for the dual reading (e200), whose threshold is empirical, not a parameter-free extreme value (e204, e205).
**Start here:** e05, e56, e60, e127, e200 · **Sessions:** S1-S3 · **Scripts:** `scripts/e05_neuron_observability.py`, `scripts/e56_prominence_curve.py`, `scripts/e60_intervene.py`, `scripts/e127_corrected_law.py`, `scripts/e200_null_certificate.py`

## How the results flow

- `e56 → e57 → e58 → e62 → e118 → e127 → e129`: the step curve seemed to sit at the isotropic floor (e57, e58); the audit found the one-shot form definitional and the floor match a k=1 vs k=64 coincidence (e62); the competitor level is mostly state covariance (e118), so a per-level covariance-matched threshold removes the bias (e127) and holds out of sample (e129).
- `e56 → e60`: moving only the write's own projection moves OMP identification both ways in 5/5, so prominence is causal, not a correlate.
- `e127 → e178 → e200 → e204 → e205`: the law carries to the dual decoder (e178); the per-atom z-score becomes the statistic, with dominant recall 0.00-0.39 below z=2 and 0.99-1.00 above 6 (e200); its k=64 match to the null's 64th extreme (e204) fails across k (e205).
- `e05 → e24 → e172`, `e05 → e130`, `e05 → e59 → e59b`: a stable, corpus-independent neuron trait, mostly an amplitude phenotype, unrelated to ablation cost.
- `e30 → e46 → e41 → e181 → e206`: never-neurons are not just small writes (e30); solver-limited in SmolLM2, state-limited in GPT-2 and Qwen (e46); misses aliased mainly in GPT-2 (e41 with the audit null, e181), swamped elsewhere; not a static geometric class (e206).
- `e189 → e195`, `e189 → e196 → e202 → e200`: known-null atoms are picked at the clean rate and OMP supports are unstable, so stability selection certifies OMP; the dual's spurious atoms are noise-selected workhorses, so dropping them fails and a z-score works; e180's self-certificate uses neither.
- `e155 → e161`: the confidence link is model-specific; in GPT-2 and SmolLM2 uncertain tokens have smaller states dominated by the block-0 lookup write. `e23`, `e42`: embedding atoms are not context copies; non-MLP atoms and twins barely affect recall.
