# 16. The vision round (S45) and its causal follow-ups (S46)

**Question.** If WDD had existed for ten years, which of the capabilities it would have enabled survive the cheapest decisive test of the assumption each one rests on?
**Established.** The native vocabulary is written, not spoken: MLP write rows frozen at their random initialisation are barely used as words (0.69-0.75 unexplained against 0.74 for their rotation), trained writer rows are, and when some writers are frozen the description moves to those that train, both in grokking and in a small sequence model (e444, e444b, e449). Native words carry meaning for concrete concepts: one MLP write row serves as a language-independent concept word for 7 of 24 nouns in SmolLM2, 15 in Qwen-0.5B and 22 of 24 in Qwen-7B (none with rotated words), sentences are described by the same words in four languages, removing a concept word costs more than a matched other word, and in Qwen-0.5B these words are largely multilingual neurons (e448, e448b-e448e); token-level proxies cannot show this, since rotated words score highest on them (e446). Generalising grokking networks become sparse in their own words while memorising ones get only the second-order part, and the word-level part led test accuracy by 500-750 steps in two of four generalising runs (e444). Three imagined capabilities failed their first test: word-for-word translation between models (0.06-0.13 of function against 0.85-0.91 for a dense map, e445), a Euclidean self-description training term (it moves variance, not function, e447b), and a low-rate native codec (it beats PCA only at 256-512 bits, e450). The backcast, the status of each imagined capability and a six-step roadmap are in [VISION.md](../VISION.md). Session 46 tested these results causally. Swapped at every block up to the middle at the noun, one concept word (an MLP write row) redirects a model's translations to the swapped-in noun and moves its category answers, while random directions do not; at one depth its effects are specific but rarely change answers (e451, e454, e455). e455's swaps accumulated to 6-8 times natural size; near natural size one word moves 35-58% of translations (e458, area 17). A block re-implemented from scratch to the same function keeps the loss within 0.02 nats with different rows, whose word advantage is about half the original's; with frozen random writers it has none (e452).

## How the results flow

- `e444 → e449 → e444b`. In grokking, freezing the MLP write rows left the network generalising but barely sparser in its own words than in rotated ones (0.59 against 0.73). e449 repeated the test outside grokking and found the whole dictionary still beat rotation, because the description moved to the trained head bases and embeddings. That forced a test of the frozen rows themselves (e444b in grokking, e449b in the sequence model): they are barely used as words (0.69-0.75 against 0.74), while trained rows are. The vocabulary is written by training the writers.
- `e444` (timing and memorisation). The word-level part (own over covA) reached half its maximum 500-750 steps before test accuracy passed 50% in both standard runs, but not in the frozen variants. Memorisers (no weight decay, permuted labels) got only the second-order part (word-level maximum 0.02-0.04 against 0.08-0.11). A candidate early signal, not yet a reliable one.
- `e446 → e448 → e448b → e448d`. Token-level proxies could not certify meaning, so meaning was tested across languages: sentence bags of native words retrieve translations (e448); a per-sentence search finds shared "Rosetta" words (e448b); a controlled test of 24 nouns in 5 templates and 4 languages counts concept words: 7 in SmolLM2, 15 in Qwen-0.5B, 22 in Qwen-7B, 0 with rotated words (e448d).
- `e448b → e448c, e448e`. Two objections to the Rosetta words. They are partly multilingual neurons: the neuron writes about half the word's component in Qwen-0.5B and a tenth in SmolLM2, where many writers share the direction. They matter causally, more than a matched other word in 4 of 4 languages in SmolLM2 and 3 of 4 in Qwen, with small effects there.
- `e447 → e447b`. e447's lambda-0 control overfit its 64 sequences (0.41 nats lost), which swamped the term's cost; e447b fine-tuned on text sampled from GPT-2 itself. Euclidean self-describability can be bought (unexplained down 9% for +0.008 nats, up to 80% for +0.91), but functional self-description does not improve and the write rows do not move. VISION's roadmap asks for a functional objective next.
- `e398 → e445`. Seeds' vocabularies were already private as vectors (e398). Across Pythia sizes, usage partners exist above chance, but a word table carries 0.06-0.13 of function while a dense ridge map carries 0.85-0.91, and a learned linear decoder from one model's word coefficients matches the other model's own 16 words (0.59-0.67).
- `e450 → VISION roadmap`. Each word costs a 17-18-bit index plus a 6-bit coefficient, so the native code loses to PCA at 64 bits in all five models and at 128 in four, and wins at 512 in all five. Entropy-coding the indices by their Zipf-like frequencies is proposed, not tested.
- `e448e → e451 → e454 → e455`. e448e measured only a generic loss cost of removing a concept word. At one depth (e451, e454) its effects are specific: a swap raises the target noun 3-13 times as much as random, and the target category 2-15 times. But answers rarely change: at one layer a single word is outvoted by the other directions that carry the noun at the same position (e469 patches the whole state there and switches 78-99%). Swapped at every depth (e455), it redirects most translations and, in the Qwen models, most category answers, so it is a causal handle. Those swaps were 6-8 times natural size (e458); near natural size 35-58% of translations move. Removal alone rarely breaks the Qwen models, so it is not the only carrier.
- `e398 → e452 → e452b`. The same function re-implemented from scratch uses different rows (median best |cos| 0.26-0.28), which are words about half as good as the original's. Writers frozen at random reproduce the function with no words at all, as in e444b and e449, now at language-model scale with the function fixed. Most of the remaining gap is fit quality (e452b): more distillation reaches 0.71-0.77 of the original's advantage, while next-token training adds nothing. Only an exact fit would pin the rows.
- `e445 → e453`. Even the most semantic words are not shared across sizes. The concept correspondence survives a dense map, but only weakly (abs cos 0.08).
