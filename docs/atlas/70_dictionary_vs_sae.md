# 70. The brake at 7B and the Fisher-graded dictionary (S162)

**Question.** The note's readers will ask two things: whether collective fields account for a state better than native rows alone, SAE features alone, or combinations, scored on the model's output; and whether the brake's numbers depend on the sixteen wires and the five percent class. e715 answers the first on GPT-2 small with a public SAE, e716 the second on two blocks.

**Established.**
- The dictionary against the SAE: sixteen native rows recover 0.92 to 0.95 of the mean-ablation KL, rows plus fields 0.93 to 0.95, the SAE's top sixteen latents 0.95 to 0.97 and its full active set 0.98 to 0.99, with intervention fidelity 0.65 to 0.71 against 0.53 to 0.60: reading: on the state's own account the SAE wins and the fields do not matter; on the state's response to an intervention the native rows win and the SAE's encoder loses: at GPT-2 small blocks 5 and 8, sixteen atoms replacing a position's state recover 0.92 to 0.95 of the mean-ablation KL from native rows and 0.93 to 0.95 with the upstream fields added, against 0.95 to 0.97 for the SAE encoder's top sixteen latents, 0.97 to 0.98 for sixteen SAE decoder directions chosen by pursuit, 0.97 to 0.99 for rows plus SAE directions and 0.98 to 0.99 for the SAE's full active set of 58 to 82 latents, every dictionary above 0.98 by sixty-four atoms and the stream's sixteen principal directions at 0.84 to 0.89. The fields add 0.01 to the rows on this score, as e703 found for the state's energy and e714 for the pursuit: a field is an atom for a neuron's readers (e706), not for a position's state. The SAE's directions are the better dictionary for this model's state, which they were trained on, by 0.02 to 0.05 at sixteen atoms; but its encoder is a worse selection rule than pursuit over its own directions (0.95 to 0.97 against 0.97 to 0.98 in KL, 0.53 to 0.60 against 0.83 in how the reconstruction responds to a fixed perturbation), and the native rows respond to perturbations more like the state than the encoder's choice does (0.65 to 0.72 against 0.53 to 0.60). The answer to the review's decisive question is therefore split by what is scored: for reconstructing a state's effect, a trained dictionary beats the native rows by a few hundredths and the fields are not the unit; for a neuron's input, the fields are the unit the rows lack (e706, e713). (e715).
- The brake under other choices: the off-class share of the sixteen-input damage is 0.95 at a class cut of 1 percent, 0.81 to 0.88 at 5 and 0.48 to 0.72 at 20, excitatory wires alone cost 18 to 2328 times the ablation damage for k of 8 to 32 against 1.97 to 3.68 with k inhibitory wires, and thirty-two wires still leave 1.19 to 2.30 at the firings: reading: the brake does not depend on the sixteen or the five percent: the off-class share of the sixteen-input damage is 0.95 with the class cut at 1 percent, 0.81 to 0.88 at 5 and 0.48 to 0.72 at 20, falling as the class is widened because more of the damage is then counted as on-class, not because the brake weakens; excitatory wires alone run away at every count, 18 to 126 times the ablation damage with eight, 45 to 561 with sixteen and 121 to 2328 with thirty-two, and the k inhibitory wires bring each to 2 to 4; at the firings the signed wires are below the k largest by magnitude at every k, and thirty-two wires of any kind still leave 1.2 to 2.3 of the ablation damage. The runaway grows with k because more excitatory inputs without their brake fire the neuron harder; the crowd is not sixteen wires or thirty-two, and the numbers in the note are the conservative middle of the table. (e716).
