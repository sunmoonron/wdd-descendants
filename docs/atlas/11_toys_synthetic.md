# 11. Synthetic constructions and toy transformers

**Question.** Which of the real models' phenomena (identification failures, the decoupling of readability from importance, circuit redundancy and selection) can be reproduced in systems whose ground truth is known?
**Established.** Synthetic states show that the largest write is always identified unless cancelled (s1), that OMP peels off only a few sizeable cancellers that are atoms (s1e), and that the real one-shot-over-OMP signature appears with a larger near-twin writer (s1f); a layered stream shows cross-block sharing degrades state reading but not increment reading, and a construction shows reconstruction cannot certify provenance (s2). s1b, s1c and s1d were construction artifacts and are retracted. In trained toys the prominence law transfers (e153); one extra block or twin re-writes leave readability tied to importance (e171, e174), while four or more residual blocks decouple them by swamping, not aliasing (e175, e176); guided decoding recovers only part of the lost provenance (e177), and the toy reinforces but never erases (e190). Tiny attention-only transformers reproduce phase 2's selection and co-selection claims under manipulation, with redundancy and self-repair mainly under training noise, a toy-only statement given prior work and e384 (e379, e386).
**Start here:** s1f, s2, e153, e175, e379 · **Sessions:** S1, S2, S3, S33, S34 · **Scripts:** `scripts/s1_synthetic.py` to `scripts/s1f_majority.py`, `scripts/s2_layered.py`, `scripts/e153_*.py` to `scripts/e190_*.py`, `scripts/e379_toy_induction.py`, `scripts/e386_toy_selfrepair.py`

## How the results flow

- `s1 → s1b → s1e`: s1 finds that only cancellation breaks recovery and guesses that real cancellation is diffuse; s1b's near-orthogonal cancellers needed 8x the write's energy (retracted); s1e's anti-aligned, appended partners show one-shot reading is prominence-limited and OMP peels only a few sizeable cancellers.
- `s1c, s1d → s1e → s1f → e174, e181`: s1c and s1d wrote partner atoms over shared rows (retracted, KILLED); appended atoms fix it; s1f traces one-shot > OMP to a dominant near-twin; e174 puts twins in a trained toy (no decoupling) and e181 finds twin aliasing in 47% of GPT-2's mid-depth misses but 1-2% in the other four models.
- `e02 → s2 → e44`: direction sharing across blocks degrades state reading but not increment reading, the mechanism offered for e02 and e44; s2(B) turns the provenance null of e09 into a hard construction, which e61 cites.
- `e153 → e171 → e174 → e175`: the law transfers to a toy; one extra block and twin redundancy leave readability tied to importance; four or more blocks reproduce amplification with unreadability, the law and the decoupling.
- `e175 → e176 → e177`, `e176 → e181`: the toy loses provenance at accumulation by swamping; guided decoding triples the state reading but cannot restore it; e181 asks the same alias-or-swamp question in the real models.
- `e173, e186 → e190`: the erasure matrix and hydra test in the toy show only reinforcement, so the toy line stopped (H6e killed; the contraction needs a trained language model).
- `e365, e368, e370b, e377 → e379 → e386`: phase 2's observational claims are reproduced under manipulation; redundancy and self-repair appear mainly with training noise; e384 and the session-34 literature check limit this to toys.
