# What WDD is: synthesis of the 2026-09-19 mapping sprint

Scope: about 75 experiment families (e00 to e126, S1 to S2), ~400 result files, on GPT-2 small, SmolLM2-135M, Qwen2.5-0.5B, Pythia-410m, OLMo-1B-0724, plus seven Pythia training checkpoints, a random-init GPT-2, Pile caches for two models, and six synthetic constructions. One A100, 16,384 WikiText-2 evaluation states per model. Everything is in `results/` (one JSON per run, one line per run in `results/FINDINGS.log`), scripts in `scripts/`, the running narrative in `FINDINGS.md`. An independent code audit ran over the key scripts; its corrections are folded in below and listed at the end. Nothing here is an absolute; every number is from these five small models.

## The one-paragraph answer

WDD is classical sparse approximation (matching pursuit 1993, basis pursuit 1998, least squares 1809) over a dictionary that is fixed by the checkpoint, exactly invariant under the model's reparameterization symmetries, and labelled with which component wrote each atom. The label is the only new ingredient, and it makes three questions separable that are usually conflated: which vectors reconstruct a state, which writes generated it, and which writes matter causally. The sprint's central result is that these three come apart in every model. Reconstruction is what the forward pass cares about and is what OMP is best at. Provenance recovery is governed by how prominent a write is in the centered state relative to a competitor level set by the state covariance, and it is what the projection readings and basis pursuit are best at. Causal importance is unrelated to either. The residual stream is an information-losing accumulation of writes; the block increments, which hidden states give for free, are the object on which provenance survives, and reading them recovers the largest writes of the gated models almost perfectly.

## Findings, grouped by what they establish

### 1. Identification is a prominence phenomenon (with the audit's framing)

- The identification rate of a token's dominant MLP write is a step function of the write's prominence, |x_c · d| / ||x_c||, in every model (e56, e57). For the projection reading this is definitional: the top-64 correlations contain the atom iff its correlation beats the 64th competitor (audit). The non-definitional content is that OMP tracks the same quantity (AUC 0.84 to 0.92, e08), that the token-level intervention is causal for OMP (e60: adding back a cancelled write's own projection takes identification from 0.02 to 0.20 up to 0.47 to 0.96 in all five models; subtracting 90% of an intact write's projection takes it from 0.75 to 0.97 down to 0.12 to 0.39), and that the competitor level is level-independent.
- The competitor level is set mostly by the state covariance's effective dimension, not by write structure. Random directions drawn with the typical-state covariance reach a maximum correlation of 0.30/0.35/0.25/0.23/0.22 against 0.35/0.39/0.32/0.27/0.28 for real states (isotropic random: 0.16/0.18/0.15/0.14/0.10), e118. The floor sqrt(2 ln m / d) that matched the crossings (e57: 0.19/0.18/0.14/0.14/0.11 vs 0.17/0.20/0.15/0.17/0.11) is the k=1 order statistic and matches the k=64 crossing by coincidence (audit); the honest version uses d_eff and the k-th order statistic (e62: multipliers of the isotropic floor 0.84 to 1.18 for one-shot, 0.98 to 1.40 for OMP).
- What the cross-level test shows (e58): the prominence distribution alone reproduces one-shot identification at every level of every model (106 model-level points, correlations 0.92 to 0.99, mean errors 0.02 to 0.11) and across Pythia checkpoints (0.97 to 0.99, bias shrinking from −0.12 at step 4k to 0 at 143k). Since the floor is constant within a model, this tests the distribution, not the threshold value.
- The corrected law (e127): with the threshold set per level by the covariance-matched 64th-order competitor level, the prominence distribution predicts one-shot identification across all levels of all five models with correlations 0.97 to 0.99, mean errors 0.02 to 0.045 and biases within ±0.04 (the isotropic k=1 floor had biases up to +0.11). The honest depth profile of the largest physically surviving write (e128) runs from 0.86 to 1.00 at level 0 to 0.81 to 0.99 at level 6 under the dual reading (level 6 is GPT-2's mid layer, and earlier than the mid layer in the other four; wording corrected when the atlas was built).
- Two failure modes separate (e46, e47, S1f): invisibility (the write is not prominent; no solver reads it; ~9 to 18% of dominant neurons in GPT-2, Qwen) and misattribution (the write is readable but OMP picks a competitor; SmolLM2's never-class drops from 16% to 3% under the dual reading). The synthetic S1f reproduces the misattribution regime exactly: one near-twin atom (cosine 0.9) carrying 2 to 4 times the write drops OMP to 0.36 to 0.44 while the projection reading stays at 1.00.

### 2. The solver ceiling and the reconstruction-attribution trade-off

- OMP is the worst identifier and the best reconstructor of every solver family tried (e04, e45, e93): on the same states, basis pursuit / LASSO, the canonical dual-frame projection (Duffin-Schaeffer 1952, one matmul), and hard-thresholding pursuit add 13 to 20 points of identification in all five models, and their supports reconstruct worse. Nonnegativity helps GELU models and hurts SwiGLU models by up to 47 points (the prior must match the nonlinearity).
- Identification is governed by dictionary size (competition), not budget: removing 15 of 16 block atoms with the true atom kept raises recall by 12 to 17 points; going from 16 to 64 atoms changes it by 2 to 3 (e10). A found write is found in the first one or two picks (e51). Beyond the first support the decomposition is reconstruction only: a second OMP pass on the residual finds no new true writes (e122).
- Function follows reconstruction (e105): splicing each reconstruction into the forward pass costs +0.10 to +0.42 nats for OMP@64 and +0.7 to +3.5 for the best attributions (dual, LASSO, the increment pipeline); the 64 largest true writes, refit, cost +1.3 to +2.8. What preserves the model's computation is not the list of writers.

### 3. Identifiability is a property of the neuron, and it is orthogonal to importance

- Per-neuron identification rates have an intraclass correlation of 0.95 to 0.97 in all five models (e05), transfer across corpora at Pearson 0.96 to 0.98 (e24), and appear as soon as anything is identifiable in training (ICC 0.90 by step 1000, e05 checkpoints).
- Single-neuron ablation (e59, e59b): the always-identified and never-identified sets are indistinguishable in cross-entropy cost, or the never set costs more (GPT-2: least readable third costs 2.8× the most readable third; Spearman with identification −0.23; other models +0.09, +0.10, −0.02, negligible). WDD reads the prominent, reinforced, redundant writes; readability is not importance in either direction.
- The information is present linearly for nearly every neuron: a ridge readout of a neuron's coefficient from the state reaches out-of-sample R² 0.5 to 0.7 (medians) including for never-identified neurons (e65). Identification failures are not information failures.

### 4. Cancellation anatomy and the two model regimes

- Physically erased writes are rare (raw survival < 0.25 in 1 to 6% of tokens). The paper's survival is centered-state survival; in GPT-2 the raw survival predicts nothing (AUC 0.43) and the centering term carries the effect (0.68), because frequent writers live in the mean (e08).
- Who cancels: always later MLPs, never attention (e08, re-run after the NeoX bias fix): Pythia's later MLPs remove 111% of the typical dominant write after earlier attention built 41% of it; Qwen −0.56, OLMo −0.44. GPT-2 and SmolLM2 dominant writes are instead reinforced to 2.4× their birth size.
- Two regimes: reinforcing models (GPT-2, SmolLM2; block-write cancel index 1.3 to 1.9, median prominence 0.26) and cancelling models (Qwen, Pythia, OLMo; 0.33 to 0.58, prominence 0.16 to 0.18). GPT-2 is competition-limited (targeted dictionaries recover most of its gap; coherence predicts identification at AUC 0.79; aliasing by the max-coherence partner is real only there, +0.12 over the base rate); the gated models and Pythia are cancellation-limited (e77, e41 with the proper null).
- A token's dominant writes do not cancel each other (59 to 80% of top-3 pairs have |cos| < 0.1; cancelling pairs 0 to 3%, e87); the crowd of small writes cancels them. Depth decay is not erasure in the reinforcing models: writes born in block 1 to 3 of GPT-2 have their direction amplified 3 to 13× while identification falls to 0.03 (e21), and in Pythia the same curve is true erasure (survival 2.0 to 0.57).

### 5. The target problem and the increment reading

- Summing a token's 8 largest true writes gives FVU 1.4 to 1.5 (> 1): the ledger's largest write is often not what is in the state (e06 oracle). From level 8 on in GPT-2, 70 to 76% of tokens' largest writes come from the block just written and are read at 0.2 to 0.3, while block-0 writes are read at 0.7 to 0.85 at every level (e22).
- A non-circular alternative target, the largest write that physically survived (raw survival > 0.75; it is the ledger's largest in 56 to 97% of tokens), is identified at 0.76 to 0.94 by the dual reading (OMP 0.55 to 0.81), e119. The "most prominent true write" target reaches 0.94 to 1.00 but is near-circular (audit) and is a mid-sized write (|c|/|c_max| 0.23 to 0.73).
- Reading each block's increment over that block's atoms (hidden states only, no hooks) is the high-fidelity mode: recall@8 of 0.73 to 0.96 in the gated models against 0.15 to 0.66 from the full state at 64 atoms (e02), and at a matched 64-atom budget the increment pipeline takes SmolLM2's top-3 attribution from 0.12 to 0.97 and the real-write share of the support from 0.24 to 0.84 (GPT-2 0.34 to 0.79) while the full-state reconstruction worsens (e44; budget caveat for three models in "equal" mode, energy mode agrees). The synthetic layered stream (S2) shows the mechanism: state-level identification falls from 0.93 to 0.29 as cross-block direction sharing rises while increment-level identification stays at 1.00. In training, block-local identifiability (0.3 to 0.5 at step 1000) precedes stream-level identifiability (0.03).

### 6. Attention, embeddings, the mean, the sinks

- Static SVD atoms do not read attention: the dominant head's subspace is selected in 2 to 24% of tokens and captures ~1% of its energy (e13); head writes are dense in their own subspace. OV value atoms (context states through W_V W_O, free from hidden states) capture 77% of a head's write when selected and recover the attention pattern from the write alone by nonnegative least squares at correlation 0.5 to 0.85 (e53); mid-block GPT-2 heads put 30 to 56% of their mass on the sink and have a large constant part (median DC share 0.44; 0.06 to 0.12 in the other models, e54, magnitude corrected by the audit). Attention is irrelevant to MLP identification in four models and reinforcing in Pythia (e11).
- The selected token-embedding atoms (9 to 36% of supports) are never context copies (0.2 to 1.5% match any earlier token vs 33 to 37% for random tokens, e23); they and the spurious MLP atoms are diffuse, not a fixed filler set, and pruning them does not help (e102). Removing all 51k embedding atoms or all attention atoms changes GPT-2's recall by ~2 points; MLP atoms alone reconstruct nearly as well as the full dictionary (e42).
- The centering mean is ~16-sparse in the dictionary and made of the sink neuron and a few attention atoms (e89); because the cached mean includes sink states, the sink neuron's atom is the first pick in 38% (SmolLM2) and 60% (OLMo) of typical supports (e126), and recentering on typical tokens shrinks the weight-vs-rotated gap by 0.01 to 0.03 without changing identification (e120). The sinks themselves are one neuron's write (GPT-2 mlp2#1825 on channel 447, OLMo mlp1#1710 on channel 1764, the published super weight, Qwen mlp2#813), cancelled by the last block in every model (by attention in GPT-2, by MLPs elsewhere; e20, e55).

### 7. What is learned, and what is invariant

- At random init the dictionary has no advantage over its rotation (FVU 0.50 vs 0.50 at every level) and nothing is identifiable (0.01 past block 4); trained states over a random-init model's dictionary sit at the random floor (e01, e80). Alignment appears by step 1000 of Pythia training, block-local identifiability by 1k to 4k, global by 4k to 32k, calibration keeps improving to 143k; the ledger sparsifies 10 to 20× (effective writes at the mid layer: SmolLM2 156, GPT-2 369, Qwen 387, OLMo 800, Pythia 2214; random init 5047; e25). Write directions keep rotating until late (atom cosine with the final atoms 0.35 at 4k, 0.93 at 64k, e81).
- Invariance, stated precisely. Under the neuron gauge of a gated MLP (rescaling a neuron's input row and output column reciprocally, sign flips, permutations) the model function, the unit atoms, the supports and the coefficients are all exactly invariant (e07: atoms match to cosine 1.0000, supports identical, coefficients equal up to permutation), while weight-norm rankings are not (Spearman 0.08). Under a coordinated orthogonal change of the residual basis applied to states and weights together, every WDD quantity (supports, coefficients, FVU, prominence, competitor level) is invariant because it depends only on inner products; the atoms themselves are equivariant (they rotate with the basis). Such a rotation is not a symmetry of these five models (per-coordinate normalization gains), so the coordinate system the dictionary lives in is the model's own. The provenance labels are coordinate-free by construction. What is decoder-dependent is the support and the coefficients, not the dictionary.
- Alignment is an extreme-value property, invisible to second-order statistics: the best single atom captures cosine 2 to 2.7× that of the rotated dictionary while frame-covariance alignment ranks a random dictionary highest (e03); the advantage lives in the first ~8 atoms and is exhausted in the residual (e09).
- Coefficient estimation error is systematic bias, not noise: a two-parameter calibration fit on held-out sequences cuts the median relative error from 0.45 to 0.12 (GPT-2) and transfers across corpora with identical parameters (e12, e95); support-based error bars fail (e48). The caveat: 84 to 97% of the identified-coefficient variance is between neurons, so WDD reads which neuron with its typical magnitude and only weakly the token-level variation (e52; OLMo is the exception).

## The decoder hierarchy and the identifiability limit

WDD is not OMP. The object is the triple (dictionary, provenance labels, observation), and different decoders answer different questions on it: the projection reading answers "which atoms are prominent" (definitionally), OMP answers "which atoms explain the state greedily" (best reconstruction, worst identification), basis pursuit and the dual-frame projection answer "which atoms are consistent with the state under a convex or whitened criterion" (best identification), and the oracle target experiments bound what any decoder could recover (the largest physically surviving write is read at 0.76 to 0.94; the most prominent true write at 0.94 to 1.00, near-circularly). Two computational histories with the same sum of writes are indistinguishable by any state-only reading, by construction, and by downstream behaviour, since the model only sees the sum; they are distinguishable by the increment reading exactly when the histories differ across blocks, and by the ledger always. That is the identifiability limit of the residual stream, and it is why the increment, not the state, is the object on which provenance survives (e44, S2).

## The five layers that do not collapse

Computational history (the ledger of attention, MLP and embedding writes) accumulates into the residual state; some writes stay prominent and others cancel or become shared (geometric observability); a decoder recovers part of the prominent provenance; and causal relevance is fixed by intervention independently of all of the above. The sprint's central empirical claim is that these layers are separable and do not collapse into one another: reconstruction quality does not certify provenance (S2B, e09, e122), provenance recovery does not track causal importance (e59), and the decoder changes which provenance is recovered without changing the dictionary (e04, e46). WDD is the framework for measuring how much computational history is observable from a state; OMP is one decoder for it.


## Session 2 (free exploration, 2026-09-19 02:17 to 06:17 EDT): what the second round established

- The observability law survives held-out and zero-parameter tests (e129): the competitor level estimated from other sequences predicts identification on new sequences at every level (correlations 0.97 to 0.99, biases within 0.03), the per-token rule "identified iff prominence exceeds the level" is 93 to 96% accurate for the projection reading and 77 to 89% for OMP, and a level estimated from the step-64k checkpoint predicts the final checkpoint (0.94). The same law predicts the provenance-survival curve of the top-1/3/8 true writes with zero parameters (e167; GPT-2 and Qwen exact at the state level).
- The provenance-survival curve (e131): the top-k true writes are recovered at 1.00 at the write level, 0.85 to 1.00 (top-1) and 0.67 to 0.97 (top-8) at the increment level, and 0.62 to 0.83 / 0.40 to 0.59 at the state level. Provenance is lost at the accumulation step. State-level never-neurons are readable in their own increments at 0.91 to 1.00 in four models (e147); in GPT-2 the loss is within the block (0.11).
- What "identifiability is a neuron property" means (e130): mostly an amplitude phenotype (the neuron's typical prominence explains 44 to 74% of the between-neuron variance; geometry adds in GPT-2 and OLMo); within a neuron, token prominence still governs (AUC 0.72 to 0.82).
- Function, three ways. Single-write first-order attribution and WDD's reading are nearly disjoint (the writes WDD reads carry ~1% of the loss attribution, e156), yet the identified provenance is collectively 1.9 to 11 times more functional per unit energy than the spurious part when removed from the state (e152; GPT-2 1.9, Pythia 2.6, Qwen 2.8, SmolLM2 5.0, OLMo 11; the first version said 1.4 to 14, mixing raw and per-energy ratios), and a per-write ablation agrees with first-order attribution only moderately (Spearman 0.46 to 0.56, with 43 to 45% of removals helping, e160). Function follows reconstruction at equal budget: the increment reading needs 3 to 4 times the atoms to match global OMP's spliced cross-entropy (e166).
- Decoders: restricting the state decomposition to increment-nominated atoms triples the real-write share of the support (0.29 to 0.34 becomes 0.76 to 0.93) at a small function cost (e144, e159), the best compromise found; reweighted l1 halves LASSO's shrinkage without changing identification (e154); differential reading of the same token in two contexts doubles the readability of contextual writes (e162).
- The support is a code: kNN next-token accuracy from support overlap alone is within 0.01 to 0.03 of the state's own cosine geometry (e151), and the model's dictionary is a shorter description of its states than PCA at equal distortion in GPT-2 and equal in SmolLM2 (e158).
- Attention, further: value atoms make about half of a block's attention write attributable inside the increment (e143); attention-mediated changes of a downstream MLP write are fully visible in the state and tracked by OMP with the right slope (e132); attention is irrelevant to MLP identification in four models and reinforcing in Pythia (e11 v2).
- Regimes, further: the erased writes are the consumed ones in GPT-2 (read strength predicts erasure at AUC 0.72) and the discarded ones in the gated models (AUC 0.32 to 0.37) (e140); cancellation is diffuse, no single opposing neuron carries it (e141); high-entropy tokens are more readable in GPT-2 and SmolLM2 because their states are smaller and dominated by the block-0 token-lookup write, and the relation is absent in Pythia and reversed in OLMo (e155, e161).
- Toys: the one-layer superposition toy reproduces the law; a two-layer toy with one extra residual block does not decouple readability from importance (it takes the real models' multi-block redundancy and cancellation to do that), which is a negative result for the simplest-system hypothesis (e153, e171).

## Session 3 (free exploration, 2026-09-19 02:17 to 06:17 EDT, second 4-hour timer): erasure, contraction, certificates, causation

- Who erases whom (e173, five models, plus the Pile caches, random init and Pythia checkpoints). The additive direct-contribution matrix (birth block × contributing block, MLP and attention split) has two topologies: within-block cancellation in GPT-2 (a block's other neurons cancel 0.5 to 0.7 of its dominant writes, its attention reinforces everything) and a next-block chain in Qwen, OLMo and Pythia after block 5 (−0.2 to −0.4, MLP-borne), with a dedicated block-5 canceller of blocks 0 to 4 in Pythia. Block-0 directions are reinforced by later writes in GPT-2, SmolLM2 and Pythia. The topology is data-invariant (Pile caches reproduce it), absent at initialisation, and the Pythia block-5 canceller exists by step 1000 while the chain forms between steps 4000 and 64000.
- The eraser is not a wire (e183, e186, e185, e187, e191, e192). Ablating the next block's MLP never restores a cancelled write (survival stays at the ablate-b+3 control in 5/5); the remaining later blocks take over the cancellation (OLMo: the sum over blocks ≥ b+2 moves from −0.03/−0.17/−0.24/−0.22 to −0.36/−0.59/−0.53/−0.53, matching the removed −0.3). The cancellation is a crowd (top neuron 1 to 3%, 200 to 400 neurons for half of it, top canceller row cos ~0 with the write), proportional to the write (constant relative cancellation across magnitude quartiles in Qwen, OLMo, GPT-2), not the output bias (2 to 13%), and it has almost no anti-aligned-row signature in the weights (within-block anti-aligned pairs exist mainly in GPT-2: 6% of rows).
- The eraser is a generic contraction (e194, e207, e210, e209). Perturbing the input of block b+1 along the block-b write direction changes its output along that direction with a gain of −0.16 to −0.42, exactly linear over 0.25 to 1× the write, carried by the MLP, and the same along random directions (OLMo −0.42 vs −0.40, Qwen −0.38 vs −0.38); the gain compounds multiplicatively over blocks. It is zero at random initialisation, grows through training (Pythia −0.03 at step 1000, −0.13 at 16000, −0.22 final), fades to about 0 in the last blocks, never becomes a generic expansion, and block 0 expands (+0.7 in Pythia). It is derived from the weights: the normalisation gain times the Jacobian trace tr(W_down diag(act') W_in)/D computed from hooked pre-activations reproduces the measured gain block by block in 5/5 (OLMo −0.37/−0.34/−0.28 predicted vs −0.37/−0.32/−0.27 measured). The static signature is each neuron's read and write vectors being anti-aligned: GPT-2 mean cosine −0.11 (49% of neurons below −0.1), Pythia −0.22 (70%), 0.00 at init, formed by step 4000; in the gated models the sign lives in the active neurons (the unweighted cosine is positive early and negative late). The next-block entries of the erasure matrix in the chain models (−0.33 to −0.36) are this contraction; GPT-2's next-block reinforcement is functional re-writing of about +0.5 on top of a −0.34 contraction.
- Persistence is active re-writing (e188, e193, e198, e208, e212). A passive write would keep (1−g)^n of itself (0.22 to 0.47 after three blocks); observed centered survival after three blocks is 0.5 to 0.7 in the damped models (OLMo, Pythia, Qwen), and grows in GPT-2 and SmolLM2 (most of GPT-2's raw growth is the level mean drifting along the dominant early directions; the rest is token-specific). Depth profiles come in three shapes: damped, damped-then-re-amplified (Qwen's late layers re-write early directions, directionally, not by generic gain), amplified. Zeroing the token's dominant write removes only 39 to 44% of the direction's component two blocks later, 27% four blocks later and 8 to 19% at the mid layer: the direction is mostly written anyway by other components. The write-dependent part decays as the contraction predicts (OLMo 0.36 to 0.39 at +2 vs 0.38), and a write's causal half-life is 1.5 to 2 blocks in all five models regardless of depth (GPT-2 and SmolLM2 shorter than the contraction alone predicts, from their write-specific cancellation). Writes born in the last third keep two to four times more of their causal footprint to the top (dependence at the last level 0.21 to 0.50 vs 0.08 to 0.19 for early writes read at the mid layer, e212), in the direction the fading contraction predicts, though late write-specific cancellation remains (dependence 0.4 to 0.55 two blocks later where a pure contraction would leave 0.9). The best level to read a write is the one right after it in four models (recall 0.95 to 1.00 at +0, 0.00 to 0.10 at the top); GPT-2 reads early writes best two to four blocks later. Across birth blocks and levels the prominence law holds with Spearman 0.90 to 1.00.
- Two kinds of spurious atom, two certificates (e189, e195, e196, e200, e202, e203). After ablating a block, OMP still selects that block's atoms at the clean rate although their true coefficient is exactly zero (Qwen 0.044 vs 0.046): selection share is dictionary geometry, not evidence of writing. The OMP support is chaotic (Jaccard 0.03 to 0.14 with itself after a one-block ablation) while the projection support is robust (0.57 to 0.82). The per-atom selection frequency on covariance-matched noise predicts the frequency on real states (dual Spearman 0.89 GPT-2, 0.88 SmolLM2, 0.70 Qwen, 0.54 OLMo, 0.38 Pythia; OMP lower): the dual's spurious atoms are the geometric prior (the workhorse atoms aligned with the state's top principal directions, 20 to 56% of its support at precision 0.25 to 0.35), OMP's are noise-driven picks. Accordingly, stability selection (atoms that survive 10% input noise, Meinshausen-Bühlmann) certifies OMP in 5/5 (precision 0.16 to 0.37 becomes 0.39 to 0.67, 0.52 to 0.80 at 30% noise, keeping 85 to 92% of the dominant-write recall) and does nothing for the dual, while the per-atom z-score of the whitened score against its covariance-matched null certifies the dual (|z| > 3: precision +0.1 to +0.3, dominant recall kept at 92 to 100%, transfers unchanged to the Pile caches). Dropping the prior atoms is a bad certificate (recall collapses). Dominant recall is a clean sigmoid in the atom's z in 5/5 (below 2: 0.0 to 0.4; 4 to 6: 0.82 to 0.97; above 6: 0.99 to 1.00); the crossover is near the 64th null extreme at k = 64 but the extreme-value account does not survive a collapse test across k = 8 to 256 in OLMo, Qwen and GPT-2 (crossover spreads 5.0, 1.6, 2.8 z-units) and only approximately in SmolLM2 and Pythia (0.2, 1.0; e205): the competition is from real, correlated writes, and the threshold is empirical. A stable real atom at level L is still read at L+1 in 58 to 76% of tokens and at L+2 in 39 to 66% (unstable ones 8 to 20%): certified provenance can be followed for two levels. The residual-based self-certificate flags 85 to 100% of tokens and does nothing (e180).
- Three quantities per token, now separated (e211, e165, e152, e181, e199, e206). Whether the decoder reads the dominant atom at level L is uncorrelated with how much the direction depends on that write (Spearman −0.09 to +0.03 in four models, +0.25 in GPT-2) while prominence predicts it (+0.40 to +0.61): WDD reads directions, and an identified atom is a correct label for who wrote the direction, not a statement that the write caused its presence. Identified writes are 1.6 to 2.9 times more functional per unit energy than unidentified ones in four models and the reverse in OLMo (unidentified writes there include neurons whose removal is catastrophic, +3.2 nats). Misses at mid depth are swamped, not aliased (an alias with cos > 0.5 exists in 1 to 2% of misses; 47% in GPT-2, the aliasing model), and late-layer misses are everything (state norm 3 to 15×). Never-neurons are not a geometric class (the predicted own-write z does not order neurons; never-read neurons have the same or higher predicted z), and canceller blocks are not the unreadable blocks (Pythia's blocks 5, 10, 11 are among the most readable). The erasure is itself readable: decomposing block b+1's increment over the atoms of blocks b and b+1 selects the block-b dominant atom in 73 to 74% of tokens (Qwen, OLMo) with a coefficient ratio of −0.47, an opposite-sign copy.
- Toys, closed (e190): a six-block residual autoencoder produces reinforcement chains (+3 to +5), never erasure; the contraction needs a trained language model.
- Gain versus norm (e213, run after the timer): the per-token contraction is inversely proportional to the state norm in 5/5 (Spearman(gain, log norm) +0.44 to +0.62; gain × norm flat across norm quartiles in Qwen, OLMo and Pythia, drifting 20% in GPT-2 and SmolLM2; position adds nothing), as the normalization-gain factor of the derived formula predicts. Small states damp writes hardest, large states preserve them.

## Session 4 (round four, after the reviewer list; 2026-09-19 12:00 box time onward): attacking the contraction

- Direction lottery and norm sweep (e214, e220). The along-direction gain is the same within 0.2 for random directions, the token's dominant and other active MLP writes, its attention write, its embedding and most principal directions in Qwen, OLMo, Pythia and SmolLM2; it is zero along the state's own direction (the normalization is scale-invariant; OLMo's −0.19 comes from its attention writes being comparable to the state); and it is linear over three decades (0.001 to 0.1× the state norm), weakening at 1×. The spectrum is not a monotone ordering (Spearman −0.5 to +0.7 across ranks): the top principal direction is damped hardest in GPT-2, Qwen and Pythia, the next few top directions weakly, the bulk moderately, only the covariance's null direction escapes, and OLMo damps uniformly. GPT-2 is the exception to direction-independence: it damps its own write directions and its top principal direction 3 to 4× more than random directions, the write-specific cancellation already seen in its within-block topology, aliasing and short causal half-life.
- Composition (e215). Single-block gains were measured for every block (they peak mid-depth and fade to zero in the last blocks; Pythia's block 0 expands by +0.70). The survival of a random perturbation after three blocks is the product of the single-block gains within 10 to 20% in four models and 50% in Qwen, passing the 2× kill test in 5/5; beyond three blocks the product over-damps and a floor of 0.1 to 0.2 of the along-direction component persists.
- What the contraction actually is (e221, e222). The perturbation's energy is conserved or amplified while its direction is scattered: after the injecting block the total change is already 0.9 to 1.5× the injected size with only 0.6 to 0.9 along the axis, and after seven blocks the total is 0.65 to 2.2× while the along-axis part is 0.08 to 0.34 (cosine 0.04 to 0.25), drifting mildly toward high-variance directions. The 'contraction' of session 3 is the diagonal of a near-isometric, strongly mixing block Jacobian. The direct test with real writes agrees: zeroing a token's dominant write, its atom's readability and along-direction footprint fall together with depth (Qwen 0.98/1.00 → 0.37/0.18 over six blocks, OLMo 0.98/1.00 → 0.39/0.13) while the write's total footprint in the state grows (Qwen 1.0 → 1.8, OLMo 1.0 → 2.4, GPT-2 1.0 → 1.5, Pythia ~1.1, SmolLM2 1.0 → 1.8). Provenance fades by decorrelation, not by loss: a write's consequences persist and grow in other directions after its atom stops being readable, which is why removing a write costs function long after it is unreadable and why the along-direction survival floors instead of vanishing.
- Normalization (e216). Freezing both normalizations of a block at a constant per-token scale removes the norm dependence (Spearman(gain, log norm) +0.47 to +0.55 → −0.10 to +0.03 in 5/5), cleanly in the GELU models where the median gain is preserved (Pythia −0.20 vs −0.21); the gated models are pushed off their operating point by a constant scale (SwiGLU is quadratic in input scale), so only the vanishing correlation is interpretable there.
- Zero-sum computation (e219). Only GPT-2 loses write energy inside its blocks (its MLP increments carry 31 to 51% of their neurons' summed write energy, 66% of all MLP write energy survives to the mid layer); Qwen, OLMo, Pythia and SmolLM2 write constructively (coherence ratios 1.08 to 1.18, 2 to 4 in block 0 and the last block). Pythia is a GELU model too, so GPT-2's destructive interference is its own organisation, not the nonlinearity.
- Granularity (e218). Coarser labels do not rescue MLP provenance: block-level recall exceeds a control block by 0.03 to 0.31 (above 0.2 only in GPT-2 and SmolLM2), and when the writer's atom is not the closest support atom the closest one is from the same block in only 2 to 5% of tokens. Attention is different: the head with the largest write in block L is identified by its own OV atom in 73% (GPT-2) and 100% (Pythia) of tokens under OMP against controls of 5 to 10%.

- Birth (e217). Pythia's read/write anti-alignment and gain are indistinguishable from initialisation through step 512, appear by step 1000 and 2000 (cosine −0.037 then −0.157, gain −0.06) as the learning-rate warmup ends, and the alignment leads the gain (70% vs 20% of the final value at step 2000). The dedicated block-5 canceller of the erasure matrix exists at step 1000, before the generic decorrelation: specific cancellation forms before generic mixing.

## Session 5 (round five): where a write's energy and provenance go

- Exact accounting (e223). The footprint of a zeroed write decomposes exactly, with zero residual, into minus the write, the MLP responses of every later block and the attention responses of every later block. The two responses are both large, of comparable size, and partly cancel (OLMo at the mid layer: MLP part 3.6, attention part 3.4, footprint 2.6, all relative to the write). The MLP part is a ledger over known writers and it is as diffuse as it can be: 400 to 1,500 effective writers per intervening block, growing linearly with depth to 5,000 to 11,000, equal shares per block, a top successor carrying 1 to 8%, half of the energy on neurons already active and half on newly activated ones, with nearly incoherent delta-writes. In whitened (Mahalanobis) units the footprint is roughly conserved (0.6 to 1.8) while it grows in Euclidean units, so the Euclidean growth is the footprint moving into high-variance directions. The provenance horizon is 2 to 3 blocks and follows the state norm more than the write's size.
- No collisions (e224). Distinct real writes of the same token keep orthogonal footprints through depth (cosine at most 0.11) and only weakly related logit effects (0.06 to 0.46), while random perturbations of the same size converge onto a shared disturbance direction (footprint cosine 0.3 to 0.5, logit cosine up to 0.92). The dynamics keep real provenance separate and funnel junk.
- No funnel (e225). The cloud of real-write footprints across tokens has an effective dimension as large as or larger than the writes' own (Pythia 44 → 217, OLMo 54 → 280) and comparable to the state's, growing with depth; random-direction footprints shrink with depth. SmolLM2 is uninformative here because one massive neuron dominates its block-2 writes.
- Verdict. A write's energy is neither lost nor merged nor funnelled: it is dispersed, exactly accountably, over thousands of later writers and over attention, in a way that keeps distinct writes distinct and conserves their distinguishability against the state distribution, but leaves writer identity useless as a coordinate. WDD's provenance reading fails at depth because the natural coordinates of the write's descendants are no longer neurons, not because information disappears.

## Session 6 (round six): how descendants are transported, and whether provenance is invertible

- Linearity (e226). Two writes' descendants add and scale linearly in Pythia (errors 0.05 to 0.12) and GPT-2 (0.12 to 0.20), mildly nonlinearly in OLMo, and nonlinearly where massive-activation neurons dominate the writes: Qwen's descendants are additive across writes but not homogeneous in a write's size (0.8 to 0.9), SmolLM2's are not additive at all (1.3 to 1.9). Opposing write pairs interact more than reinforcing ones.
- Paths (e228). Freezing the later attention outputs (MLP-only transport) or the later MLP outputs (attention-only transport) shows the two paths are not additive: the interaction residual is 0.35 to 0.92 two blocks after the write and 1.1 to 2.8 at the mid layer. Attention-only transport overshoots the full footprint (1.4 to 9× the write) and the MLP responses cancel part of it; MLP-only transport tracks the descendant's direction better (cosine 0.62 to 0.88 at two blocks). Transport alternates between MLPs and attention and the paths depend on each other.
- Learned dispersal (e229). At initialisation Pythia transmits a write verbatim along its own direction (along-component 1.00, footprint cloud of the writes' dimension, no collisions); training lowers the along-component monotonically (0.79 at step 2000, 0.53 at 16000, 0.41 at 64000, 0.33 final at four blocks) while the Mahalanobis footprint stays at 1.0 and real writes never collide. The footprint cloud passes through a transient low-dimensional phase at step 2000 (dimension 32 to 52 against the writes' 66) before expanding past the writes (197 final).
- Descendant identity (e227, e230). The descendant is neuron-specific: footprints of the same neuron across tokens have cosine 0.20 to 0.31 at the mid layer against 0.01 to 0.12 between neurons, and the source neuron is identified from its footprint in 88 to 98% of tokens (chance 3 to 6%), the source block in 84 to 95% (chance 33%). A descendant dictionary built from footprint centroids reads the source neuron from the centered state itself in 37 to 66% of tokens in four models (chance 3 to 4%; SmolLM2 19%), beating the neuron's own atom used the same way in the damped gated models (Qwen 0.53 vs 0.24, OLMo 0.66 vs 0.33) and trailing it in the GELU models where the write's own direction survives longer (GPT-2 0.37 vs 0.47, Pythia 0.48 vs 0.54). WDD's reading of a write does not predict the write's behavioural influence (Spearman of the removal's KL with readability −0.16 to +0.24).
- Verdict. The transformation from a local write to its distributed descendants destroys the write's local coordinates but not the invertibility of its provenance: the descendant carries the neuron's identity, recoverable almost perfectly from the footprint and partially from the state. WDD is the coordinate system at birth; at depth the natural coordinates are descendant signatures, which are neuron-specific, high-dimensional, approximately linear in the GELU models, and carried by alternating MLP and attention transport.

## Session 7 (round seven): the descendant signature under attack

- It is the vector's image, mostly (e231). A neuron's median write vector injected into foreign states where the neuron is inactive produces a footprint that classifies as that neuron in 0.89 to 0.97 of tokens four blocks later in GPT-2, Pythia, OLMo and SmolLM2 (0.55 in Qwen; natural held-out 0.91 to 1.00; chance 0.03), independently of the injected magnitude (0.5× and 2× identical), while random directions of the same size classify at chance. At the mid layer transplants reach 0.25 to 0.71 against natural 0.71 to 0.98: about half of the natural identifiability there is vector-driven and half comes from the states the neuron naturally fires in. Two injected vectors are both recovered in the top two centroids in 0.55 to 0.83 of tokens at four blocks and 0.04 to 0.33 at the mid layer.
- The transport curve (e232). With 50 to 64 candidate neurons (5.6 to 6.0 bits), source identification by the native atom, by the descendant centroid on the state, and by the intervention footprint, plotted against depth, shows the crossover: the native coordinate leads for the first two to four blocks, the state-level descendant signature overtakes it at two blocks in Qwen and four in OLMo and Pythia (GPT-2 from the first block; SmolLM2 keeps the native read ahead until the end), and both fade in the last two blocks, where even the intervention footprint drops to 0.37 to 0.49 accuracy (2.5 to 3.7 bits) after holding 4.7 to 5.6 bits through the mid layer. The final layers are where provenance is finally scrambled.
- Robust (e233). Perturbing every weight by 3% of its RMS (the clean state moves 8 to 15%) leaves identification unchanged in 5/5; at 10% (state moved 28 to 56%, loss up by 0.1 to 7 nats) it is still 0.98 in Pythia and OLMo, 0.87 in GPT-2, 0.56 and 0.48 in Qwen and SmolLM2. The signature is a property of the linearised dynamics around the trained weights, not of their exact values.
- Transfers (e234). In GPT-2, centroids fitted on WikiText identify the source neuron on Pile tokens in 0.95 of cases without refitting (within-corpus 0.98 and 0.91; chance 0.07), and the reverse in 0.83. SmolLM2 transfers weakly (12 shared neurons, chance 0.08: within-corpus 0.61 and 0.45, WikiText to Pile 0.35, Pile to WikiText 0.27), consistent with its weaker signatures throughout.
- Both paths carry it (e235). MLP-only transport preserves the full footprint's signature in 5/5 (0.68 to 1.00); attention-only transport preserves it in GPT-2, Pythia and OLMo (0.91 to 0.94) and in Qwen and SmolLM2 carries a different, self-consistent neuron-specific signature (0.88 and 0.62 against its own centroids, 0.15 and 0.10 against the full ones). Attention rewrites the vector and keeps the source.
- Verdict. The descendant signature survives the attacks it was given: it is largely the deterministic image of the write vector under the network's transport, linear in the write's size, robust to weight noise, portable across corpora, carried redundantly by the MLP chain and by attention, readable from the state after a depth-dependent crossover, and only destroyed in the final two blocks. What it does not do is predict function, and at the mid layer it shares its identifiability with the contexts the neuron fires in. WDD is the chart at birth; the descendant signature is a second chart that takes over a few blocks later; neither survives the last layers.

## Session 8 (round eight): transported coordinates

- Transported WDD fails the kill-test (e236, e240). Transporting every native atom through the network (one atom per token position, averaged over three random contexts) and decoding states at depth with the transported dictionary gives dominant-write recall no better than the native dictionary anywhere and worse at the mid layer in GPT-2 (dual 0.89 → 0.62) and Pythia (0.68 → 0.48), equal in Qwen (0.51 → 0.49), much worse in OLMo (0.82 → 0.37) and slightly worse in SmolLM2 (0.78 → 0.74). The transported atoms become more coherent only in the gated models (Qwen, SmolLM2) and not in OLMo where the failure is largest; each is simply a blurred, context-averaged version of the token's actual descendant. The descendant coordinate is legible for classification among tens of candidates (sessions 6 and 7), not for full sparse decomposition against tens of thousands of atoms.
- Transport is an isometry within a state (e238). A write vector and a rotated copy injected at the same tokens keep their initial cosine to within 0.02 at every level, from 0.99 down to 0, in all five models: no resolution limit beyond the initial separation. Across tokens, the geometry of natural descendant centroids follows the write vectors' geometry only in the GELU models (Spearman 0.55 and 0.31, neighbour overlap above chance) and not in the gated ones, where context dominates the centroids.
- Capacity does not saturate (e237). Provenance bits recoverable from the state keep growing with the number of candidates up to 128 (Qwen 5.6 bits at one block and 5.1 at four, OLMo 4.9, Pythia 4.0, GPT-2 3.0 at 64), and the intervention footprint carries 6.0 to 6.4 of 7 bits to six blocks; the residual stream supports at least 128 distinguishable sources in descendant coordinates at the mid layer, except SmolLM2's mid layer where the state-level signal collapses to under one bit.
- The signature follows the vector across checkpoints (e239). Pythia's final write vectors pushed through the step-16000 network produce descendants that either checkpoint's centroids classify at 1.00 (the reverse 0.99 and 0.93), although the vectors themselves differ (cosine 0.74) and the centroids more so (0.54). The transport is stable from step 16000 on and the identity rides on the vector.
- Verdict. Provenance survives transport as an angle-preserving image of the write vector carrying, under the tested decoder and candidate sets, 6 to 6.4 of 7 recoverable bits, robust, portable and stable across training; but it is a classification coordinate, not a decomposition basis: the token-averaged transported atom is too blurred to replace the native dictionary in sparse coding. WDD's frozen dictionary is the right basis for reading what a token's largest write was; the descendant signature is the right object for asking which neuron a downstream footprint came from. Neither reads the last two blocks.

## Session 9 (round nine): compositionality, prediction, training order

- Compositional (e241). Injecting several distinct write vectors at once, the mixture's footprint is additive within 0.5 up to eight writes in all five models (sixteen in GPT-2, Pythia and SmolLM2), scales as the square root of the count, and the injected identities are recovered from it two blocks out at 0.61 to 0.88 for eight writes (chance 0.28 to 0.38) in all five; at the mid layer eight identities are still recovered at 1.5 to 2× chance in GPT-2, Pythia and OLMo, and at chance in Qwen and SmolLM2, whose single-write state-level identification is already weak there.
- Predictable (e242). A neuron's natural footprints are identified by the transplant image of its write vector, computed without any natural footprint of that neuron, as well as by fitted centroids: two blocks out in every model (0.93 to 1.00), and at the mid layer everywhere except the massive-activation block-2 neurons of Qwen and SmolLM2. The descendant of an unseen neuron is a function of its write vector, and the network's own transport is the decoder.
- Ordered by training (e243). On Pythia the transport is an isometry with identity along the write at initialisation (pair cosine preserved at every checkpoint); native readability appears with the warmup (0.01 at step 0, 0.18 at 1000, 0.38 at 4000) and then declines slightly as dispersal grows (along-survival 1.00 → 0.34); the footprint cloud contracts transiently at step 4000 (dimension 58 against 70) while provenance bits dip (3.4 of 5), then both expand (dimension 223, 4.7 bits); the scrambling of the penultimate block is a late-training effect (4.6 bits at initialisation, 3.3 at step 64000, 2.3 at the end).
- Not the gate (e244). The context variability of the MLP's first-order factor does not distinguish GELU from SwiGLU (relative deviation 1.23 and 1.86 versus 1.16 to 1.46), so the gated models' context-dominated centroid geometry is not a linearisation effect; it tracks their massive-activation neurons, the only neurons whose descendants the write vector fails to predict.
- Verdict. The descendant chart is compositional, predictable from the write vector, present as an isometry from initialisation, and sharpened rather than created by training; what training adds is dispersal (which kills native readability) and, late, the scrambling of the last blocks. The exceptions are the massive-activation neurons, not the architecture family.

## Session 10 (self-directed): the recoverability frontier

- Transport is compositional where decomposition is not (e245). With 64 candidate neurons and zero-shot transplant images, the largest of k heavy-tailed superposed writes is identified from the mixture's descendant at the mid layer at 0.92 (GPT-2), 0.84 (Pythia), 0.72 (Qwen) and 0.46 (SmolLM2) for k = 64 (chance 0.02), and equal-amplitude sets of 16 are recovered at 0.77, 0.60, 0.58 and 0.49; the native dictionary reads the largest injected write at 0.00 to 0.01 at every k. The context-independent part of a descendant is 0.27 to 0.47 of its energy two blocks out and 0.07 to 0.29 at the mid layer, the same for random unit vectors, and that small coherent part is enough for 64-way identification. Random unit vectors are transported and identified exactly like neuron writes: the code is vector-specific, not neuron-specific.
- The native failure is prominence (e247). An injected write at its neuron's median natural size has prominence 0.06 two blocks out and is read natively at 0.06; at 2×, 4× and 8× it is read at 0.52, 0.99 and 1.00 while its descendant identity stays at 1.00 throughout; natural writes of the same prominence are read the same. The native chart is a prominence-gated read of the along-direction component, which a natural write keeps only through its context's maintenance; the descendant chart reads identity from the transported image independently of magnitude and prominence.
- Data-free chart (e246). On Pythia, transplant images built on uniformly random token sequences identify natural WikiText footprints at 1.00 two blocks out and 0.93 at the mid layer (text-built images 1.00 and 0.95, fitted centroids 1.00 and 0.99); the chart needs the model and nothing else. The other four models were queued when the program closed.

## Session 11 (self-directed): characterising the transported object

- Crossover from gains. The depth at which the state-level descendant chart overtakes the native read is predicted by where the product of single-block gains drops the passive along-direction survival below one half: exactly in Pythia and Qwen, within a measured level in OLMo, and not where a chart is anomalous (GPT-2's prior-dominated projection read, SmolLM2's weak state-level signature).
- Not low-dimensional (e249). Ninety percent of full source identification needs 32 to 64 random dimensions (8 to 32 principal components) two blocks out and 128 to 256 (32 to 128 components) at the mid layer. The descendant is a distributed code, not a few hidden dimensions.
- Location (e249). Within a block, the attention residual add leaves the write's along-direction component unchanged and the MLP add reduces it, every block, in all five models; the descendant identity is unchanged by both through block 6. The native coordinate dies one MLP at a time; the descendant coordinate does not die at any sub-operation in the middle of the network.
- The massive-neuron boundary (e250). The largest neurons are transported linearly and angle-preservingly like any vector, but their natural descendants point away from their transplanted images (cosine −0.45 to −0.75 in Qwen, OLMo and SmolLM2): their natural effect goes through the attention-sink mechanism, which a transplanted copy of the vector does not trigger. They bound vector-determined transport rather than break it.
- An explicit transport operator (e248). A ridge map fitted on random injections predicts the descendants of unseen directions (cosine 0.58 to 0.94 two blocks out against a zero baseline), partially preserves their Gram matrix, and composes: the 2→4 map followed by the 4→6 map identifies natural footprints as well as the direct 2→6 map. Injecting a+b is classified as the a+b image, never as a or b, in all five models: the network transports vectors, and neuron identity is a label we attach at birth. The random-fitted operator reads natural provenance zero-shot and inverts descendants to their source in the GELU models (Pythia 1.00, GPT-2 0.88 two blocks out) but not in Qwen, where real writes and off-manifold directions are transported differently; the inverse identifies the source without reconstructing the vector (cosine 0.1 to 0.4), so the code is decodable, not linearly invertible.

## Session 12 (from the six-experiment program): recoding, regimes, function

- No single phase transition (e252). Measured jointly at every level, the observables are staggered: the write's along-direction component halves at blocks 4 to 8, the state-level descendant chart at 10 to 16, and the intervention footprint's identity only in the last one to three blocks or never; injected pairs keep their angle (0.73 to 0.76 for an initial 0.75) at every level to the last block in all five models. The late "scrambling" is a loss of the natural descendant's distinctness, not of the isometry. Whitened footprint energy is conserved to the mid layer and then grows in the amplifying models, so it is not a global invariant.
- Sparse at birth, distributed downstream (e251). With an equal budget of coefficients the native sparse code reconstructs the write perfectly at birth and identifies its source perfectly; at depth no small code reconstructs the descendant, but the dense descendant basis identifies the source far better than the native sparse code (32 coefficients at the mid layer: 0.70 to 0.90 vs 0.03 to 0.67, random directions 0.49 to 0.66). For provenance the recoding is real; for reconstruction the descendant is simply high-dimensional.
- The bridge to function (e254). A write's logit footprint identifies its source neuron at 0.52 to 0.60 (chance 0.03 to 0.05), and the geometry of descendant centroids predicts the geometry of logit footprints better than the geometry of write vectors in all five models (Spearman +0.23 to +0.61 vs −0.02 to +0.37); per token the descendant's nearest neuron matches the functional identity in about half the tokens, the native atom's in 2 to 21% in the gated models. Provenance does not predict a write's importance, but the descendant predicts what kind of effect it has.
- No within-token state variable for the massive neurons (e253). The transplanted descendant's match to the natural one is flat across the neuron's own activation, uncorrelated with it, and near zero in every context at the mid layer: the massive neurons' natural effect is cross-token, through the sink they create at other positions, so no single-token transport picture covers them.

## Session 13 (from the five-experiment list): descendants and function

- Descendants organise neurons by function (e255). Neurons that are neighbours in descendant space have more similar logit footprints than neighbours in write space in all five models, and than random neighbours in four; pairs with dissimilar write vectors but similar descendants are functionally similar in all five (functional convergence without convergence at birth); a held-out neuron's logit footprint is predicted from its descendant neighbours better than from its write neighbours in Qwen, SmolLM2 and Pythia, and no better than from random neighbours in GPT-2 and OLMo, where logit footprints share a large common component.
- Function composes (e256). The logit footprint of up to eight simultaneously injected writes is as additive as the state descendant (errors equal within 0.03 at every count), so the chain write → descendant → effect is compositional end to end; but identity is far less legible in function space than in the state (single-write logit identification 0.15 to 0.52 against 0.88 to 1.00; chance from an eight-write mixture).
- The massive neurons stay unexplained (e257). Their effect is token-local (0.89 to 1.00 of the footprint energy at the source position) and receivers do not identify them, so the cross-token route is dead along with the within-token variables; a side result is that cross-position provenance exists in GPT-2, where receiver positions identify a normal write at 0.46, and not in the other models.
- Coupling over training (e258). The descendant-function geometry coupling is highest at initialisation (Spearman +0.84, a nearly linear network), is broken by training (+0.14 at step 64000) and partly rebuilt by the end (+0.31); it is a property training reshapes, not one it creates after dispersal.
- One perturbation, three questions (summary of sessions 6 to 13, block-2 writes at the mid layer, K-way candidate sets):

## Session 14: the descendant as the functional coordinate, geometric but not operational

- Matched pairs (e259). Descendant similarity predicts functional similarity after controlling for write similarity in all five models (partial Spearman +0.29 to +0.60), and write similarity does not after controlling for descendant similarity (−0.06 to +0.16); in a 2×2 design functional similarity follows the descendant cell in every model, and the same-descendant, different-writer pairs are functionally similar above the median in every model. Functional convergence through transport survives the matched design.
- Depthwise geometry (e260). The correspondence between descendant geometry and logit-footprint geometry rises monotonically with depth in all five models, from the write-logit value at birth (−0.05 to +0.41) to +0.65 to +0.88 near the top, while the descendant's correspondence with write geometry decays over the same depths. Write to descendant is strong early, descendant to logit strong late, write to logit weak throughout.
- Functional dimensionality (e263). Predicting a token's logit footprint from its descendant saturates by 8 to 32 principal dimensions where identity needs 32 to 128, and the descendant-based prediction exceeds the identity oracle (the neuron's own mean footprint) in all five models: the descendant says more about what a token's write will do than the neuron label does.
- Two operational readings die (e261, e262). Neurons with similar descendants are not substitutable under intervention (substituting the partner's write worsens or does nothing for the removal effect), and the neurons that compensate for a removed write are a diffuse crowd only weakly aligned with either the write or its descendant. The descendant is a functional coordinate in the geometric sense, not a set of interchangeable parts.

## Session 15: what kind of object a descendant is

- The preimage coordinate of function (e265). A neuron's nearest neighbours in logit-footprint space are among its five nearest descendant neighbours in 31 to 75 percent of cases against chance 18 to 25, and only 3 to 12 percent of the most functionally similar pairs have dissimilar descendants. The write-space neighbour is lost in descendant space one to four blocks after the write (never in GPT-2) and the logit-space neighbour is found there by the same depths: that is the empirical depth of the coordinate change. The descendant also predicts a write's causal importance moderately (Spearman with the removal KL 0.33 to 0.64, saturating by 32 dimensions), which readability never did.
- Stable lineages (e267). A write's top compensators are the same set on disjoint halves of its tokens (Jaccard 0.53 to 0.76) and different for different writes (0.01 to 0.08), with no hub neurons: the thousands of successors are diffuse but reproducible per neuron.
- No universal builder (e266). The transition that builds the descendant-logit correspondence is attention in Pythia, OLMo and SmolLM2 and the MLP in GPT-2 and Qwen, located right after the write or at the very end.
- Why similar descendants are not interchangeable (e264). At tokens where two descendant-similar neurons are both active, their removal effects are independent (joint removal is additive) and uncorrelated, and one's vector injected at the other's tokens yields a footprint nearly orthogonal to the other's natural one. Descendant similarity is a population-level property of centroids, not a token-level identity of effects.

## Position relative to neighbouring work (reviewer-assisted, unverified citations omitted)

The nearest traditions are the Transformer Circuits view of the residual stream as an additive channel that components write to and read from; superposition and polysemanticity, which explain why neuron coordinates are poor feature coordinates; sparse autoencoders, transcoders and cross-layer transcoders, which learn overcomplete or cross-layer sparse bases; activation and path patching and causal tracing, which locate causally relevant components by intervention; causal abstraction, which separates representational variables from causal structure; and the representation-geometry literature on how population geometry changes with depth. What this program tracks is a different object from all of them: the identity of one native write, injected or removed as a perturbation, followed through depth. Against that background the claims that appear not to be established elsewhere are that a perturbation's identity survives after its neuron direction becomes unreadable, that its pairwise geometry is preserved while its sparse decomposability in the native basis collapses, that transporting the native dictionary does not yield a usable downstream basis, that downstream perturbation geometry predicts functional geometry better than birth geometry after controlling for it, and that functional similarity and causal substitutability are distinct. The cross-layer transcoder line is the closest neighbour and the natural comparison; the reviewer's literature pass found no prior work testing this exact object, which is a statement about that pass, not a novelty certification.

## Session 16: sufficiency and causal carriage

- Sufficient (e268). Predicting a token's logit footprint from its descendant is never improved by adding the write vector, at any depth in any model, and is worsened by it in the last blocks: the descendant is a sufficient statistic for the write's functional effect. Pairs that are far apart in write space but close in descendant space are correspondingly close in function. The dimensions the network spends on identity grow with depth (to 128 to 256) while function stays at 16 to 32 dimensions and behaviour at 4 to 32: identity bits exceed function bits exceed behaviour bits. The natural state's own geometry also becomes functional with depth, comparable to the descendant's in the gated models and below it in the GELU models.
- Causally carried (e269). Injecting a neuron's descendant centroid directly into the stream reproduces that neuron's natural logit effect at cosine 0.28 to 0.62, whereas injecting its write vector in a foreign context does not (−0.29 to +0.16); the effects of injected descendants follow descendant geometry (Spearman +0.38 to +0.76) more than write geometry. The descendant is the vector that carries the effect.
- Stable (e270). Descendant neighbourhoods agree across token halves (Jaccard 0.43 to 0.69, chance 0.11 to 0.15) and across corpora (0.50, 0.53), and moderately across Pythia checkpoints (0.40, 0.27), less than write-space neighbourhoods (0.77, 0.45): descendants keep changing late in training after the writes have settled.

## Session 17: parameterisation and the capacity ladder

- Continuous parameterisation (e271). Across all candidate pairs the distance between the logit effects of directly injected descendants tracks the distance between the descendants (Spearman +0.47 to +0.88, slope about one), and injecting a point on the segment between two descendants gives an effect equal to the linear interpolation of the endpoint effects (cosine 0.99 to 1.00). The effect of an intervention is a near-linear function of its position in descendant space.
- The ladder (e272). Reconstructing a descendant needs the full dimension; identifying its source needs 4 to 256 dimensions, growing with depth; predicting its function 8 to 64; its behavioural weight mostly 8 to 32. The descendant is not a reconstruction basis of anything; it is a compressed coordinate for what a write does and how much it matters.

## Session 18: no kernel, a shared core, and the massive neuron off the law

- No functional null space (e273, e273b). Displacing an injected descendant by a fixed amount changes its logit effect by the same relative amount along the top, middle and bottom principal components of the descendant cloud and along random directions (ratio 1.2 to 1.4 in four models); directions toward other descendants are only 15 to 30% more consequential. The descendant-to-function map has no kernel, so there are no functional equivalence classes, as a near-isometric mixing map followed by the unembedding predicts. GPT-2's one exception, a 2.6× response along its bottom principal components, is the massive-activation channel: near-constant across descendants, so a bottom component, yet the logits are highly sensitive to it.
- A shared core, not nesting (e274). The identity subspace (between-neuron scatter) and the function subspace (descendant-to-logit PLS) share half to three quarters of their 8-dimensional energy (50 to 100 times chance) and rotate apart beyond it (0.39 to 0.47 at 32 dimensions). The linear direction predicting the removal KL lies outside both in four models; since the KL is quadratic in the logit change, this is recorded, not read as a nesting verdict.
- The massive neuron is off the transport law (e275, e275b). With the coefficient sign restored (43 to 75% of dominant writes in the gated models are negative, a lesson in itself), the corpus-averaged linear transport of a write meets its natural descendant at cosine 0.07 to 0.47 at mid depth, and the fit is best for the neurons whose write direction survives most (Spearman −0.40 to −0.78 in 5/5). SmolLM2's massive neuron is anti-parallel to its transport at z +7.6 against a bulk spanning 0.77 to 0.98: a separate mechanism, not the tail of the same law. The other models' largest-coefficient neurons are not massive and sit inside the bulk.
- J-space, placed. J-space (the Jacobian from a layer to the final state, corpus-averaged, read through the unembedding, as recorded in the superweight study) indexes what the model can say; WDD indexes what it writes. The descendant is the un-averaged, per-write Jacobian image of a WDD atom between two intermediate levels; e248's transport operator is the J-space construction between intermediate levels; the descendant-to-logit map of e262 to e269 is J-space applied to a descendant; and e275b measures the gap between the averaged and the per-write object.

## Session 19: the reviewer's remaining questions, answered against the record

- Used or induced (e276). A natural descendant injected at the block-(L+1) input moves the logits no more than the descendant of a random vector transported the same way (ratio 0.94 to 1.18; KL 1.0 to 2.0) and no more than a covariance-matched random direction (0.65 to 1.14). The raw write direction at that level has random leverage. The descendant carries the write's specific effect (session 16) but its leverage is generic to the transport: the coordinate is induced by the downstream network, not something it is tuned to.
- Why the dictionary dies while provenance survives (e277). The consistent component of a per-token descendant (its energy along the class centroid) falls from 61 to 82% one block after birth to 16 to 33% at mid depth and 5 to 14% late, while identification stays at 89 to 100% at mid depth. The remainder is token-specific and not a linear function of the context (R² ≤ 0.10 at mid depth in four models). Classification needs only the small consistent component, distinct per neuron with angles preserved; a decomposition would have to explain the remainder, which no fixed atom indexes. That is the resolution of the paradox.
- The massive neuron, bounded (e278). Its write direction transports linearly and by the law at every amplitude from a tenth to ten times natural, through a tenfold shift of the operating point, and its natural descendant is anti-parallel to that transport at every amplitude. The anomaly is context-bound: a downstream reaction at the write's own token to its removal, developing between two blocks after birth and mid depth, absent for an added copy. Ordinary writes have context-free descendants. The reviewer's local-versus-global split is contradicted by the footprint's locality (e257); context-free versus context-bound is the split that holds.
- Drift, not transition (e279). The identity subspace rotates in the one to five blocks after birth and in the last block, and is stable between; identification collapses late without any rotation, by loss of coherent energy. The function subspace rotates in step. There is no mid-depth coordinate transition (SmolLM2 has one mild dip at block 10).
- The thirteen rows. Answered by the record: writes are identified; provenance survives; survival is not leakage; the descendant is predictable from the write by transplant (weakly by the averaged linear operator); it encodes function; the invariant is pairwise angle and energy, both decoder-free (e238, e210); the abstraction boundary is drift with two rotation zones (e279, e260); the dictionary dies for want of coherent energy (e277); the massive neuron is context-bound (e278). Open and not experiments of this kind: why training builds the coordinate (the record has the when: isometry at initialisation, dispersal and readability at warmup, late scrambling), the placement against superposition, SAEs and transcoders (e241 supplies the superposition numbers), and a workflow demonstration of the practical capability (zero-shot, data-free provenance, e246; effect prediction from descendants, e269 to e271).

## Session 20: the descendant as a dynamical state variable

- No snapping (e280). An interpolated descendant injected where neither endpoint is written follows the interpolation of the endpoint trajectories through every later level and to the logits (cosine 0.97 to 1.00, fitted position exact within 0.01). The network's own subsequent computation carries the continuous coordinate unchanged.
- The law composes in function (e281). The product of two shorter transport operators predicts the actual image, and the effect of injecting that prediction, as well as or better than a one-shot operator, in state and in function. The transport is a state-space dynamics, not a set of level-specific facts.
- The geometry is Euclidean (e282). Whitening the residual stream makes the transport less isometric at every level. The near-constant gain of random directions is concentration of measure over an operator whose singular values spread tenfold and more; actual writes vary in gain several times more than random directions. What the transport preserves on writes is angle, not gain. The general "find a metric" question is trivially solvable for a linear map and is not a test.
- The future follows from the present (e283). The descendant two blocks later is predicted from the present descendant through the data-free operator at cosine 0.43 to 0.78 (0.25 to 0.61 four blocks later), from the original write at 0.03 to 0.12, from a random vector at 0.00; the state adds at most 0.16; and the predicted future reproduces the future logit effect at 0.59 to 0.85. The descendant's future depends on its present, not on its origin.
- The reaction has a carrier (e284, e284b). Holding downstream component outputs at their unablated values at the massive neuron's own tokens, with the sanity check that holding everything returns the write itself, the MLP of block 10 alone flips SmolLM2's massive-neuron footprint from anti-parallel (−0.67) to aligned (+0.52) with its context-free transplant image; attention does nothing. Block 10 is also the one mid-depth rotation of SmolLM2's identity coordinates. Ordinary neurons' reactions are small and spread over blocks.
- The representation question, resolved as far as it can be. The network uses the descendant coordinate in the dynamical sense: it is what the next blocks act on and carry, its future and its effect follow from it and not from its origin, and it interpolates without snapping. It does not use it in the sense of being tuned to it (session 19): a random vector transported the same way has the same leverage. Both statements are now measured.

## Session 21: the theory, and where the program sits in the literature

- The theory (THEORY.md). One axiom, d_i(ℓ, t) = c_i(t) [J̄_C + ΔJ_t] ŵ_i + O(c²) with a generic, non-isometric, token-dependent Jacobian and a linear range up to about the natural amplitude, plus f = G d with G the un-averaged Jacobian lens, derives the program's results: homogeneity, additivity in the linear range, vector determination, sufficiency, causal carriage, interpolation without snapping, the absence of a functional null space, the crossover of functional alignment with depth, the state-variable and composition results, generic leverage, and the provenance-versus-decomposition asymmetry as an inequality in the coherent fraction (identification needs κ ≳ K / D_eff, reconstruction needs κ ≈ 1). The massive neuron enters as the one write whose token-specific Jacobian term is coherent at its own tokens, carried by one MLP. Not derived: the numbers, the reacting component, why training shapes J as it does.
- The theory's new predictions (e285, e285b). Gain follows the operator's spectrum (top > random > middle ≥ bottom, 5/5, no null direction), and the pair-cosine preservation of session 8 follows quantitatively from gain concentration (formula within 0.07 in 15/15): "isometric transport" in this record means angle preservation for random-component pairs. The norm-versus-count prediction for additivity could not be tested cleanly (cross-token confound) and is open; superposition of many writes is at least as linear as one, and amplitude is what breaks linearity.
- The literature (RELATED_WORK.md). The nearest published objects are the Jacobian lens and J-space (Transformer Circuits, July 2026: the corpus-averaged Jacobian to the final state, read through the unembedding), Activation Transport Operators (Szablewski, 2025: linear transport of SAE features between layers), the Jacobian eigendecompositions of Fernando and Guitchounts (2025, 2026: a learned spectral gradient and low-rank bottleneck), the near-linearity of consecutive residual states (Razzhigaev et al., 2024), self-repair (McGrath et al., 2023) and feature-specific error correction (Ferreira da Silva and Heimersheim, 2026), single-layer activation of multi-layer SAE latents (Lawson et al., 2025) and cross-layer transcoders, and massive activations (Sun et al., 2024; Vemula, 2026). What the program adds is the per-write, causal, cross-token-resolved transport of the model's own atoms, its functional sufficiency and state-variable behaviour, the coherent-fraction account of why provenance outlives decomposition, and a localised exception.

## Session 22: two rungs

- The compression test (e286). Decoding every downstream observable from the same per-token descendant at mid depth, through observable-specific subspaces and one decoder family: reconstruction needs 512 to 1024 directions, while the future descendant, the source identity, the logit footprint and the removal KL each need 8 to 32. The ladder the reviewer asked for has two rungs: a full-dimensional physical perturbation and a 8-to-32-dimensional causal description in which who wrote it, where it goes next, what it does and how much it matters are of similar size. That is the compact statement of the program: a write's downstream causal content is a low-dimensional coordinate that its descendant carries in full dimension. Two phrasings to keep exact: the descendant does not become unrelated to the write (it remains its Jacobian image); what falls with depth is the usefulness of the write coordinate as a description of the descendant, while the descendant's usefulness for predicting effects rises. And the low-rank causal read-out (measured) is not the same claim as a low-rank shared transport (a mechanism); e287 tests whether the token-specific part of the descendant is itself read.
- The theory document now separates the theorem-like object from the empirical discovery and tags each derived item by what it rests on.

## Session 23: the read-out lives in the shared term

- Shared versus token-specific (e287). The descendant's component in the span of the class centroids, 28 to 46% of its energy, reaches the full score on identity, function, behaviour and the future in 5/5; the token-specific remainder, 54 to 72% of the energy, carries no identity and only what a random projection of the same dimension carries, and adds nothing to the span. (Corrected in session 24: the adversarial controls show that any covariance-matched K-dimensional subspace decodes function, future and KL as well as the centroid span; the read-out lives in the high-variance, low-rank part of the descendant cloud, and only source identity is specific to the shared term.) The physical descendant is mostly token-specific computation that the read-out does not use for these questions; that is the mechanism behind the two rungs of session 22.
- The candidate abstract sentence, from the second reviewer and adopted: for these models and this perturbation family, a neuron write that begins as a sparse, source-specific WDD coordinate becomes a high-dimensional residual descendant whose downstream-relevant information is concentrated in a much smaller causal subspace; the descendant is a better state variable for its own future and its functional effect than the original write, even as the WDD dictionary ceases to provide a decomposition. In one line: the perturbation spreads physically, but its causal identity compresses.

## Session 24: the kill-tree

- What survived. The causal coordinate is manipulable one direction at a time: injecting along the k-th direction of the descendant-to-logit pairing moves the logits along its paired direction and along no other, in 40 of 40 cases (e289). Identity, function and future are read from a shared core (half to three quarters of a 16-dimensional subspace in common) and any one of them decodes the others (e288). The two rungs are learned: at initialisation the descendant is low-dimensional and fully predictive, dispersal makes it full-dimensional within the first four thousand steps, and the causal dimension then grows slowly from 2 to 4 directions to 8 to 32 (e291).
- What was corrected. The session-23 claim that the read-out lives in the shared term: a covariance-matched random subspace does as well on everything but identity, so the read-out lives in the low-rank, high-variance part of the cloud, of which the shared term is one basis (e288). The theory's massive-neuron prediction: adding the massive direction at its own tokens obeys the ordinary first-order law, and it is removal that is reversed; the response at those tokens is even in the deviation from the natural amount, a set point of the reacting MLP (e290). No sharp elbow in the compression curves; closure of the causal subspace is stationarity, since the residual transport is near the identity on it; the token-specific remainder is a weakly transported state of its own (e288, e286 curves).
- Where this leaves the object. A write's causal description at depth is a soft ten-to-thirty-dimensional coordinate inside the dominant directions of its descendant cloud, readable for four questions, steerable one direction at a time, approximately stationary across blocks, learned in the first four thousand steps, and violated at one write whose downstream reaction is second-order.

## Session 25: the four questions

- What the coordinate is. An 8-to-32-dimensional subspace inside the dominant directions of the descendant cloud, shared by identity, function and future, nested within a larger (about 128-dimensional) subspace that predicts the future descendant linearly, inside a full-dimensional physical descendant (e288, e294). Three scales, not two.
- How it moves. Over two blocks its future is a near-diagonal, evenly and mildly contracting, rotation-free linear function of its present (R² 0.89 to 0.96, gain 0.64 to 1.16, rotation ≤ 0.11); four to eleven percent of it is new per two blocks (e293).
- What it does when pushed. Each direction moves its paired logit direction and no other, three to four times above chance, but the paired span holds only a fifth to two fifths of the response and the linear map predicts the whole response at cosine 0.09 to 0.33 (e289, e292). Steerable, selectively; not a complete dictionary of effects.
- What the rest is. The leftover dimensions, half the descendant's energy, are carried by the near-identity transport and are, under intervention, a random vector as far as the logits, the KL, the later core and the other tokens can tell (e295). Carried, not used.
- The closing sentence. A neuron write becomes a physically full-dimensional descendant that carries, inside its dominant directions, a small coordinate which is read for identity, function and the future, transported almost unchanged from block to block, steerable direction by direction, learned in the first thousands of steps, and violated at one write whose downstream reaction is second-order; the remaining physical degrees of freedom are carried along and, at every resolution tested, unread.

## Session 26: the literature-derived program, run end to end

- Geometry. Under a causal metric fitted from the descendant-to-logit map the cloud is low-rank and its centroid geometry is the functional geometry (Spearman 0.52 to 0.82 against 0.35 to 0.65 Euclidean); each causal coordinate persists as itself across blocks (self-correlation 0.90 to 0.97); the physical dimension reaches its full scale within a few blocks of birth while the causal dimensions creep, as they do across training (e296, e297).
- Dynamics. The impulse response of a single injection is mostly local, with 15 to 41% of its energy at later tokens by mid depth; the propagation tensor is rank one only where a massive channel dominates; the core predicts attention routing weakly (e298, e299).
- Algebra. The coordinates are linear channels (linear R² ≥ 0.82 in 39 of 40, no saturation, no thresholds) that interact weakly and uniformly, with amplitude the only strong nonlinearity; the massive neuron's own-token response is even, every other response odd (e301, e302).
- Origin. Across hundreds of neurons the cores occupy a six-to-eight-dimensional effective space fixed by the downstream read-out, unrelated to the writes' mutual geometry, while a single write's core coordinate is largely computable from the write itself in the GELU models (e300, e303).
- The correction. Every perturbation family injected into the residual stream shows the same two-rung ladder (e304). What the descendant program characterised is the residual stream's treatment of any perturbation; WDD supplies the perturbations the model itself makes, and its writes are not privileged. That is the honest scope of the whole enterprise.

## Session 27: what P_ℓ is

- Not an operator subspace. The causal core has chance overlap with every spectral subspace of the data-free transport operator (top and bottom singular, update, slowest and fastest modes) and generic gain under it (e305).
- Not a sensitivity subspace. It has chance overlap with the read-out's high-gain directions estimated forward and with the true backward gradient subspace; forward transport modes and backward sensitivity modes are nearly orthogonal, and the decodable information sits in the forward core (e305, e308). The first-order identity f = G d holds with the true Jacobian at per-token cosine 0.58 to 0.67.
- Persistent channels. In a fixed basis the sixteen coordinates keep their identities at every block of every model but one (unit gain, 2 to 6% mixing, no births or deaths outside the massive channel) (e307).
- A shared coordinate system that emerges with depth. The function cores of physically unrelated perturbation families overlap at chance two blocks after injection, partly at mid depth, and at the within-family reliability near the output; identity cores stay family-specific (e306).
- The answer. P_ℓ is the dominant-variance subspace of the model's own perturbation cloud, transported almost unchanged as persistent channels, converging across families with depth, and read by a read-out that is nearly isotropic in gain. The residual stream does not funnel perturbations into an operator's subspace; its own perturbations live in a low-dimensional region, and function is read from where they are. The low-dimensional causal coordinate is the geometry of the perturbations, not of the operators.

## Session 28: the quotient picture, and the end of exploration

- The funnel is generic. Perturbations orthogonal to the model's natural residual manifold, heavy-tailed, sparse, low-rank or shuffled, all acquire the same 8-to-32-dimensional functional coordinate as the model's own writes; proximity to the manifold buys coherence of transport, not the funnel (e310). The cloud's geometry is its own, curved (locally about 25-dimensional, globally a hundred), and not the residual variance (e309).
- The quotient. Physical geometry comes to match functional geometry with depth (Spearman 0.06 to 0.17 rising to 0.37 to 0.66); functional distances are transported unchanged through the middle and mildly consolidated at the end; functional equivalence is a many-to-one map nearly independent of physical proximity and of family (e311). The converged coordinate system and its metric transfer across perturbation distributions near the output, partly at mid depth; identity never transfers; superposition of functionally equivalent, physically unrelated perturbations is linear (e312).
- What the object is now. A transformer's residual stream quotients physical perturbations into a low-dimensional functional state that is transported almost unchanged, shared across perturbation distributions near the output, curved in the physical space, unrelated to the operators' spectra, and read by a nearly isotropic read-out. WDD supplies the model's own perturbations as the cleanest probes of this, and they are not privileged. By the reviewer's criterion the exploratory phase ends here.

## Session 30: the external-researcher test, and the answer to "are we done"

## Session 31: the quotient program (40 scripts, 191 runs)

- What the quotient is. One causal information content for every observable and every perturbation family (the coordinates that carry it are not unique: any high-variance projection of the cloud will do), of dimension 8 to 32 set by the map, homogeneous and additive in its coordinate, linear along its directions to several times the natural amplitude, read out nonlinearly by the later MLPs, transported along the identity path with the MLPs as dampers and attention as the sole cross-token route, persistent as a subspace across depth, context-general, two thirds implicit in the state and the source, born by step 4000 of training, and collapsing rather than expanding beyond the linear range (e317 to e356).
- What it is not, which changes the wording. A quotient fitted to shuffled targets scores the same as the real one; PCA, PLS, CCA and kernel PCA coincide; the discarded 64-dimensional complement decodes everything the core does; there is no null space, no coset structure and no fibre structure; the core component cancels nothing at the logits; and the WDD atoms are neither a sparse, a concentrated nor a semantic basis of it, a block's functional coordinate living in the small-coefficient tail of its ledger. The right sentence is therefore: the descendant cloud of any perturbation is a low-rank object whose every moderate-dimensional high-variance projection carries the same causal information redundantly. The transformer's response to perturbations is compressible, not structured into a privileged subspace or classes.
- WDD as the foundation. Tested and not found: the atoms are ordinary members of the perturbation cloud (e332, e334, e342), and the sparse ledger is not where a block's functional coordinate lives (e333). WDD remains the birth coordinate and the instrument.
- Records: e01 to e356, 290 experiment scripts plus helpers, 1,458 result files, hypotheses H0 to H152. ## Session 32: phase 2 on an A100, circuits, co-selection and drift (20 scripts, about 250 runs)

- Anchor and scope. The LessWrong post "What if not Circuits?" asked whether computation lives in circuits, in co-selected coherent structures, or in something that drifts; its comments added redundancy versus superposition, two kinds of drift, neuron turnover, speciation and developmental stages. Phase 2 tested the parts of that agenda that the WDD program's tools can reach on the same five models, with the post as an anchor rather than a checklist.
- The instruments pass their positive controls, and the old negatives are narrowed. Pairwise ablation interaction and joint non-additivity find the induction circuit and the IOI circuit in all five models (GPT-2's IOI top twelve contain ten published heads; induction top-8 non-additivity 2 to 25 times random). The same statistics barely see these circuits in natural-text population averages, which is how the earlier sessions measured units (e264, e302); scoring at the positions a circuit serves recovers part of the signal (e366). The earlier negatives are true of population averages and are not evidence against circuits.
- Co-selection, the post's own proposal, is a weak detector at the trained point and a better one during development. At the final checkpoint, batch-level co-selection of the induction heads is strong in two models and weak in three, and its structure is mostly layer structure; token-level co-selection is stronger (it finds the circuit in three of five); across Pythia training, co-selection of the induction circuit appears when the circuit forms, peaks while it is refined (steps 3000 to 16000), and fades by the end, while the circuit's use and redundancy keep growing; OLMo repeats the fade at batch level (z 5.9 to 1.5) but not cleanly at token level. The reason is structural: at an optimum every unit's mean selection vanishes, so first-order selection measures local mis-scaling, not use (e370b).
- Circuits are a large-move property. Ablation interactions exceed the local curvature 2 to 50 times and are weakly ranked by it; the loss along a top head's scale is flat near full strength and rises near zero (a top head halved costs a median 4-30% of its full ablation, a random head 24-57%), so the heads that matter are robust to partial removal and fail only near full removal, and a quadratic model at the trained point captures a median 3-65% of their ablation effects (e371, e372). Anything computed at the trained point without leaving it (gradients, co-selection, curvature, Fisher) underpredicts circuit structure. This is the same lesson as the descendant program's O(c^2) caveat, now measured.
- Drift has a direction. In Pythia a neuron's implementation (write vector) settles before its function (reliability-corrected logit signature), which keeps moving as the downstream network settles; OLMo's implementation keeps changing through most of its 3T-token run. Early-layer neurons' logit effects become strongly context-dependent over training (sample-to-sample agreement 0.93 to 0.28), late-layer ones do not. Important neurons turn over late (a third or more of the final top 5% enter in the last three quarters of training); clusters split and merge equally (no speciation at this resolution); co-selection structure is re-formed throughout training rather than carried.
- The quotient, re-examined. Its session-31 properties hold at four birth depths in all five models (e361), so they are not a block-2 artefact. In development it is born with the induction transition (dimension 2 to 8, logit gain over random directions peaking at 1.9 times between steps 512 and 1000), the induction circuit supplying about a third of the descendant cloud's spread right after the transition and none by the end (e373), and grows to 16 to 32 dimensions; it does not persist through training even in function space (its logit image is no closer to the final quotient than to a random direction's image), and its decodability and linearity fall as the model learns. The functional coordinate is a property of each trained map, not a conserved object.
- What this says about the post's question. Circuits are real, redundant, task-conditional and non-quadratic; natural-text averages hide them, and first-order, point-local measures like co-selection see them mainly while they are being built. The structures that carry function (units' functions, co-selection communities, the functional coordinate) are re-formed through training rather than carried, and function lags implementation. What the program can defend is a two-level picture: stable, ablation-visible circuits for specific tasks, and a drifting, context-dependent, low-rank functional coordinate for everything else.
- Records: e357 to e373 (21 scripts on the box plus helpers, about 255 runs, results in results/ with FINDINGS_phase2.log), hypotheses H153 to H169, THEORY section 3i, RELATED_WORK section 11.

## Session 33: phase 3, readers, readouts and toys (9 scripts, about 70 runs, 13 toy transformers)

- The brief. After re-reading the original prompt, one-to-five-minute experiments aimed at mechanisms and at the program's own conclusions rather than more models or checkpoints; the first training in the program (tiny toys, allowed by the prompt) to manufacture phenomena instead of only observing them.
- WDD as a circuit instrument. Reading WDD's state-only code through a reader's input map recovers the previous-token to induction edge in all five models (rank 1 or 2 in all 20 induction heads) and the S-inhibition to name-mover edge in GPT-2, with no intervention; as a general edge finder it is above chance but unreliable. This is the most direct evidence so far that WDD is useful for mechanistic work: provenance plus the reader's metric gives circuit edges.
- The loss is a biased lens on interactions. Removal knees are made mostly by the softmax readout; the loss's convexity makes nearly all pairs look like backups when logit effects add; a quarter to a half of pair signs flip on a linear readout; and phase 2's "induction circuits keep adding redundancy" is a loss-readout effect (sub-additive in logit space at every checkpoint). Interaction structure should be read on logits.
- Manufactured phenomena. In toys, redundancy appears only with training noise (head dropout); selection vanishes at convergence while use stays large; co-selection marks a circuit under construction. These are phase 2's observational claims, now reproduced under manipulation (except redundancy growth, which the readout correction removes).
- Killed along the way: the degenerate-direction reading of the knee (the loss is quadratic near full strength), near-twin and composition geometry as predictors of backup and series (on the loss), and "implementation settles first" (Pythia's lower and upper halves co-adapt to the end; stitching late lower parts costs more than the whole late gain).
- Records: e374 to e382, hypotheses H170 to H180, THEORY section 3j, corrections to H169 and to the reading of e357 and e365 in KILLED.

## Session 34: the readout audit and the literature check (4 scripts, 20 runs)

- Nothing needs redoing wholesale. Of about 340 experiments, seven measured interactions on a convex readout (six on the loss, one on KL); all seven are now re-read. The rest work in state, coordinate or logit-change space and are unaffected.
- What changed: redundancy claims shrink several-fold (the loss inflates joint over sum 1.4-5.3x; on the centred logit the induction circuit is additive or sub-additive in four models and redundant only in Pythia, 1.72); e264's KL interactions were Fisher overlaps (its additivity survives in logit space, its control-pair redundancy is retracted); the conditional natural-text result survives.
- WDD and computation: readers draw 1.33-1.46 times more of their input from writes WDD can identify than size predicts, in every model and size band: an aggregate link between the prominence law and use. The reader-weighted edge finding is a validation of WDD's provenance, not a new capability; the exact split it approximates is standard (Franco and Crovella 2025).
- Literature: selection vanishing at convergence is 1989-1990 pruning lore; self-repair without dropout is documented in real models, so the toy dropout result is a toy statement; the measured loss-versus-logit sign flips, cross-checkpoint block swaps in a language model, co-selection of known circuits across checkpoints and the readout-versus-trajectory split of the knee were not found in prior work.

## Session 35: a fresh-slate pass (4 scripts, 22 runs)

- Three questions an outsider would ask, answered. Is the reader preference for identifiable writes deep? Mostly geometry (present at initialisation); a genuine part only in GPT-2 and OLMo. Is WDD's sparse code functionally faithful? Yes: 64 atoms keep 84-99% of the loss, and at 8-32 atoms the model's own MLP write rows keep far more than a rotated copy of the dictionary, PCA, embeddings or head bases. Is that provenance? Yes: only rows that nearly equal the ones that wrote the state do it.
- The reading side is diffuse (half of an input from 8-14 components) and a 64-atom WDD reading sees about half of it; reading concentrates before Pythia's induction transition and spreads after.
- What this adds to the story: WDD earns a place as a non-learned sparse code whose value is provenance, measured functionally; its limits are on the reading side, where computation is less sparse than writing.

## Session 36: what WDD's code is a code of (4 scripts, 20 runs)

- The code is not a record of the largest writes. Keeping the 64 largest actual writes recovers 0.53-0.79 of the loss, and 1024 of them 0.75-0.92. WDD's 64 own words recover 0.91-0.99, and most of its MLP atoms are not among the top writers. The model writes densely and can be read back sparsely in its own words.
- Limits: replacing every layer at once compounds the errors (+0.43 to +3.77 nats at 128 atoms per layer), and the atoms are not sparse causal nodes (effects spread over 13-16 of 32, linear attribution weak).

## Session 37: self-description length and private languages (5 scripts, 13 runs)

- The weights as a vocabulary. At the middle depth, 90% of a model's function is described by 32-64 of its own write directions. The same description needs thousands of its actual writes, so the writing is more than 64 times redundant with respect to its shortest own-word description at the end of Pythia's training.
- Training creates self-describability. At initialisation the own vocabulary is exactly as good as a random rotation of itself. By step 256 it is better, and the advantage peaks around the induction transition and persists. The description length stays at 32-64 words while the writing becomes denser.
- Within one run the vocabulary grows by accretion and converges. Late words read early states; from step 33000 the vocabularies are interchangeable. One stage, step 4000, has false friends: words aligned with the later model's high-variance directions that, at 4-8 words, leave its function worse than the mean state. Their random rotation is harmless.
- Across individuals the language is mostly private. The shared tokens do not translate it, and a state-fitted translation carries a third to a half of the own-word advantage. What two individuals share linearly is where the function is: random directions confined to it describe a network as well as its own words, a caution for linear feature transfer.

## Session 38: an external review's controls (8 scripts, 26 runs)

- The review's strongest challenge was that the own words might be good only because they sit where the states vary. It is right about early training: up to about step 8000 the advantage over a rotation is mostly the vocabulary's second moment and span, an accent. Chosen by function, only 0.04 of it remains at step 1000.
- It is wrong about late training. From step 16000 vocabularies with the same second moment or span sit at the rotation level. The advantage is carried by the individual words, survives a function-aware pursuit (0.24 at the end), holds in OLMo, and is absent at initialisation in all five architectures.
- The transition coincides with a few huge, locally insensitive directions taking over the state's variance. These directions also mediate the false friends: kept exactly, the step-4000 words become partial friends. The false friends do not replicate in OLMo, which has no misleading stage on its checkpoint grid although its late states have huge directions too. OLMo's final vocabulary also reads early states worse than their own words, so accretion is not universal.
- The own words are not the best sparse vocabulary. Random words drawn from the states' covariance, and a learned SAE at small k, do better. The own words are free, created by training, overtake the SAE at k = 64, and keep more function per unit of variance explained.

## Session 39: round 2, the held-back tests and fresh angles (8 scripts, 23 runs)

- The review's decomposition resolves cleanly. Atom-specific structure belongs to the writers alone. The readers only ever have an accent. The accent-to-vocabulary transition holds at 70m, 160m and 410m.
- False friends are precursors: MLP rows on their way to writing the huge directions (from step 4000, formed by 16000). They mislead only in Pythia-410m, whose huge directions are the largest, and appear after the cross-seed divergence phase, not during it.
- The weights alone know that the huge directions carry almost no local function. A pursuit metric built from them recovers much of the Fisher gain in GPT-2 and mid-training Pythia, less at Pythia's end. Weight-only selections of words fail: they pick the embedding table.
- Fresh angles:
  - The network's errors have a native language that changes hands during training, from the writers early to the readers later.
  - The vocabulary covers in-context copying on unseen sequences as well as corpus prediction.
  - Its word usage is Zipf-like, with the most used words aimed at the huge directions.

## Session 40: the workspace agenda on a 7B model (6 scripts, 9 runs)

- WorkspaceBench asks for readers of a model's intermediate variables that neither miss nor invent them. Read as sixteen of the model's own words, a state's parts can be read one by one.
- At the subject token of a two-hop question, in middle layers, those words surface the hidden bridge entity where the whole-state logit lens essentially never does (0.11-0.21 against 0.00-0.01 in the top 20). Rotated words and principal parts never do.
- At the final position the bridge appears only late, together with the answer, and the plain lens reads it at least as well. There the native words hold bridge and answer in separate words, and removing the bridge word costs the answer about ten times more than removing another word.
- Role resolution is late for every reader. The native words show it as suppression of the other name.
- The arithmetic test was beyond the model.
- At 7B, self-describability holds and is larger (0.79 against 0.26 at 16 words).
- The huge directions reach an extreme: 97% of the variance, 0.3% of the local Fisher trace. Yet kept exact alone they recover 29% of the loss, so local functional metrics mislead at this scale.

## Session 42: what WDD unlocks, on five models

- Is WDD novel? As an instrument, yes. Its findings mostly coincide with known phenomena, which is what a sound new instrument should show. The question is what it measures or reads that others cannot.
- Tested as a reader of hidden content, it is not a general improvement:
  - it leads for recalled entities in OLMo-1B and Qwen2.5-7B (even against centred actual writes);
  - it loses to the value-vector reading in Pythia and to the plain lens in GPT-2;
  - like every reader that goes through the output vocabulary, it cannot see context features.
- Tested as a measurement of the model's own structure, the self-description layer holds in all five architectures:
  - absent at initialisation;
  - word-level at the end of training;
  - Zipf-like word usage;
  - the variance concentrated in directions the readers do not weigh above chance;
  - states in the writers' words and errors in the readers' in three of five.
- That is the foundation: the native vocabulary as an instrument for a property of trained networks (how their own weights describe their computation) that was not measured before.

## Session 43: the instrument, extended (6 scripts, 28 runs)

- Self-description tracks model quality. Across Pythia sizes the advantage of a model's own words over rotated ones rises from 0.17 (70m) to 0.40 (1b) as loss falls. Larger models have most of it within the first thousand steps.
- The neurons a model most uses to describe itself are the ones it can least lose, in three of five models, beating first-order Taylor importance and every other gradient-free score there. It fails where a few norm-dominant neurons (super weights, massive-activation writers) carry the damage.
- With the attention readers included, states speak the writers' language and errors the readers' language at every depth of all five models.
- Two fresh angles failed:
  - self-description does not flag unfamiliar or badly predicted input;
  - instruction tuning leaves the native vocabulary unchanged (row cosine 0.997) and fully intelligible, so fine-tuning differences are below the vocabulary's resolution.

## Session 44: a survey for unconnected results, and the bridges (16 scripts, 73 runs)

- The survey. Five agents read all 400+ experiments in groups of ten and listed the results never connected to WDD. About 40% of the probability mass pointed at one hub, the huge directions M: phase 1's sinks, phase 2's set point and knee, phase 3's false friends and Fisher lightness. The rest pointed at where the vocabulary lives (lexical or contextual, increments or states, own path or broadcast), at whether its privilege is statistical, at development, and at applications. The map is in `survey/uncharted_map.md`.
- Three corrections.
  - Pythia's "86% of the variance in eight directions" was one sink token per sequence ("\n\n\n", 41 times the median norm), and OLMo's 48% one outlier. Phase 1 excluded such positions and phase 3 did not. At ordinary positions M holds 10-26% in all five models, in the same directions.
  - The false friends were those sinks mis-described. With the sinks kept exact, the step-4000 words beat their rotation.
  - At 7B, "97% of the variance" was one token in the fitting text (" series", 221 times the median norm). Without it M holds 11%, and kept exact alone it recovers 29% of the loss.
  - Self-description itself is unchanged by all of this. The replacement model's compounding is real (not a sink effect).
- What M is.
  - An early-written, identity-heavy channel: the most lexical subspace, 65-91% predictable from the state after block 0, and position-bearing in GPT-2.
  - Downstream it is carried rather than repaired, and later attention reads it (removing it moves attention 1.75-3.1 times as much as a random move of the same size).
  - Removing it costs the average per unit of variance, 2-4 times its local quadratic prediction. A random move of the same size stays quadratic.
  - It is not a set point, not normalisation ballast and not a temperature knob. The superquadratic cost has no single seat: attention carries a third to two thirds of it, and in gated models the MLPs absorb large moves.
- The native vocabulary under four new deflationary controls.
  - It is not the lexicon. Dropping the token rows, position rows and block-0 MLP rows leaves the advantage over rotation intact at every checkpoint, and descriptions almost never name the current token.
  - It is not a union of per-block second-order accents at the end of training (at most 0.33 of the gap). Per-block mixtures of words, a span control, carry more (0.12-0.52; about half in SmolLM2), so the words themselves carry the rest.
  - It is not a property of the states' covariance (Gaussian states with the same covariance keep 15-45%).
  - It also exists for what context adds to a token (word-level in all five).
  - It holds on both the self and the broadcast path.
- Development.
  - Early self-description is a per-block accent: 0.82 of the gap at step 1000. The words appear between steps 4000 and 16000.
  - They appear first in the middle block's own increment (word-level at step 4000, while the state is still an accent), then in the state.
  - The actual writes grow sparser from step 1000 to 16000 and denser again by the end, which reconciles phase 1's "sparser" with phase 3's "denser".
- Two analogies lost their content. Zipf-like usage is geometry (Gaussian states reproduce it), and the network does not keep its own words preferentially (retention follows variance).

## Session 45: a decade of WDD, backcast (e444-e450; the vision is in `VISION.md`)

- The user asked what the world would look like had WDD existed for ten years, and how to get there. Each imagined capability rests on an assumption that none of the 400+ earlier experiments tested; each got the cheapest decisive test. For the first time, self-description was measured in models trained from scratch under manipulations (earlier toys measured other things).
- The vocabulary is written, not spoken. Writer rows frozen at their random initialisation are barely used as words, both in grokking (modular addition) and in a small sequence model with sparse latent topics. Trained writer rows are used. When some writers are frozen, the description moves to the ones that train. A grokking network with frozen writers still generalises, with little vocabulary.
- Native words carry meaning.
  - One MLP write row serves as a language-independent concept word for 15 of 24 concrete nouns in Qwen2.5-0.5B, 22 of 24 in Qwen2.5-7B (twelve of them in block 7) and 7 in SmolLM2. Each is used at the noun in nearly all of 20 sentences across four languages and almost never elsewhere; rotated words give none.
  - Whole sentences are described with the same native words in four languages.
  - Removing a concept word costs more than a matched other word.
  - The concept words' neurons do fire on the concept. In Qwen they write about half of the direction, so the method rediscovers multilingual neurons without labels. In SmolLM2 they write about a tenth, and the word names a channel that many components write.
  - Token-level proxies of meaning cannot show any of this: they reward lexical units, and rotated words score highest on them.
- Generalisation is visible in the vocabulary. Generalising grokking networks become sparse in their own words; memorising ones only in a second-order sense. The word-level part led test accuracy by 500-750 steps in both standard runs, but not in the frozen variants.
- Word-level interoperability is refuted: models' words do not translate one to one (0.06-0.13 of function), while dense maps do (0.85-0.91).
- Interpretable by design needs a functional objective. A Euclidean self-description term makes GPT-2's states much sparser in its own words (fraction unexplained down 41% for 0.28 nats, 80% for 0.91), but the words do not move and function is not better described.
- The model's own vocabulary is a usable but not a winning codec. It beats PCA at 512 bits per position in all five models (256 in four), but loses at 128 bits and below, where each word's 17-18-bit index costs too much.

## Session 46: causal tests of concept words, and a re-implemented block (e451-e455, e452b)

- An external review, relayed by the user, listed what it thought was still open. It had read the README as pushed before this session, so two of its items were already done: writer and reader freezing (session 45) and a public SAE at matched sparsity (e403). Its other two, and one question from the program, were tested in about 30 minutes of GPU time.
- Concept words are causal handles, not correlated provenance.
  - Swapped at every block up to the middle at the noun, one MLP write row redirects 67-94% of a model's translations of the noun to the swapped-in noun, English copies included. In the Qwen models it moves 39-78% of category answers to the target's category. An equal-size random direction moves 0-3% of translations.
  - Corrected in session 47 (e458): those swaps accumulated across blocks to 6-8 times the target word's natural size. Near natural size (0.7 times) a concept word moves 35-58% of translations; the contrast with random stands.
  - At a single depth the effects are specific (3-13 times the controls) but change answers only in SmolLM2, because one word is outvoted by the other directions that carry the noun at the same position (corrected in session 52: whole-state patching there at one layer switches 78-99%).
  - Removing the word alone rarely breaks the Qwen models: it is the handle, not the only carrier.
- The function does not determine the vocabulary.
  - A middle MLP block retrained from scratch to reproduce its own input-output map keeps the model's loss within 0.02 nats, with different rows (median best |cos| 0.26-0.28 with the originals; two seeds 0.22-0.25 with each other).
  - Its rows are words, about half as good as the original's (54-61% of the advantage over rotation).
  - With the writer rows frozen at random the block still reproduces the function (+0.03 to +0.05 nats), but its rows are not words at all.
  - Most of the remaining gap is the quality of the fit, not the end-to-end objective. More distillation raises the rows to 0.71-0.77 of the original's advantage. Training the block on the model's own next-token loss adapts it to the text but makes its rows no more word-like (0.52-0.70). Only an exact fit would pin the rows (MLP identifiability up to permutation and scale); these fits keep the loss within 0.02 nats.
- Concept words correspond across sizes only at the concept level. Mapped through a dense map from Qwen2.5-0.5B to 7B, a concept word is most aligned with the same noun's 7B concept word in 11 of 14 cases (chance 1 in 14), but at |cos| 0.08, and it is the nearest 7B atom for only 1 of 14.

## Session 47: WDD as a forensic instrument, four quick tests (e456-e459)

- A second relayed review proposed twelve forensic uses of WDD. Eight were already answered by the atlas or were not quick (the list is in FINDINGS); four were run in about 10 minutes of GPU time.
- Self-description does not detect compression damage. Quantised to 4 bits, GPT-2 loses 3.9 nats and keeps 96% of its words' advantage over rotation; own words stay far below rotation in unexplained variance at every level of quantisation and pruning. The measure says the states are built from the model's own rows, which stays true when the function is broken.
- WDD can name what a fine-tune changed, but it does not compress the change.
  - Instruct tuning moves chat states 2-3 times as much as natural text.
  - A few words carry much of the usage change. Qwen's block-10 rows 3276 and 1521 go from under 1% to 12-13% of chat tokens.
  - The chat change itself is low-rank: its own principal directions describe it far better than native words (0.27-0.32 against 0.57-0.65 unexplained).
- The e455 correction.
  - Adding the target concept word at every block accumulates; e455 injected 6-8 times its natural size.
  - Near natural size one word moves 35-58% of translations. The dense difference of mean states moves 82-100% with 4-6 times the displacement.
  - Per unit of displacement the native word is about as efficient (Qwen-0.5B) or more efficient (Qwen-7B).
  - A WDD checksum downstream confirms the intended word was moved and predicts success (AUC 0.70 and 0.92), but no better than the intervention's size.
- Native words persist across generated and natural text modestly more than rotated words (1.3-1.8 times at lags of 4-32 in Qwen), with no regeneration beyond their usage rate.

## Session 48: authorship labels as forensics, two clean nulls (e460-e461)

- A third relayed review proposed using WDD's authorship labels for "weird" forensics. Several proposals assumed that a state's write history, or the order of its writes, could affect the future. It cannot: the forward pass depends only on the current states of all positions.
- Near-identical states (cosine 0.95-0.998, always the same token) do have different futures, but 76-85% of the difference is context, not the vector. The WDD ledger difference does not predict the vector's effect beyond the cosine (e460).
- A forged write (a token's largest neuron write swapped for a random vector of the same norm) is barely detectable at its block (AUC 0.60-0.64, no better than a covariance detector) and invisible a few blocks later (e461).

## Session 49: the learning signal in native coordinates (e462)

- A fourth relayed review asked what learning looks like in the model's own words. Most of its proposals were already answered (errors are described in the readers' words, e412 and e429; the fate of cancelled writes, e221-e225) or follow from the chain rule.
- One batch's gradient on a word is unrelated to the word: its share along the row is 0.5-0.8 times chance, its sign is a coin flip, and it does not predict the row's net change between checkpoints (cosine 0.000).
- Over Pythia's training, words are formed by rotation while growing (steps 1000-16000, when the words appear, e443). After that they shrink in place.
- The words the model uses most receive no more gradient than others, and early in training less (Spearman -0.63 to -0.04). WDD provenance is a forward coordinate, not a learning coordinate.

## Session 50: no grammar over the native words (e463-e464)

- A sixth relayed review asked whether the native vocabulary has a grammar: inflection (the same word's coefficient changing with its role) or syntax (the network correcting words written in illegal contexts).
- Neither. A word's noun or verb role is carried by which native words describe it (decoded at 0.94-0.97), not by the coefficients of the words both roles share (0.61, chance 0.5; no sign flips). Rotated words decode the role as well (e463).
- A native word written where it never fires is damped and scattered exactly like the same word where it does fire (survival after 2 blocks 0.60 against 0.64 in GPT-2, 0.48 against 0.47 in Qwen) (e464).
- The vocabulary behaves like fixed labels with context-dependent amounts, not like an internal language.

## Session 51: a description is not a self-sufficient state (e465)

- A seventh relayed review asked whether WDD can judge its own reliability. Most of its proposals repeat phase 1's certificates (e180, e195, e200), the prominence law, aliasing, the rotated-dictionary control (e388, e395) or e388's splice.
- The new test: after a state is replaced by its 16-word native description, the network does not regrow what the description left out. The divergence from the natural run stays at 0.67-0.69 of the growing state four blocks later, while a random error of the same size is damped to 0.49 and costs almost no loss.
- What the description omits is functional content that later blocks cannot re-derive from the words.

## Session 52: native words as interchange coordinates, and a large Kalman gap (e466-e469)

- An eighth relayed review framed WDD in systems-theory and causal-abstraction terms.
- Interchange interventions (e469) give the strongest positive result for native words since the concept words. Setting a few native words of the base state to the source's values at the last noun token, at every block up to the middle, carries the noun across contexts:
  - one word per block switches 67% of Qwen's answers, and four switch 96%;
  - against 0-36% for rotated words and 8-66% for the task's own principal directions of the difference;
  - SmolLM2 shows the same order at lower levels.
  At a single block the principal directions win. Patching the whole state at that one position and block switches 78-99%, which corrects session 46's explanation of weak single-depth effects: the noun had not left the position.
- The Kalman gap is large (e466). Sixty-four extra directions of what a 16-word description omits still leave 7-11% of the loss unrecovered.
- What it omits is the same kind of information it keeps (e467): token identity, the neighbouring tokens and position sit in both halves.
- Later tokens depend very little on one position's middle-depth state (e468).

## Session 53: native words are handles while a variable is being written (e470)

- Where e469's native-word interchange works:
  - Whole-state patching at the noun works at a single block from early on (Qwen from block 2, SmolLM2 from blocks 4-6).
  - A few native words work only across a band of blocks in which the noun is written: Qwen blocks 0-6, SmolLM2 about 6-12. Four words over Qwen's blocks 0-4 already switch 85% of answers.
  - Starting after the band fails (0.15 from block 8), although the whole state still moves there.
- The carrier words turn over: consecutive blocks share 41-47% of them, and a third to a half are written by the last two blocks.
- A PCA basis shared across blocks does as well as per-block bases at 16 dimensions, so the variable's direction is largely shared across depth. Native words lead at small k in both models.

## Session 54: becoming a word and becoming important are separate processes (e471)

- A tenth relayed review proposed a "life cycle" of native words: causal handle, then word, then readable.
- Across eight Pythia-410m checkpoints, a neuron's usage as a native word and its importance (the loss change when it is zeroed) evolve independently. Neither leads the other (lagged Spearman -0.01 and +0.06), they do not co-move (+0.04), and words that fall out of use keep their importance.
- Single-neuron importance is tiny and noisy, so a small effect could be hidden.
- The number of distinct words in use peaks at step 2000 and falls from 14,115 to 9,435 by the end.

## Session 58: WDD calibrated against theory older than OMP (e483-e492)

- The instrument's threshold is an extreme-value law. The largest competitor correlation is a Gumbel variable scaling as sqrt(2 ln m) in all five models, and its two fitted parameters predict the detection curve of an injected write within 0.1; the resolution is 0.25-0.4 of the state's norm (e483). For a rotated dictionary the law's scale is exactly the atoms' second moment; for the native dictionary it is 2.9-6.2 times that, and this provenance factor is 6-14 at block 1, falls with depth to 3.5-5 in four models, and is already 2.2-3 at Pythia step 1000 (e492).
- The description is a non-redundant code with correction: every word matters (a random half refitted reaches 0.59-0.80 of OMP's own eight), yet refitting native words after erasure recovers 0.02-0.09 of loss recovered where refitting rotated words costs (e484); the supports are equally near-orthogonal for both dictionaries, so the correction is in what the words fit, not in their geometry (e490).
- The first native word carries 0.4-1.2 bits about the next token (rotated 0.2-0.3, the current token 0.9-1.4) and 1.6-4.4 bits about the current token: lexical first (e485). The word stream has sequential structure of its own, 0.2-0.9 bits from the previous word, half of the text's; the rotated stream has almost no entropy (e487). The Heaps exponent is the same with and without provenance; provenance concentrates usage on 2-5% of the words used (e486).
- The rows are the heavy-tailed directions of the state cloud (kurtosis 2-10 times rotated at the 90th percentile; usage follows it, e488), and FastICA on the states alone lands on them at 2-3 times the rotated rows' match and 1.7-2 times the principal directions', in part: its components are built from several rows (e489). Provenance loss has one timescale, a per-block retention of 0.71-0.88 that is the contraction's (e491).

## Session 59: the provenance factor is write sparsity on an accent; SAE features are written by the rows (e493-e494)

## Session 60: the SAE bridge is depth-independent and partly active; the accent forms by step 512, the words from step 1000 (e495-e496, e492-e493)

## Session 61: features are state-like, not write-like, in the native dictionary (e497)

## Session 62: the two clocks are two quantities; the ledger adds nothing over the top row (e498, e496 v3)

Attention's share of the state rises from 0.06 to 0.75 at block 12 over training while the MLP writes' share falls.

## Session 72: the boundary is the floor, and it predicts (e516, e516b)

- Ranked at a checkpoint by its largest projection over the calibrated Gumbel floor, a non-word row's chance of being a word at the next checkpoint is read at AUC 0.76 (from step 1000) to 0.97 (from 16000), matching or beating its own current usage and far ahead of magnitude, selectivity and kurtosis (0.5-0.7); nothing fitted, nothing from the future used. The projection that crosses carries the chord, since a row's own write almost never clears the floor; the own write governs staying (0.90-0.99). The extreme-value criterion is the selection mechanism, not a retrospective description.

## Session 71: the turnover is generic churn under a moving boundary (e515, e515b)

- The vocabulary is replaced in identity: 0.04-0.05 of the step-1000 words are words at the end, 0.70-0.73 of the end's words are new after step 4000, and the last pair of checkpoints still exchanges a quarter of the set. The rate of the turnover is the rate at which the 256 largest rows churn (retention within 0.15 at every pair), so it is not an event of the vocabulary's own. The boundary, though, moves: through steps 256-16000 the rows entering the word set are more selective and larger than the rows leaving it (AUC 0.66-0.76 and 0.76-0.95), the hysteresis of a reorganisation, and the word set never overlaps the largest rows by more than 0.27. A churning population under a wordhood boundary that drifts toward larger, sparser rows and then holds.

## Session 70: the future words have no lineage before sparsification (e514)

- Followed back through fourteen Pythia checkpoints against non-word rows matched on eventual magnitude, the rows that end as native words are indistinguishable through step 1000 in activity, write kurtosis, gradient share, direction and magnitude, while against the population they are already the larger and more reinforced rows by step 1000; from step 2000 their selectivity and kurtosis separate one doubling before their usage does, and the three grow together to the end, where a word fires at a tenth of positions against a quarter for a non-word of the same size. Direction never leads, and the gradient reinforces them more than typical rows but not more than rows of their size. Before step 2000 the future words are used less than their controls: the early vocabulary is other rows, and it turns over between steps 1000 and 8000.
- The winner split closes e512-e513's trap: the early wins were the token embedding, the maxima of steps 512-2000 are head atoms, and the rise from step 2000 is MLP writers.

## Session 69: what precedes sparsification (e513)

- Pythia's first 2000 steps at fine resolution: the gradient concentrates transiently on a few rows (steps 0-16), the state cloud collapses to 9-16 effective dimensions and attention's share of the state collapses with it while the accent is born (16-64), a collapsed plateau (64-256), then sparsification (256-2000) as every row turns selective, the cloud re-expands, attention takes the state back, the ledger's tails heavy, and the contraction is born at warmup's end. The gradient's concentration does not initiate sparsification; it precedes the collapse and is gone by the time the writes sparsify. What precedes sparsification is the cloud's re-expansion and attention's return.

## Session 68: the sparing is write history; wordhood is a fast crossover (e511, e512)

- Only the position's own writes are spared: a chord of native rows with the same coefficients and norm, random or the block's most-used, is contracted like a random direction (GPT-2, Pythia step 16000) or most of the way to it (OLMo, Pythia's end). The downstream MLPs respond little to removing what they were trained to find there and fully to any other displacement of the same size (e511).
- Across 18 Pythia checkpoints wordhood turns on in a fast phase (half its rise between steps 2000 and 8000) and then creeps; sparsification precedes it (midpoint near step 1500-1700), the contraction and the chord's growth follow (2400-19000); the accent is in place before step 512 and flat after; the attention share moves early and again late, and the late phase erodes the last block's words (e512). A crossover with an order, not a discontinuity.

## Session 67: the contraction does not know the atoms (e510)

- As a linear response the learned contraction is isotropic: at the end of training a block contracts native rows, embeddings, head bases, random, covariance-matched and principal directions alike within 25% (GPT-2, Pythia, OLMo), native rows slightly more than random (1.01-1.17), used rows like unused (0.86-1.19), and it is born isotropic between Pythia steps 512 and 4000. The sentence "the network learns to preserve its vocabulary" stays unwritten.
- The chord's relative sparing (e509) is a pattern-level effect at chord amplitude: at a perturbation of the state's size the most-used rows are contracted less than random directions in GPT-2 (0.63-0.80) and at Pythia's block 18 (0.66), and more in OLMo (1.02-1.19), the same model-by-model pattern as e508's residual. At Pythia step 512 the MLP amplifies on-manifold directions only, the early anisotropy as a response property.

## Session 66: selection, and the contraction as a response (e508, e509)

- In GPT-2 the chord's clean maximum is extreme-value selection over a nearly exchangeable residual: keep the chord, permute the residual, and the level of the maximum (within 2%), the winner's rank within 100 and the winner's residual excess all come back; the chord's top atom is calibrated and the winner's excess is the selection. In OLMo the residual contracts the chord's extremes specifically (the chord's top atom wins 0.13-0.19 against 0.60-0.79 under the null); in Pythia mid-training it reinforces them (two to six times the null).
- The crowd's contraction of the chord is the network's response to the chord (removing the chord from what the MLPs see removes 1.2-1.9 times the actual gain), it is the learned contraction of area 05 (born between Pythia steps 512 and 4000, growing to the end, as e217 placed it), and the chord receives a third to a half of what a random direction of its size receives: the vocabulary is what the contraction spares. The early amplification is not a response but the anisotropy of the early state, and the chord's own rows come to be excited by the chord (+0.3 to +0.45 at the end).

## Session 65: the chord against the whole profile (e507)

- The centred chord is a partial predictor of a state's projection onto every atom: in GPT-2 it is calibrated within 13-25% on its own top 200, explains 54-66% of their variance, recovers the largest atom in 32-46% of states and a third of the top ten, and orders the true top hundred at 0.30-0.41; the crowd takes 0.16-0.31 of it and attention gives back 0.13-0.16, two large anti-correlated corrections. ## Session 63: row-following is a property of the feature, not the SAE (e501); the model written down (THEORY 3m)

- Feature by feature on the same states, the TopK family's stronger row-following is its features' sparsity: within both families the AUC falls with frequency and rises with activation strength and the top-row cosine; at matched frequency the families are alike at both ends of the range, and the family label carries no effect once frequency is controlled (-0.09; raw +0.18). - THEORY.md 3m states the extreme-value account of vocabulary formation: an atom is a word when its projection stands above the Gumbel level of the competing atoms; writers do so through their coefficients (write sparsity), non-writers through second-order alignment; energy is a sum and wordhood a maximum, so the MLP rows are the vocabulary while attention carries the state.

## What the whole program established (ten rounds, e01 to e247)

WDD is classical sparse approximation over the model's own write directions, and its label means "this neuron wrote this direction". Reading a write from the state is a prominence phenomenon: it works at the write's birth, degrades with a causal half-life of about two blocks, and depends on the context re-writing the direction; identified atoms are 1.6 to 2.9 times more functional per unit energy than unidentified ones in four models, and the reading says nothing about whether the write caused the direction's presence. Two certificates, stability selection for OMP and a per-atom null-model z-score for the projection reading, make the reading usable. Every trained block is, along any direction, a learned linear contraction that is really the diagonal of a near-isometric, strongly mixing map: a write's energy is conserved or amplified while its direction is scattered, exactly accountably, into thousands of later writers and into attention. The scattered descendant is an angle-preserving, compositional, magnitude-independent image of the write vector, present as an isometry from initialisation and populated with dispersal by training, carrying 6 to 6.4 of 7 recoverable bits under the tested decoders through the middle of the network, robust to weight noise, portable across corpora and checkpoints, predictable for unseen neurons and for random vectors alike, and destroyed only in the last two blocks. It is a classification coordinate, not a decomposition basis: transporting the native dictionary does not restore sparse reading, and the native chart fails on transported writes for the same reason it fails on unmaintained natural ones, prominence. The exceptions throughout are the massive-activation neurons, not the architecture family. The descendant is a sufficient statistic for the write's functional effect and the vector that causally carries it when injected; it organises neurons by function at the population level without making them interchangeable at the token level; and the dimensions the network spends on who grow with depth while what and how much stay compressed; the effect of an intervention is a near-linear function of its position in descendant space, and no small basis reconstructs the descendant itself.

## Negative results worth keeping

- Support co-occurrence carries no information about true co-firing (correlation −0.03 to 0.16, e101).
- Cramér-Rao error bars from the support do not predict the error (e48).
- The linear null-space fraction is trivially large for any sparse ledger and says nothing (e61); the sparse version (exact recovery condition) is the right identifiability statement (e03, e63).
- Alias credit, filler pruning, twin merging and aggressive ridge do not raise identification meaningfully.
- Synthetic constructions that assign per-token partner atoms into a shared dictionary overwrite other tokens' atoms (S1c, S1d artifact); cancelling a write with near-orthogonal atoms needs 8× its energy (S1b artifact). Both are retracted; S1e, S1f and S2 v2 replace them.
