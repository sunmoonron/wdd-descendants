# A first-order transport theory of residual-stream writes

Written after round 20 (2026-09-19), revised after the reviewer's reading. The aim: state one compact theory, derive from it the results the program observed, say where the massive-neuron exception enters, and say what the theory does not derive.

Two statements, kept apart. The theorem-like object is the first-order model,

    d_ℓ(t) ≈ c(t) · J_t · ŵ,

whose consequences are mathematics conditional on the assumptions. The empirical discovery is separate: across five models the downstream Jacobian is structured enough that its image of a write remains causally identifiable and functionally meaningful long after the write direction has ceased to be recoverable by sparse decomposition; and the image is a state variable for this perturbation family, predicting its own future and its effect better than the original write does. Each derived item below is tagged: [derived] for a consequence of the first-order model, [J] for a measured property of the transport Jacobian, [G] for a measured property of the read-out Jacobian, [reading] for an interpretation of how the model uses the coordinate. Every item names the experiment that observed it; the two predictions that were new were tested in e285 and e285b.

## 0. Objects

- Residual stream of token t at level ℓ (the output of block ℓ): x_ℓ(t) ∈ R^D. A pre-norm block acts as x_{ℓ+1} = x_ℓ + f_ℓ(x_ℓ; X_{≤t}), where f_ℓ is the attention plus MLP update and depends on the whole prefix through attention.
- The write of neuron i of block b at token t: c_i(t) ŵ_i, with ŵ_i the unit row of the down-projection and c_i(t) = a_i(t)·||w_i|| the WDD coefficient (activation times row norm).
- The descendant of the write at level ℓ, per token: d_i(ℓ, t) = x_ℓ(t)[with the write] − x_ℓ(t)[without it], the natural footprint of ablating the neuron. Its class centroid d̄_i(ℓ) is the mean over the neuron's dominant tokens. WDD reads c_i ŵ_i at ℓ = b + 1, where the descendant is the write itself.

## 1. The axiom

Within the linear range of the perturbation,

    d_i(ℓ, t) = c_i(t) · J_t · ŵ_i + O(c²),      J_t := ∂x_ℓ(t) / ∂x_{b+1}(t)

where J_t is the Jacobian of the downstream map along the token's natural trajectory (including the effect of the perturbation on other tokens through attention and back). Decompose it into a class mean and a token-specific fluctuation,

    J_t = J̄_C + ΔJ_t,

with C a context class (the neuron's own tokens; or the typical tokens over which the operator T of e248 was fitted). Three measured properties of J make the theory concrete:

- (J1) Generic spectrum. The singular values of the averaged operator spread by a factor 11 to 50 between the 90th and 10th percentiles (e282); there is no exact null space (e273; e285: gain along the operator's bottom singular directions 0.16 to 0.35, not zero); the root-mean-square gain is of order one (energy conserved or amplified, e210; random-direction gain 0.26 to 0.57 at mid depth, e285).
- (J2) Token dependence. By mid depth the fluctuation term is larger than the mean term: the coherent fraction of a per-token descendant is 0.16 to 0.33 at mid depth and 0.05 to 0.14 late (e277).
- (J3) Linear range. The response is homogeneous in amplitude up to about the natural coefficient: gain per unit coefficient constant within 10% from 0.1× to 1× and the image direction self-consistent at 0.78 to 0.99 (e278); distortion sets in at 3× to 10× for ordinary directions.

## 2. Derived consequences, each with the experiment that observed it

- D1 [derived] Homogeneity. d(αŵ) = α d(ŵ) in the linear range. Observed: e250 (half amplitude within 15 to 25%), e278 (gain per coefficient constant from 0.1× to 1×).
- D2 [derived, range is J] Additivity. d(Σ_i c_i ŵ_i) = Σ_i c_i d(ŵ_i) up to the nonlinearity of the total perturbation, so the additivity error is a function of the total injected norm, not of the number of superposed writes. Observed: e241 (error grows with m when every write keeps its natural amplitude), e248 (a + b classified as a + b), e285b (see §2a).
- D3 [derived] Vector determination. d depends on ŵ, not on which neuron produced it, and every vector has a descendant. Observed: transplant classification of natural descendants (e236, e237), zero-shot classification from transplant images (e242, 0.77 to 1.00), random-token images (e246), random vectors transported by the same operator (e248).
- D4 [derived; the absence of a null space is G] Function. The logit footprint is f_i(t) = G_{ℓ,t} · d_i(ℓ, t) with G_{ℓ,t} = W_U · ∂x_final(t)/∂x_ℓ(t), the un-averaged Jacobian lens. Hence: the descendant is a sufficient statistic for the effect given the level (e268: adding the write gains ≤ 0.01); injecting the descendant reproduces the effect (e269, cosine 0.28 to 0.62 where the write vector gives −0.29 to +0.16); the effect is linear along descendant segments (e271) and the whole trajectory of an interpolated descendant is the interpolation of the endpoint trajectories (e280, cosine 0.97 to 1.00, fitted position exact within 0.01), because the downstream map is linear in the perturbation; there is no functional null space unless G has a kernel on the descendant cloud (e273: response equal in every direction class; e285: no null directions of the transport).
- D5 [derived, given that later factors vary less across tokens: J] The crossover. Since f = G_ℓ d(ℓ) and G_ℓ = G_{ℓ'} J_{ℓ→ℓ'}, a later descendant is separated from the effect by fewer factors, each varying less across tokens, so the Gram of d(ℓ) approximates the Gram of f better as ℓ grows. Observed: e260 (descendant-to-logit alignment rising from +0.10 two blocks after birth to +0.81 at the last block in Pythia while the descendant-to-write alignment falls), e259 (descendant geometry predicts functional geometry given write geometry, partial Spearman +0.29 to +0.60; the converse ≤ +0.16).
- D6 [derived; that the operator composes well is J] State variable. To first order d(ℓ + k, t) = J_{ℓ→ℓ+k, t} · d(ℓ, t): the future descendant is the Jacobian image of the present one. The write predicts the future only through the whole chain, which loses the token-specific history that d(ℓ, t) already contains. Observed: e283 (descendant law 0.43 to 0.78 two blocks ahead, write law 0.03 to 0.12, random 0.00, state adds ≤ 0.16; the law-predicted future reproduces the future effect at 0.59 to 0.85), e281 (operators compose in state and in function; the two-step product is the better estimate because each factor is fitted over a shorter, more linear stretch).
- D7 [derived from the axiom having no privileged direction; the measured tie is G] Generic leverage. Nothing in the axiom privileges ŵ: the leverage of J ŵ under G is that of J r for a random r, up to the broader gain distribution of structured directions. Observed: e276 (natural descendants over random-vector descendants 0.94 to 1.18 in logit response; the write direction at depth has random leverage).
- D8 [derived; the gain concentration is J] Angle preservation without isometry. For a pair (u, v_θ = cos θ · u + sin θ · r) with r random and orthogonal to u, and ⟨J u, J r⟩ ≈ 0 for random r,

      cos(J u, J v_θ) = cos θ · ρ / sqrt(cos²θ · ρ² + sin²θ),      ρ = ||J u|| / ||J r||.

  For random u, ρ ≈ 1 by concentration of measure (random-direction gain has coefficient of variation 0.02 to 0.07, e282), so the cosine is preserved exactly: that is the e238 result (0.99/0.95/0.90/0.75/0.50/0 in, the same out) and it does not require J to be an isometry. The theory predicts that pairs built on the operator's top singular directions have their cosine pushed up and pairs on its bottom directions pushed down, by the formula. Tested in e285: measured/predicted 0.81 to 0.93 / 0.83 to 0.94 (top), 0.40 to 0.74 / 0.40 to 0.76 (bottom), 0.71 to 0.76 / 0.75 to 0.77 (random), within 0.07 in all fifteen cases, with the gain ordering top > random > middle ≥ bottom in 5/5 (e.g. SmolLM2 1.12 / 0.51 / 0.29 / 0.25). Correction to the record: "isometric transport" means angle preservation for random-component pairs; on structured writes the Gram is preserved partially (Spearman 0.57 to 0.72 two blocks after birth, 0.25 to 0.60 at mid depth, e282) and gain varies three to six times more than for random directions.
- D9 [derived, given the coherent fraction: J; the "own computation" sentence is a reading] Provenance versus decomposition. Write d_i(ℓ, t) = c_i [J̄ ŵ_i + ΔJ_t ŵ_i] and let κ(ℓ) be the coherent fraction, the share of energy in the class-mean term. Nearest-centroid identification among K candidates uses only the projection of d onto the K-dimensional span of the centroids; the incoherent term, spread over D_eff dimensions, projects onto that span with relative size about sqrt(K / D_eff). The identification signal-to-noise is therefore about sqrt(κ) · Δ / sqrt((1 − κ) K / D_eff), with Δ the centroid separation, and stays above one down to κ of order K / D_eff (a few percent), whereas any reconstruction by a fixed dictionary has fraction of variance unexplained at least 1 − κ. That is the paradox resolved: provenance needs κ ≳ K / D_eff, decomposition needs κ ≈ 1. Observed: identification 0.89 to 1.00 at κ = 0.16 to 0.33 and 0.49 to 0.83 at κ = 0.05 to 0.14 with K = 21 to 29 (e277); the transported dictionary fails as a basis (e2xx kills, five models); the capacity ladder's identity dimensions grow with depth as κ falls (e272). The incoherent term is deterministic (same input, same footprint) but not a linear function of the state (R² ≤ 0.10 at mid depth, e277): it is the token's own downstream computation, which no fixed atom indexes and which transplant images sample by construction (e242).
- D10 [J across checkpoints; no derivation] Training. At initialisation f_ℓ is small, so J ≈ I: transport is the identity (along-survival 1.00 at step 0, e243) and provenance is trivially isometric. Training grows ||∂f_ℓ|| (dispersal, along-survival 1.00 → 0.33), installs the erasure of a write along its own direction (the diagonal gain −0.15 to −0.45, e210, born at the end of warmup, e217) and the rotation of the coordinates in the last block (e279). The theory takes J as given; why training shapes it this way is outside it.

### 2a. The additivity prediction, tested cleanly (e285b)

The first design (e285, part 3) compared against single images measured at a quarter of the amplitude, which are noise-dominated, and was superseded by e285b, which measures single images at the natural amplitude and injects (A) m writes each at the natural amplitude, (B) m writes at constant total norm, and (C) one write at the same total norm as (A). Outcome: the relative-norm error has a floor of 0.51 to 0.73 already at m = 1, because single images come from a pass in which neighbouring tokens carry different directions while the reference sums are injected uniformly at every token, so the cross-token part of the response differs between the two constructions; the norm-versus-count prediction is therefore not cleanly tested and is left open. What the run does show: at constant total norm the direction of the response to sixteen superposed writes agrees with the sum of the single responses at cosine 0.95 to 0.97 (up from 0.69 to 0.86 for one write), and one write at four times the natural amplitude degrades to 0.50 to 0.77; at the same count, the larger total norm gives the larger error in 5/5. Superposition of many writes is at least as linear as a single write; amplitude beyond the natural range is what breaks linearity (as in e278).

## 3. Where the exception enters

The axiom assumes the response to removing a write at its own tokens equals the response to adding it elsewhere: a context-free J. For ordinary writes the fluctuation ΔJ_t ŵ is incoherent across the neuron's own tokens, so the natural centroid is J̄ ŵ and the transplant image agrees with it (e242: 0.44 to 0.73 at mid depth). For the massive write of SmolLM2 the fluctuation at its own tokens is coherent (the same reaction at every one of its tokens: coherent fraction 0.88 against 0.34 for ordinary neurons two blocks after birth, 0.22 against 0.06 at mid depth, e250) and anti-parallel to J̄ ŵ. It is not an amplitude effect (the transplant obeys the linear law from 0.1× to 10×, e278), not the vector (which transports better than any ordinary one, cosine 0.56 to 0.72 to the linear prediction), not position, activation or norm (e253), not a cross-token route (the footprint stays at the source token, e257). It is carried by one component: the MLP of block 10, held at its unablated value at the write's own tokens, flips the footprint from −0.67 to +0.52 against the transplant image, while holding every attention output does nothing (e284b). In the theory's terms the massive neuron's own tokens form a context class whose J̄_C differs from the typical J̄ by a term supplied by one MLP whose input at those tokens sits in a gating regime that reads the massive direction with reversed sign. Block 10 is also the one mid-depth rotation of SmolLM2's identity coordinates (e279), so the two anomalies are one. The prediction that adding the massive direction on top of its own write at its own tokens would show the reversed response was tested in e290 and is false: the own-token addition obeys the first-order law (cosine +0.55 to the foreign addition, +0.66 to the linear transport) and is anti-parallel to the natural removal footprint (−0.90). Removal, not context, is what is reversed: removing the write moves the downstream state in the same direction as adding more of it. The response at the massive neuron's own tokens is therefore even in the deviation from the natural amount, R(δ) ≈ a δ J ŵ + b δ² q with the quadratic term dominant along the transport direction at δ = −c, so that the removal footprint x(c) − x(0) = a c J ŵ − b c² q points against J ŵ while the addition response a c J ŵ + b c² q points along it. The natural coefficient sits at a set point of the reacting MLP (block 10, e284b), which answers any deviation the same way. In the theory's terms the exception is a failure of the first-order expansion at one write's own tokens, not a different Jacobian; it enters where the second-order term wins, and it is the only such place found.

## 3a. The compression test (e286)

Decoding every downstream observable from the same per-token descendant at mid depth (observable-specific supervised subspaces, one decoder family, held-out tokens): reconstruction needs 512 to 1024 directions; the future descendant, the source identity (ceiling K − 1), the logit footprint and the removal KL each need 8 to 32. Two rungs: the physical perturbation is full-dimensional, its causal description is a 8-to-32-dimensional coordinate inside which the four questions are of similar size. Two statements must be kept apart here: the low-rank causal read-out (established by e286) and a low-rank shared transport (a mechanistic explanation, not established by e286). The first-order split J_t = J̄_C + ΔJ_t does not by itself say that the read-out lives in the shared term; e274 found the function subspace inside the between-neuron subspace at only 0.48 to 0.76 of its energy at eight dimensions, and D9 says the token-specific term dominates the physical descendant while provenance stays readable. Whether the token-specific term is itself causally read is tested in e287 (§3b).

## 3b. Shared versus token-specific read-out (e287)

Each observable was decoded from the descendant's component in the span of the K class centroids (the shared part, 28 to 46% of the energy) and from the orthogonal remainder (the token-specific part). The span part alone reaches the full score on all four observables (e287). But the adversarial controls of e288 show that a covariance-matched random K-dimensional subspace and a permuted-label centroid span decode function, future and KL just as well; only source identity is specific to the true span. So the correct statement is weaker than the one first drawn from e287 and stronger than the reviewer's caution: the read-out G sees the high-variance, low-rank part of the descendant cloud, of which the shared term J̄_C ŵ is one basis; identity alone is carried by the shared term specifically; the token-specific term ΔJ_t ŵ is redundant for these four questions while being a weakly transported state of its own (its future predictable at 0.09 to 0.28). This is why reconstruction needs the full dimension while every causal question needs tens (e286): G is effectively low-rank on the cloud, and the cloud's dominant directions carry what G reads.

## 3c. The core as a dynamical object (e292 to e295)

The 16-dimensional core evolves over two blocks by a near-diagonal, evenly contracting, rotation-free linear map (R² 0.89 to 0.96), as the first-order model with a near-identity residual transport predicts; the leftover dimensions evolve the same way but nothing reads them (e295), so D9's "token-specific computation" is, at the resolution of these observables, carried rather than used. Steering along a core direction moves its paired logit direction selectively but accounts for only a fifth to two fifths of the response (e292): G maps the core to the logits faithfully in direction, not exhaustively. The predictive subspaces are nested (e294), with the linear future at about 128 directions between the effect coordinate and the physical descendant.

## 3d. The literature-derived tests (e296 to e304)

The first-order model predicted, and the runs found: a causal metric under which the cloud is low-rank (e296), persistent individual channels under near-identity transport (e296), linear independent coordinates with amplitude as the only strong nonlinearity (e302), the even response confined to the massive neuron's own tokens (e301), and the same ladder for every perturbation family (e304), since nothing in d = c J ŵ refers to the origin of ŵ. It did not predict, and the runs added: the cores of all neurons share a six-to-eight-dimensional receptor space (e300) while each core is largely the write's own projection onto it in the GELU models (e303); the physical dimension saturates within a few blocks (e297); routing is only weakly a function of the core (e298).

## 3e. What the projection P is (e305 to e308)

The first-order model leaves P unspecified. The tests say what it is not: no spectral subspace of J (e305) and no high-gain subspace of G, forward or backward (e305, e308). It is the dominant-variance subspace of the cloud {J_t ŵ_i}: since G is nearly isotropic in gain (e273, e305) and J is nearly the identity on the cloud (e293, e307), the directions that carry function are simply the directions in which the model's own perturbations vary most, and unrelated perturbation families converge to the same functional directions with depth (e306). The true Jacobian confirms f = G d per token at cosine 0.58 to 0.67 (e308). The theory's variables are therefore: a generic J, a near-isotropic G, and a low-dimensional perturbation cloud; the last is the empirical fact the theory takes as input.

## 3f. The quotient (e309 to e312)

The first-order model with a generic J and a nearly isotropic G predicts that any perturbation family acquires a low-dimensional functional coordinate, because the reachable logit changes are few and the map to them is linear on the linear range; e310 confirms it for perturbations orthogonal to the natural manifold and for synthetic families, and e312 confirms linearity of superposition across families. The convergence of functional geometry with depth (e311, e306) is then the statement that G_ℓ = G_{ℓ'} J_{ℓ→ℓ'} with J near the identity: the later the level, the more nearly the physical geometry is the functional one. What the model does not predict is the cloud's own geometry (e309: curved, low-rank, unrelated to the state's variance), which remains the empirical input.

## 3g. WDD and the quotient, mathematically

WDD writes the centred state at the birth level as a sparse ledger over the model's own atoms,

    x_{b+1}(t) − μ = Σ_i c_i(t) ŵ_i + r(t),

with c the coefficients (activation times row norm) and r the part no atom explains. The descendant program follows one term under the first-order transport, d_i(ℓ, t) = c_i(t) J_t ŵ_i, and the quotient program reads a functional coordinate from any perturbation by a linear map on the linear range (e312, e313), z_ℓ(δ) = P_ℓ^T δ. Composing the three:

    z_ℓ( Σ_i c_i(t) J_t ŵ_i ) = Σ_i c_i(t) · P_ℓ^T J_t ŵ_i = Σ_i c_i(t) z_i(t) ≈ Z_ℓ c(t),      Z_ℓ := P_ℓ^T J̄ [ŵ_1 … ŵ_N].

Z_ℓ is the functional dictionary: the image of the WDD dictionary under transport and quotient, a 16 × N matrix whose columns are the atoms' core coordinates. Three of the program's results are statements about Z:

- Its effective rank is six to eight for hundreds of atoms (e300): the quotient collapses the dictionary onto a handful of functional directions, and the fibres of Z are the functional equivalence classes of atoms. WDD reads c (which atom), the quotient reads Zc (what the atoms do); the kernel of Z is what provenance sees and function does not.
- In the GELU models Z ≈ P^T A restricted to the atoms (e303: a write's own projection onto the core predicts its core coordinate at cosine 0.53 to 0.71), because J is nearly the identity on the core (e293, e307): the functional dictionary is the WDD dictionary projected onto the core. In the gated models the constructed part is larger.
- Sparse coding over transported atoms fails (the transported-WDD kills) because the token-specific term ΔJ_t ŵ_i, which dominates d_i physically (e277), is not in the span of any fixed dictionary, while the quotient discards it (e295, e287): the ledger survives the transport as functional coordinates, not as a basis.

The equation makes one prediction the record had not tested: the functional coordinate of a joint perturbation must be the ledger-weighted sum of the atoms' coordinates, P^T d_S(t) ≈ Σ_{i ∈ S} c_i(t) z_i, with the coefficients read by WDD at birth and the z_i measured once per atom in a foreign context. That is e313: for random subsets of four to eight atoms ablated jointly, the ledger-weighted sum of the atoms' coordinates meets the joint footprint's coordinate at cosine 0.66 GPT-2, 0.54 Pythia, 0.68 OLMo, 0.34 Qwen, 0.30 SmolLM2 (random atoms −0.03 to +0.04), and better than the same prediction in full dimension (0.16 to 0.43); the quotient is additive on unrelated atom pairs at 0.78 to 0.97. So WDD and the quotient are two coordinate systems of one linear map: WDD the fine one (atoms), the quotient the coarse one (function), related by Z, tightly in the GELU models and OLMo and more loosely where the per-atom coordinate is context-constructed.

## 3h. The quotient program's correction to the theory (e317 to e356)

The first-order model with a generic J and a near-isotropic G predicted the universality of the functional coordinate across observables and families and its additivity, and both held (e317, e335, e337, e342). It did not say that the coordinate is a privileged subspace, and the audits show it is not: a projection fitted to shuffled targets, a PCA, or the discarded complement all carry the same information (e340, e346, e347), there is no null space or coset structure (e336), and the fibres are random (e338). The consistent reading is that G restricted to the descendant cloud is of moderate effective rank with a long tail (e355) and near-isotropic, so that any high-variance projection of the cloud of dimension a few tens suffices for prediction while none is structurally distinguished. P in the theory should be read as "any such projection". Two additions the model did not anticipate: the coordinate is two thirds implicit in the state and the source (e339), and the later MLPs are its readers and dampers while attention only spreads it across tokens (e341, e351, e352).

## 3i. Selection, curvature and use (phase 2, e357 to e372)

Give every unit u a scale a_u multiplying its write (a = 1 is the model). The quantities of phase 2 are all derivatives or finite differences of the loss L(a) on some data:

- selection (the post's co-selection signal, e359 to e368): s_u = dL/da_u at a = 1, per batch or per write position;
- co-selection: the covariance of s_u and s_v across batches or tokens; for the per-example loss of a well-specified model at a stationary point this covariance is the Fisher matrix, which equals the Hessian H of the loss in the scale coordinates;
- use (ablation): E_u = L(a_u = 0) - L(1), a finite move of size one along a_u; the circuit statistics are E_uv - E_u - E_v and joint non-additivity.

Three consequences, each tested.

(i) At a trained optimum the mean selection vanishes for every unit, E_data[s_u] = 0 (weight decay adds a small shrinkage term), whatever the unit's use. Selection therefore measures local mis-scaling, and its token-level variation tracks use only where a unit is locally under-scaled. Observed: the induction heads' mean selection is about zero in GPT-2 and SmolLM2 while their use is positive (e370b); at the positions they serve, GPT-2's selection has the wrong sign while SmolLM2's matches its use (e370); and across Pythia training the circuit's co-selection rises after the circuit forms and decays toward the end (e363, e368), while its use and redundancy keep growing (e365).

(ii) If L were quadratic in a over [0, 1], then E_u = -s_u + H_uu / 2 and E_uv - E_u - E_v = H_uv exactly, and co-selection (Fisher = H) would equal the local form of circuit interaction. Observed: second order ranks single effects well (Spearman 0.63-0.94 in 8 of 10 conditions) but misses their size, and measured interactions are 2.4-48 times H_uv and ranked by it at 0.11-0.53 (e371). The loss along a top head's scale is flat near a = 1 and rises near a = 0 (e372: for the top heads L(0.5) - L(1) is a median 4-30% of L(0) - L(1) across the five models against 24-57% for random heads, and the quadratic model at a = 1 captures 3-65% of a single head's ablation effect and 1-54% of the top-8 joint effect). Units that matter are robust to partial removal and fail only near full removal, so circuit structure is a global property of L(a) that the local quantities at a = 1 (gradients, co-selection, curvature, and any susceptibility defined at the trained point without sampling away from it) cannot reproduce. This is also the O(c^2) term of section 5 made concrete: for whole units it is not small.

(iii) Drift decomposes the same way. A neuron's implementation (its write and read vectors) and its function (its basis-free logit signature under the current downstream network) are different objects, and in Pythia the implementation settles first: the ceiling-corrected signature reaches the final one at 0.34-0.48 when the write is already at 0.60-0.72 (e360d). The function of an early-layer write is also increasingly context-dependent through training (two token samples of the same neuron's signature agree at 0.93 at step 1000 and 0.28 at the end in block 2), so "the function of a unit" is a distribution over contexts whose spread is itself learned.

## 3j. Readers, readouts and convexity (phase 3, e374 to e382)

Three results change the picture of section 3i.

(i) The prominence law has a reader-side form. WDD reads a write when its projection on the state beats a competitor level (section 1). A downstream reader sees only its own input map W_R applied to the normed state, so the relevant provenance of the state for that reader is the WDD code weighted by each atom's share of W_R's input, <a, r_n> with r_n the reader's direction at position n. Read this way, WDD recovers the previous-token to induction edge in every model tested (e375), without intervening. It stays an identification method: shares are underestimated because 64 atoms explain 59-87% of the state, and near-twin heads share credit.

(ii) The removal knee is a readout effect, not a singularity. Around full strength the loss is quadratic in a head's scale (exponent 2, e374); the large-move behaviour comes mostly from the softmax. Cross-entropy is convex in the logits (log-sum-exp), so by Jensen's inequality two effects that add exactly in logit space still interact on the loss: with the single logit effects added, 97-100% of top head pairs are super-additive (e382). The loss interaction is the net of this readout term and the logit-space interaction c = d_ij - d_i - d_j, and the split between them depends on where c is evaluated (at the intact model the correct token dominates the softmax, at the joint point its competitors do). The same holds for KL: for effects that add in logit space, KL(A+B) - KL(A) - KL(B) is, to second order, the Fisher inner product d_A' H d_B (e383 confirms it at per-token correlation 0.73-0.98), and the scale dependence of interactions is textbook in epidemiology (VanderWeele and Knol 2014). Consequently: the sign of a loss interaction is not a property of the circuit; backup readings on the loss are largely readout convexity (e377, e380); and interaction structure should be read on linear readouts (logits, logit differences), where the path dependence disappears.

(iii) Selection, redundancy and co-adaptation under manipulation. In toys trained from scratch, first-order selection vanishes at convergence while use does not, and tracks use far from an optimum (e379), as section 3i(i) predicts; co-selection is high only while a circuit is under construction; and redundancy appears when training noise (head dropout) makes it pay. In Pythia, the lower and upper halves co-adapt to the end of training (e378): write vectors at cosine 0.95 of their final form still cost the final upper half more than the entire late-training gain, about half of it a change of basis.

## 3k. WDD as a code of the computation (e387 to e390)

WDD was built as an identification method: which writers are in the state. Judged instead as a sparse code of the state that the rest of the network must run on, it is functionally faithful and the faithfulness is provenance. At 8 to 32 atoms, the reconstruction over the model's own MLP write rows keeps far more of the next-token loss than OMP over a rotated copy of the same dictionary (same Gram matrix), than PCA, or than embeddings or head bases alone (e388, e389); only rows that are nearly the ones that wrote the state do this (e390). At 64 to 128 atoms every overcomplete dictionary reconstructs well, which is the regime where random-baseline sanity checks of learned sparse autoencoders also find random decoders competitive (Korznikov et al. 2026). The reading side is less sparse than the writing side: a reader input draws half its content from 8 to 14 components (e387), a sparse reading at 64 atoms sees about half of what readers take in, and the apparent preference of readers for identifiable writes is mostly shared alignment with the state.

## 3l. Native-vocabulary description length (e391 to e414)

Terminology (adopted in session 39). The native vocabulary (NV) of a network is a dictionary derived deterministically from its own parameters: its write directions, which WDD supplies. The native-vocabulary description length (NVDL) is what that vocabulary can say: the number of native words a k-sparse description needs to keep a given fraction of the network's function. Functional distortion (loss recovered when the description replaces the state) is primary; Euclidean reconstruction (FVU) is a secondary analysis, because the two disagree sharply (the few highest-variance directions hold a large share of the variance and, in four of five models, about a tenth of the average local sensitivity per unit of variance; session 44). "The network's own words" is the intuitive name for the same thing. WDD supplies the native vocabulary; NVDL measures what it can say.


Let V_θ be network θ's unit write directions up to depth ℓ: token embeddings, MLP write rows, and orthonormal bases of the head output subspaces. A k-word description of a state x is μ + Σ_{i∈S} c_i v_i, with |S| = k and v_i ∈ V_θ; the support comes from OMP and the coefficients from least squares. Its adequacy R_θ(k; V) is the loss recovered when the description replaces the state in θ's forward pass (1 at the clean state, 0 at the mean state). Define:

    SDL_q(θ)  = min{ k : R_θ(k; V_θ) ≥ q }                       self-description length
    W_q(θ)    = min{ k : the k largest actual writes reach q }   write length (the rest at their means)
    ρ_q(θ)    = W_q / SDL_q                                      writing redundancy
    M_ij(k)   = R_θj(k; V_θi)                                    mutual intelligibility

The measure is MDL-inspired (Rissanen 1978): a conditional description length under a fixed, model-derived dictionary and a specified functional distortion, with a codebook whose cost is the network's own parameters, already paid. It is not an estimate of an absolute MDL quantity or of Kolmogorov complexity. A random rotation of V_θ has the same cost and the same Gram matrix, so the difference between the two is provenance.

In this language:

- SDL_0.9 is 32-64 own words at the middle depth in all five models (e388) and throughout Pythia's training (e395).
- W_0.9 is at least 1024 (e391), and above 4096 at the end of Pythia's training (e395).
- The property is learned: at initialisation V_θ cannot be told apart from its rotation.
- Across training, M_ij is asymmetric (later vocabularies also do the earlier words' work) and converges.
- Across seeds, M_ij is at the random level unless the vocabulary is translated, and translation carries a third to a half of the provenance advantage.

Why a partly aligned vocabulary can do worse than a random one (false friends, e397; mechanism tested in e400 and e404). By step 16000 the state develops a few huge directions: its top-8 principal subspace M holds 0.52 of the variance at step 16000 and 0.86 at the end, but only 0.017 and 0.015 of the Fisher trace. Locally the network barely feels them. At large displacement it does: setting M to its mean costs the own description 0.17 of the loss at k = 16. A word with a component in M is therefore dangerous in a short description:
- Under Euclidean OMP it is chosen to fit the huge component, and its large coefficient imports errors into the function-carrying directions.
- Under the Fisher metric it is chosen for its function, and its M component then lands with the wrong magnitude.

The step-4000 words carry more of M than random words do (1.6% of their squared norm against 1.0%, 8/1024 by chance), and they are false friends under both metrics. With M kept exactly and removed from every word, they are better than random (e404). So the harm is an interaction between an old vocabulary and directions that did not yet dominate when that vocabulary was current. Huge directions alone do not produce false friends: OLMo's late states have them (top-8 share 0.61-0.63) and no stage of OLMo misleads its later states (e406). What is needed is a stage whose words are partly aligned with directions that later become huge, and Pythia's steps 2000-8000 are such a stage. The own vocabulary avoids it because it contains the words that write M (5.0% of its picks' norm lies in M). About 40% of its final advantage over a rotation at k = 16 is exactly this ability.

Correction (session 44, e432 and e437). The 0.86 was not M's share at ordinary positions: one position per sequence, the token "\n\n\n" at 41 times the median state norm, holds 0.83 of Pythia-410m's centred variance at middle depth. At typical positions M holds 0.17, and 0.10-0.26 in all five models. The subspace is the same (overlap 0.88), and replacing the sink states by the typical mean state costs nothing. The false friends were those sinks mis-described: with the sinks kept exact, the step-4000 words recover +0.15 at k = 4 against their rotation's +0.08 (previously -0.37). Presumably four words fitted to a state 41 times the typical norm leave a vector of that size in a wrong direction; how later positions read it was not measured. The account above (words aligned with directions that later become huge) describes the sink directions. It does not describe a mechanism acting at ordinary positions.

What the controls change (session 38). The self-description gap Δ_θ(k) = R_θ(k; V_θ) − R_θ(k; R V_θ) mixes two things:
- Δ_2, the part a vocabulary with the same second moment or span also achieves (Gaussian words with covariance V_θᵀV_θ/N, or signed sums of 8 words of one family);
- Δ_w, the part only the individual words achieve.

Up to step 8000 in Pythia, Δ_2 is 41-88% of Δ at k = 16 (an accent). The accent is per block (session 44, e443): Gaussian words with each block's own second moment carry 0.82 of Δ at step 1000, 0.70 at 4000, 0.32 at 16000 and 0.09 at the end, so early self-description says which block wrote a component; the words take over between steps 4000 and 16000. From step 16000 it is near zero (a vocabulary), at 70m and 160m as at 410m (e409): training progressively converts a second-order geometric alignment into atom-specific structure. Chosen under the Fisher metric, the gap is 0.04 / 0.09 / 0.24 at steps 1000 / 16000 / 143000: most of the early gap is a Euclidean effect, and the late gap is functional. None of this makes the own vocabulary optimal. Random words drawn from the states' own covariance (which requires activations) describe the states better at k = 16, and a learned SAE does better at small k. The own vocabulary's distinction is that it is free, that it is created by training, and that its descriptions keep more function per unit of variance explained.

Round 2 and fresh angles (session 39).
- The review's decomposition of self-describability into write alignment, read alignment and word structure resolves as follows. The word structure belongs to the writers alone. The readers' alignment is second-order at every checkpoint: their second moment points where the states vary, but their individual rows are not a vocabulary (e407).
- The weights already encode the split between variance and function. The downstream readers and the unembedding put 0.9-6.3% of their trace on the few directions that hold 52-86% of the variance (e408).
- The false friends are precursors: MLP rows partly aligned with directions that become huge later (from step 4000, formed by 16000; e411). They are harmful only in the model whose huge directions are largest (Pythia-410m; e409, e406).
- The backward pass has a language too. The error signal at the state, g = Σ_readers J_rᵀ δ_r, reaches it through the readers, and from step 16000 it is better described by the readers' words than by the writers'. The states remain better described by the writers'. During early learning the order is reversed: the writers, being built from the errors (Δw_i ∝ -Σ_t a_i(t) g_t), describe them best (e412).

The workspace reading (session 40). The native-word lens reads a state as k native words, each a candidate workspace variable with a provenance (the writer), a sign (promote or suppress) and a fidelity (how much of the model's output the k words together keep). The whole-state logit lens reads the sum. The two differ exactly when a variable is present as a component but out-ranked in the sum, which is what happens to a two-hop bridge at the subject's token in middle layers (e420). There the logit lens ranks the country well among countries but below many generic tokens. At the final position the bridge only becomes present when the answer does, and the sum shows it as well as the parts (e415). Role resolution is readable in the signs: the losing name is carried on suppressing words (e417). At 7B the huge directions were reported to hold 97% of the variance and 0.3% of the local Fisher trace, yet alone recover 29% of the loss (e418, e419). Corrected in session 44 (e437b): the 97% was one token in the fitting text (" series", 221 times the median norm). At ordinary positions they hold 11% of the variance and 0.4% of the Fisher trace (chance 0.2%), and they carry 29% of the function. The local metric still misses their large-displacement role (the knee, e432), so a functional pursuit needs a non-local notion of function.

A shared-subspace reading of e398b. Let W_ij be the ridge map from network i's states to network j's. Its image concentrates on the directions of j's state that i's state predicts. Random words mapped through W_ij describe j's states as well as j's own words at k = 16. So j's function concentrates in the part of its state that an independently trained network also represents, which SVCCA observed in vision networks (Raghu et al. 2017).

## 4. What the theory does not derive

The numerical values (gains, coherent fractions, the depth at which κ crosses K / D_eff); which component reacts and why; the training trajectory (why dispersal, erasure and late scrambling are learned); the sizes of the identity, function and behaviour subspaces beyond the statement that function is low-rank because G_ℓ is effectively low-rank on the descendant cloud (e272, e274); and anything about the massive activation's role for the model.
